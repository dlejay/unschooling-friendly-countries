# Unschooling in Jersey

## Compulsory education

Under article 2(1) of the [Education (Jersey) Law 1999](https://jerseylaw.je/laws/current/l_27_1999),
compulsory school age begins on the first day of the term containing the
child's fifth birthday and ends on 30 June in the school year in which the
child turns sixteen. Article 12(1) requires full-time education suited to the
child's age, ability, aptitude and special educational needs, at school or
otherwise under article 13.

## Approval for home education

[Article 13(1)–(2)](https://jerseylaw.je/laws/current/l_27_1999) provides:

> (1) A child of compulsory school age shall not receive full-time education
> otherwise than by regular attendance at school unless the arrangements
> made for the child’s education have been approved by the Minister.
>
> (2) The Minister shall approve arrangements made for the education of a
> child otherwise than at school if the Minister is satisfied that the
> instruction to be received by the child is at least equal to the
> instruction that a child of the same age would receive in a provided school.

Under the Department for Children, Young People, Education and Skills
(CYPES) [Elective Home Education policy](https://www.gov.je/SiteCollectionDocuments/Education/P%20Elective%20Home%20Education%2001102024%20RB.pdf#page=4),
sections 3.7 and 4.0, parents need no teaching qualification. They submit an
online educational proposal and meet departmental officers. Proposals for
children approaching compulsory school age are due one term beforehand;
processing may take four weeks. Enrolled children remain on the school roll
pending approval.

## Curriculum and learning methods

The Government's [home-education guidance](https://www.gov.je/Education/Schools/HomeEducation/pages/homeschooling.aspx)
states that the Jersey curriculum does not directly apply to elective home
education. Parents must demonstrate breadth and balance, provision suited
to individual needs, academic progress, and emotional and social development.
The guidance distinguishes these requirements from reproducing school
provision and states:

> Education does not always have to follow a set pattern of lessons or even
> a structured timetable.

## Planning and assessment

[Policy sections 4.1.1–4.1.4](https://www.gov.je/SiteCollectionDocuments/Education/P%20Elective%20Home%20Education%2001102024%20RB.pdf#page=5)
require a request for each subsequent academic year and an annual planning
and work review form before 15 June. The form covers learning objectives,
resources and progress across subject areas. On planning, section 4.1.4
specifies:

> These may include retrospective planning arrangements.

Progress reviews normally occur annually. From Year 3, assessments and
reviews include literacy and mathematics; parental preference informs their
format. Formal assessment stops when GCSE or equivalent examination studies
begin. Younger children have annual visits without formal assessment.
Parents must supply evidence of progress, but home entry requires consent
and alternative review arrangements are possible (sections 4.1.5–4.1.13).

[Article 13(3)–(4)](https://jerseylaw.je/laws/current/l_27_1999) permits
approval conditions and assessments, and requires withdrawal of approval
when the Minister is no longer satisfied with the educational arrangements.

## Statistics

The Government's [response of 21 March 2025](https://www.gov.je/government/freedomofinformation/pages/foi.aspx?ReportID=8353)
reports the following numbers of electively home-educated children of
compulsory school age, from the annual spring pupil census:

| Academic year | Home-educated children |
| --- | ---: |
| 2023/24 | 81 |
| 2022/23 | 69 |
| 2021/22 | 56 |
| 2020/21 | 57 |
| 2019/20 | 41 |
