# Unschooling in Transnistria

## Legal context

Transnistria is internationally recognized as part of the Republic of Moldova but is administered by the de facto authorities of the self-proclaimed Pridnestrovian Moldavian Republic (PMR). The rules described below are those applied by the de facto PMR authorities rather than the national education law of Moldova.

## Compulsory education

Under the PMR *Law on Education*, **basic general education** (*основное общее образование*) and the state final assessment conducted at its completion are compulsory for citizens of the PMR. Where a student has not obtained basic general education earlier, the requirement remains in force until the age of 18.

Accordingly, the legal requirement is more precisely expressed as an obligation to obtain **basic general education by age 18**, rather than as a general requirement that every resident complete “nine years of education”. The law expressly formulates the obligation by reference to PMR citizens. Foreign citizens and stateless persons are, however, entitled under the applicable admission rules to primary, basic and secondary general education on the same generally accessible and free basis as PMR citizens.

Ordinarily, children entering the first year of general education must be at least 6 years and 6 months old on 1 September of the year of admission.

## Family education

The *Law on Education* expressly recognizes **family education** (*семейное образование*). Parents or other legal representatives may provide a child with preschool, primary general, basic general and secondary (full) general education in this form. The child's opinion must be taken into account when the form of education is chosen.

The detailed procedure is governed by Ministry of Education Order No. 263 of 24 March 2022, in its current consolidated version. Family education means that the child studies the educational programme outside an educational organization at the initiative of the parents.

Family education is nevertheless part of the regulated education system. A parent submits an application to an educational organization, which then issues an administrative act changing the child's form of education. The organization continues to register the student and is responsible for conducting the required assessments.

Parents are responsible for ensuring that the child completes the educational programmes **in full and in accordance with the state educational standards**.

There is no continuous school assessment of students receiving family education. However, they must undergo **intermediate assessment by examination in every subject in the school's curriculum at the end of each academic year**. Remote educational technologies may not be used for these examinations. Basic general and secondary (full) general education also conclude with compulsory state final assessment.

Unsatisfactory results or failure to attend the required intermediate assessments without valid reason create an academic debt. Students at primary or basic-general level who fail to clear that debt within the prescribed period must repeat the year.

### Teaching qualifications

The former regulation on education in the family, adopted in 2004, contained restrictions concerning the qualifications of persons providing instruction. **That regulation is no longer in force.** Order No. 263 expressly repealed it when the present regime entered into force on 1 September 2022.

The current family-education regulation contains **no requirement that a parent or other person teaching the child hold a pedagogical qualification, pass teacher attestation or possess a state teaching certificate**. The relevant obligation now falls on the parents to ensure completion of the prescribed educational programmes and compliance with state educational standards.

## Unschooling

The legislation does not prescribe a particular pedagogical method for family education. It does, however, require parents to ensure completion of the prescribed educational programmes in accordance with state educational standards. Students educated in the family form are also subject to annual intermediate assessment in every subject included in the relevant school curriculum and, at the appropriate stages, to state final assessment.
