# Unschooling in Montenegro

## Compulsory education

Primary education in Montenegro is compulsory for children from age 6 to 15
and lasts nine years.

## Home education

Montenegro permits home education at primary-school level.

No particular reason, such as illness or disability, is required by the law.
Home education is therefore open to families generally, but it is not an
automatic right to withdraw a child from school.

Article 3 of the Primary Education Law provides that a parent or guardian may
organise a child’s education at home for one semester or one school year.

The child must first be enrolled in a school. At least two months before home
education is due to begin, the parent must notify that school in writing.

The notification must state:

- the child’s name;
- the address at which the education will take place; and
- the name and educational qualifications of the person who will educate the
  child.

The school’s teachers’ council then decides whether the child may be educated
at home. The procedure is therefore not one of notification alone, and home
education cannot begin immediately.

## Curriculum and assessment

A child educated at home must attain the knowledge standards prescribed by the
public educational programme.

The school must assess the child in every subject for the relevant grade at the
end of the semester or school year.

A student who has not attained the required standards by the end of the school
year must repeat the grade and continue their education in school.

Students educated at home are also subject to the applicable national
assessment requirements at the end of each educational cycle.

Because the child must meet the prescribed standards in every subject, a fully
child-led or purely interest-driven form of unschooling is not legally
compatible with the system. Parents may nevertheless use flexible or
interest-led teaching methods provided that the required curriculum standards
are ultimately met.

## Assessment regulations

The detailed assessment procedure is governed by the *Rulebook on the manner
and procedure for assessing the knowledge of students educated at home*.

The Rulebook was published in the *Official Gazette of Montenegro* No. 43/2009
and remains listed as in force. Although a copy was published in the
government’s online document library in 2015, 2015 is not the date on which the
Rulebook was originally enacted.
