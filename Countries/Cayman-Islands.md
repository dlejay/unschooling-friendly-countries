# Unschooling in the Cayman Islands

## Compulsory education

Compulsory school age generally begins at age five, where the child turns five
before 1 September, and ends when the child turns seventeen.

It may end earlier if the child has met the requirements for a secondary-school
diploma or its equivalent.

Parents and legal guardians must ensure that a child of compulsory school age
receives suitable full-time education, either by attending school or through an
authorised home-school programme.

## Home education

Section 16 of the Education Act allows the Director of the Department of
Education Services to authorise a child to be educated at the child's normal
residence or at another home approved by the parent or legal guardian.

Home education is not restricted to children with exceptional circumstances.
However, the child must be legally and normally resident in the Cayman Islands,
and the parent must apply to the Department of Education Services and obtain
approval before beginning the home-school programme.

The child must be registered before the programme starts and re-registered
annually for as long as home education continues. A child should therefore not
be withdrawn from school before authorisation has been granted.

The application must include written information describing the curriculum,
educational programme or learning philosophy that will be used.

Parents may develop or adapt their own educational programme or learning
philosophy. Nevertheless, the programme must:

- provide full-time education;
- be broad, balanced and suitable to the child's needs and interests;
- cover the mandatory subjects established by the Education Act;
- establish appropriate learning goals and expected outcomes; and
- enable the child to demonstrate adequate progress and achievement.

The mandatory subjects include literacy, numeracy, science, information and
communications technology, the arts, physical education, and studies relating
to civics, religion, and the history and culture of the Cayman Islands.

The National Home Schooling Policy defines full-time education as at least five
hours of curriculum instruction per day for 185 days per year, or an equivalent
total of 925 hours per year.

Parents must submit a written report on the child's educational progress during
the tenth month of each registration year.

The Department of Education Services conducts annual visits for safeguarding
purposes and may also use home visits to verify the child's residence,
attendance and compliance with the approved programme. The Office of Education
Standards may assess the programme and determine whether the child is making
progress and meeting expected standards of achievement.

The Minister must terminate a home-school programme, on the recommendation of
the Office of Education Standards, if the programme ceases to meet the statutory
requirements or the child fails to meet established standards of progress and
achievement.

A home-school provider may educate a maximum of five children. Providing home
education to six or more children requires registration and approval to operate
as a school or other educational institution.

## Unschooling

A personalised, flexible or interest-sensitive learning philosophy may be
approved. However, completely child-led unschooling is not legally sufficient
because parents must provide a documented programme, cover mandatory subjects,
meet the full-time education requirement and demonstrate the child's educational
progress.
