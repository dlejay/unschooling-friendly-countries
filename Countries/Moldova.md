# Unschooling in Moldova

Quotations below are unofficial English translations from Romanian.

## Compulsory education

Article 13(1)–(3) of the [Education Code, No. 152/2014](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=156377&lang=ro)
provides:

> (1) Compulsory education begins with the preparatory group in preschool
> education and ends with lower secondary education.
>
> (2) The obligation to attend compulsory education ceases at the age of 16.
>
> (3) Responsibility for the compulsory schooling of children under the
> age of 16 rests with parents or other legal representatives and with
> first- and second-level local public administration authorities.

The preparatory preschool group concerns children aged 6–7, as specified
in the Ministry's [early childhood education resources](https://mec.gov.md/ro/node/15515).
Article 27(4), concerning entry into primary education, provides:

> Schooling becomes compulsory after reaching the age of 7.

Article 27(5) permits earlier entry at the request of parents or other
legal representatives, depending on the child's psychosomatic maturity
as confirmed by specialists. Primary education comprises grades I–IV;
lower secondary education comprises grades V–IX, under articles 27 and 29.

## School attendance

Article 27(1) provides:

> Primary education comprises grades I–IV and is organised as
> attendance-based education, generally in the first half of the day.
> For persons with learning difficulties caused by disabilities, primary
> education may be organised as distance education or home instruction.

Article 29(1) provides:

> Lower secondary education comprises grades V–IX and is organised as
> attendance-based education. For persons with learning difficulties
> caused by disabilities, lower secondary education may be organised
> as distance education or home instruction.

## Home instruction for medical reasons

Article 35 provides:

> (1) Home instruction is organised for a fixed period for children and
> pupils who, because of health problems or a disability, are temporarily
> unable to travel.
>
> (2) Home instruction is conducted in the manner established by the
> Ministry of Education and Research, in coordination with the Ministry
> of Health.

The [Instruction approved by Order No. 486 of 16 March 2026](https://mecc.gov.md/sites/default/files/ordin_aprobare_instr._inv._la_domiciliu.pdf)
entered into force on 1 August 2026. [Order No. 686 of 27 March 2026](https://mecc.gov.md/sites/default/files/ordin_punerea_in_apl._a_instructiunii_org.inv._la_domiciliu.pdf)
repealed Order No. 98/2015 with effect from the same date.

Paragraph 2 of the 2026 Instruction states:

> The purpose of home instruction is to ensure the right to education
> for pupils who, for objective, temporary medical reasons, are physically
> unable to attend the educational institution.

Paragraph 5 covers pupils in primary and secondary education who are
temporarily unable to travel because of health problems or a disability.
It also covers pupils in special education who become temporarily unable
to travel because of additional health problems arising after their
enrolment in special education.

## Application and approval

Paragraph 6 requires a written application from a parent or other legal
representative to the director of the school in which the pupil is
enrolled, with copies of identity documents and supporting documentation
appropriate to the pupil's circumstances. The supporting documents
specified in paragraphs 6.1 and 6.2 include a Medical Consultative Council
decision attesting temporary restrictions on participation in school;
for children with disabilities, a disability and work-capacity certificate
with the individual rehabilitation and social inclusion programme; and,
for pupils with special educational needs, the comprehensive developmental
assessment report prepared by the territorial educational psychology
support service.

Under paragraphs 7–10, the director forwards the application dossier to
the local education authority or, for schools under its authority, the
Ministry of Education and Research. A five-member specialist commission
decides whether to approve or refuse home instruction and records the
period specified in the medical documentation.

Paragraph 11 provides:

> On the basis of the specialist commission's decision, the Minister of
> Education and Research or the head of the local education authority
> issues the order organising home instruction.

Paragraph 12 requires the school director to issue an internal order
on that basis. Under paragraphs 13 and 14, the school designates teachers
qualified in the relevant subjects. The lesson timetable is coordinated
with parents or legal representatives and approved by the school's
administrative council before the school year begins or, at the latest,
within seven days of the ministerial or local authority's order.

## Curriculum, teaching and assessment

Paragraphs 15 and 16 provide:

> (15) The educational process at home is conducted on the basis of the
> Framework Plan for Home Instruction.
>
> (16) For pupils in primary and lower secondary education who study
> school subjects according to an adapted or modified curriculum, the
> educational process is conducted in accordance with the individualised
> educational plan, drawn up and approved in accordance with the regulatory
> framework approved by the Ministry of Education and Research.

Paragraph 20 requires teachers to adapt teaching, learning and assessment
methods to the pupil's health. Paragraphs 21–23 apply the Ministry's rules
on assessment, grading, progression and graduation; for pupils with special
educational needs, assessment is based on the individualised educational
plan. National primary tests and lower and upper secondary graduation
examinations follow the arrangements established by the Ministry.

Paragraph 26 provides:

> The director of the educational institution ensures monitoring of the
> educational process at home, which includes, but is not limited to:
>
> 26.1. compliance with the approved timetable;
>
> 26.2. full implementation of the curriculum in school subjects;
>
> 26.3. methodological assistance to the teachers and specialists conducting
> the educational process at home.

Under paragraphs 17 and 18, lessons may take place online where the pupil
lives in a different locality from the school, regular teacher travel is
logistically difficult, and the school can provide the necessary equipment
and resources. Online teaching may not exceed 50% of the total allocated
subject hours and requires a written parental request and an order from
the school director.

## Foreign families

Article 9(11) of the Education Code provides:

> Citizens of other states, as well as stateless persons, have access to
> education through the national education system under the conditions
> of this Code.

Article 150(1) provides:

> Admission of foreigners to studies takes place under the conditions
> applicable to citizens of the Republic of Moldova.
