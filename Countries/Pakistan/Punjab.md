# Home education in Punjab

## Compulsory education and education outside school

The *Punjab Free and Compulsory Education Act 2014* covers children aged five to sixteen. Section 9 requires admission and attendance at school, subject to a reasonable excuse. Explanation (b) expressly includes a child receiving:

> otherwise than in a school, education or instructions which in the opinion of the school management body, is sufficient.

This exception concerns education being received outside school and makes its sufficiency the statutory test. The school management body established under section 11 includes government or local-authority representatives, teachers and parents. [Act 2014, sections 2(a), 9 and 11](https://punjablaws.gov.pk/laws/2580.html); [legislative text reproduced in ILO NATLEX, PDF pp.5–6](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/natlex2/files/download/99329/PAKD99329.pdf#page=5).

## Educational standard and oversight

For the outside-school exception, section 9 assigns the assessment of educational sufficiency to the school management body. Section 18 permits a written complaint concerning a child's education rights; the government or prescribed authority must decide within thirty days after allowing the parties a reasonable opportunity to be heard. [Act 2014, sections 9 and 18](https://punjablaws.gov.pk/laws/2580.html).

## Official statistics

PIE records 20,610 non-formal education centres and 678,751 learners in Punjab in 2024–2025. These are programme enrolments. [NFE Annual Statistical Report 2024–25, p.16, Table 1](https://pie.gov.pk/SiteImage/Publication/NFE%202024-25.pdf#page=21).
