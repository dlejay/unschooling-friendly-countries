# Home education in Pakistan

## Constitutional framework

Article 25A requires the State to provide free and compulsory **education** to children aged five to sixteen, in the manner determined by law. Its opening words are:

> The State shall provide free and compulsory education […]

School attendance and its exceptions are addressed by the separate territorial Acts linked below. [Constitution, Article 25A, p.15, consolidated to 21 November 2025](https://www.na.gov.pk/uploads/documents/6926e060076ed_467.pdf#page=37).

Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan expressly recognise sufficient education outside school as a reasonable excuse for non-attendance. Their respective sufficiency tests and competent authorities are set out in the individual files. Sindh and Islamabad impose school attendance subject to reasonable excuses, including circumstances concerning the child's capacity to attend or continue studying and the availability of a nearby school.

## Official statistics

The Pakistan Institute of Education's *Non-Formal Education Annual Statistical Report 2024–25* records 30,543 centres and 1,238,573 learners nationally. These are statistics for non-formal basic education, accelerated learning programmes and adult literacy centres; they measure enrolment in those programmes rather than parental home education. [Report, pp.5 and 16, Table 1](https://pie.gov.pk/SiteImage/Publication/NFE%202024-25.pdf#page=21).
