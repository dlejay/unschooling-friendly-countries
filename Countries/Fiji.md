# Unschooling in Fiji

## Compulsory schooling

Section 10(2) of the Education Act 2026 provides for compulsory school enrolment and regular attendance. Section 10(1) defines regular attendance by reference to each school day, subject to a reasonable excuse for absence. Section 10(8) provides exceptions, including exemptions granted by the Minister under section 11. [Education Act 2026, §§10–11](https://www.parliament.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Act-5-Education-Act-2026.pdf#page=15).

Under section 2, the compulsory-school-age range is prescribed by the Minister; the Act itself specifies no numerical starting or leaving age. Home schools have a separate approval procedure under section 58. [§2](https://www.parliament.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Act-5-Education-Act-2026.pdf#page=10); [§58](https://www.parliament.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Act-5-Education-Act-2026.pdf#page=29).

Parliament passed the Education Act 2026 on 25 May 2026; presidential assent followed on 2 June. Section 1(2) makes commencement dependent on a date or dates appointed by the Minister by notice in the Gazette. These provisions are subject to that commencement mechanism. [Parliament, May 2026 sitting statistics, Act 5](https://www.parliament.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Compiled-Parliament-Sitting-Statistics%E2%80%93May-2026.pdf); [Education Act 2026, §1](https://www.parliament.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Act-5-Education-Act-2026.pdf#page=9).

## Home education

### Eligibility and approval

Section 58 establishes an approval procedure involving a written application and a prescribed fee. Conditions include suitable premises, the student's best interests, an eligible instructor and other prescribed requirements. Section 58(1)(c) additionally requires that:

> there are not enough adequate schools already available in the proposed area to cater to the type of school proposed;

This restricts eligibility beyond parental preference. Conditions may accompany approval; refusals are appealable. [§58, printed p.41](https://www.parliament.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Act-5-Education-Act-2026.pdf#page=29).

### Educational approach

Section 23(2) requires home schools to meet a prescribed minimum threshold for curriculum, assessment and educational standards. Section 60 permits closure for persistent, material curriculum non-compliance. Pedagogical flexibility cannot therefore be equated with freedom from prescribed content. [§23, printed p.32](https://www.parliament.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Act-5-Education-Act-2026.pdf#page=20); [§60, printed pp.42–43](https://www.parliament.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Act-5-Education-Act-2026.pdf#page=30).

## Statistics

The Fiji Bureau of Statistics published *Education – 2024* on 7 August 2026. The publication covers educational establishments and expressly excludes educational activities in the informal sector. Its scope therefore does not provide comprehensive coverage of family-based learning. [FBoS, *Education – 2024*](https://www.statsfiji.gov.fj/education-2024/).

The Ministry's provisional 2025 Year 8 examination results were announced on 2 December 2025:

| Indicator | 2025 |
| --------- | ---: |
| Students registered for the Year 8 examination | 19,007 |
| Students who sat the examination | 18,628 |
| National pass rate | 69% |
| Schools achieving a 100% pass rate | 105 |

These indicators concern the national Year 8 examination cohort. The reported pass rate was also 69% in 2024, when 87 schools achieved a 100% pass rate. [FBC News, reporting the Ministry's announcement, 2 December 2025](https://www.fbcnews.com.fj/news/69-percent-pass-rate-achieved-for-year-8-examinations/); [The Fiji Times, 2 December 2025](https://www.fijitimes.com.fj/year-8-exam-results-to-be-released-tomorrow-pass-rate-at-69/).
