# Unschooling in Iran

## Educational obligation and school attendance

Article 2 of the [Protection of Children and Adolescents Act, 1399 (2020)](https://law-ir.ir/law/cat/1658/), applies the Act to everyone under eighteen. Article 7 requires parents, guardians and other responsible carers to enrol eligible children and provide for their education through the end of secondary education. Refusal or obstruction is subject to a court order to fulfil the duty and a statutory fine.

The [School Executive Regulations, approved 1400/05/10 (2021)](https://www.ekhtebar.ir/%D8%A2%DB%8C%DB%8C%D9%86%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%87-%D8%AC%D8%AF%DB%8C%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D8%AC%D8%B1%D8%A7%DB%8C%DB%8C-%D9%85%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%B3/), Article 54, give normal primary-grade ages of six to eleven for Grades 1–6. Article 55 separately provides (unofficial translation from Persian):

> Any educational activity by natural or legal persons … resulting in a pupil's non-enrolment and non-attendance at school is prohibited.

## Free candidates

Article 35 of the School Executive Regulations restricts assessment as a free candidate within Iran at primary level to **Grade 6**, with a **minimum age of fifteen**; earlier grades' pass certificates are not required. The minimum ages for lower- and upper-secondary free candidates are **sixteen and seventeen**, respectively, with proof of passing the preceding grade. The comprehensive lower-secondary examination route requires age eighteen and a Grade 6 pass certificate.

## Virtual schools: eligibility and permission

The [Virtual School Charter, adopted 1404/03/05 (2025), Article 5](https://www.irancodify.com/b/%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%B3%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%87_%D9%85%D8%AF%D8%B1%D8%B3%D9%87_%D9%85%D8%AC%D8%A7%D8%B2%DB%8C_%D9%85%D8%B5%D9%88%D8%A8_1404-03-05), provides a school-based distance-education route. For school-age Iranian applicants within Iran who are out of education, admission requires permission from the local education authority's special commission and one of these grounds:

- lack of access to a school and inability to provide in-person education, confirmed by the local authority;
- disability or a specified serious medical condition preventing school attendance, supported by the required competent-authority confirmation;
- a married girl applying for virtual schooling.

Article 5 also admits foreign applicants, Iranians abroad unable to access or attend classes, and adults over eighteen who have missed education.

## Programme and assessment

Article 6 of the Virtual School Charter requires an approved Iranian educational system and adapted curricula approved by the Organisation for Educational Research and Planning. Article 7 provides online registration, teaching, educational services and formative assessment.

Article 8 states (unofficial translation from Persian):

> Assessment and evaluation of virtual-school pupils' educational progress shall take place in person.

The same article places assessment under national evaluation oversight and allows special arrangements determined by the Supreme Council's designated commission under the applicable assessment rules.
