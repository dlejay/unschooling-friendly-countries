# Unschooling in the Australian Capital Territory

## Compulsory schooling

Compulsory education runs from age six until age 17 or completion of Year 12, whichever occurs first. Parents must arrange enrolment with an education provider or home-education registration ; school enrolment entails attendance duties. Alternative training and employment arrangements are available after Year 10. [Education Act 2004, §§9–10A and Part 2.4](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/a/2004-17/current/PDF/2004-17.PDF).

## Home education

### Eligibility

Parents with parental responsibility may register a child of compulsory education age who lives, or usually lives, in the ACT. No medical, religious or other exceptional reason is required. Part-time home education can be combined with school attendance through an agreement with the school. [ACT Government — eligibility and part-time registration](https://www.act.gov.au/education-and-training/find-a-school-and-enrol/home-education-home-schooling).

### Immediate start and initial registration

The Directorate states :

> You can start home educating your child as soon as you have submitted your complete application.

A complete application includes certified identity, parental-responsibility and residence documents. [ACT Government — new registrations](https://www.act.gov.au/education-and-training/find-a-school-and-enrol/home-education-home-schooling).

Section 131(6) excuses non-compliance with compulsory education requirements while the application awaits a decision. Registration nevertheless requires the Director-General to be satisfied that its conditions will be met. Section 131(7) expressly provides for registration or refusal within 28 days of a complete application. The choice remains subject to initial registration approval. [Education Act, §131](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/a/2004-17/current/PDF/2004-17.PDF#page=177).

For a new registration, the initial application does not require prior inspection of educational programmes. Quality, documentation and educational-record inspection conditions do not apply during the first three months after new registration. [Education Act, §§131(4)–(5) and 132(2)](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/a/2004-17/current/PDF/2004-17.PDF#page=177).

Within those three months, parents must submit a Statement of Intent explaining their educational approach, opportunities and learning strategies, and meet an authorised officer to discuss the education and show relevant documents. Current practice is an online review meeting. The Directorate does not conduct home visits, but may request photographs or videos of the home base. [Education Regulation 2005, §8(1)(a)](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/sl/2005-1/current/PDF/2005-1.PDF#page=21) ; [ACT Government — Statement of Intent and review meeting](https://www.act.gov.au/education-and-training/find-a-school-and-enrol/home-education-home-schooling).

### Educational approach

Section 128 recognises diverse educational philosophies and each child's needs, interests and aptitudes. [Education Act, §128](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/a/2004-17/current/PDF/2004-17.PDF#page=175).

The Directorate expressly states that the Australian Curriculum is optional. Parents develop their own strategies and may change them over time ; examples include games, free play, excursions and everyday activities. This accommodates interest-driven learning, subject to the requirements for educational quality, documentation and progress reporting. NAPLAN participation is optional. [ACT Government — educational approach, strategies and NAPLAN](https://www.act.gov.au/education-and-training/find-a-school-and-enrol/home-education-home-schooling).

### Annual reporting and renewal

The annual report must include examples of the child's educational progress. Regulation 10 defines progress as spiritual, emotional, social and physical development, together with intellectual development, including literacy and numeracy. The obligation concerns the child's development, in addition to documenting parental provision. For part-time home education, examples need only concern the home-educated component. [Education Regulation, §10](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/sl/2005-1/current/PDF/2005-1.PDF#page=25).

The Directorate requires submission by 31 December. Parents may choose their own format ; its templates are optional. [ACT Government — Home Education Report](https://www.act.gov.au/education-and-training/find-a-school-and-enrol/home-education-home-schooling).

Registration lasts at most two years. Renewal normally requires application three months before expiry. Registration may be cancelled for breached conditions, following written notice and an opportunity to respond. [Education Act, §§134–138](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/a/2004-17/current/PDF/2004-17.PDF#page=179).

Renewal documents include the latest progress report and a statement explaining educational provision during the previous registration and plans for the next period. [Education Regulation, §9](https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/DownloadFile/sl/2005-1/current/PDF/2005-1.PDF#page=23).

## Recent administrative developments

During 2025–2026, the Directorate met with parents of 319 registered children to review compliance. It provided introductory, record-keeping and reporting webinars, individual coaching, careers support and community meetings. Approximately 8.7% of home-educating parents chose the part-time option. These are administrative supports within the registration framework. [Education Directorate Annual Report 2025–26, printed p.123](https://www.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0010/3138994/ACT-Education-Directorate-Annual-Report-2025-26.pdf#page=129).

## Statistics

The Education Directorate reports registrations at the annual February census :

| Census year | Registered children |
| ----------- | ------------------: |
| 2026 | 610 |
| 2025 | 571 |
| 2024 | 495 |
| 2023 | 465 |
| 2022 | 413 |
| 2021 | 395 |

The reported increase in 2026 was 6.8%. These figures count registered ACT resident children across home-education approaches, including part-time arrangements ; they do not identify an unschooling subgroup. [Annual Report 2025–26, Table 13, printed p.55](https://www.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0010/3138994/ACT-Education-Directorate-Annual-Report-2025-26.pdf#page=61).

During the 2025–2026 reporting year, there were 303 new-registration applications and 186 renewal applications. The Directorate approved **100% of applications processed**, notified all new applicants within the statutory 28-day period, and cancelled no registrations. This describes administrative outcomes for that year, while the Act continues to permit refusal and cancellation. [Annual Report 2025–26, printed p.55](https://www.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0010/3138994/ACT-Education-Directorate-Annual-Report-2025-26.pdf#page=61).
