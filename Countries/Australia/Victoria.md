# Home education in Victoria

## Compulsory education and the home education route

Section 2.1.1 of the Education and Training Reform Act 2006 applies to children aged 6–17. Parents must either enrol the child at a registered school and ensure attendance, or register for home schooling and provide instruction in accordance with that registration. The latter route expressly permits:

> […] to register the child for home schooling in accordance with the regulations […]

[Education and Training Reform Act 2006, s.2.1.1, p.42](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-09/06-24aa111-authorised.pdf#page=84).

## Registration and continuation

Parents or guardians with a Victorian residential address apply to the Victorian Registration and Qualifications Authority (VRQA). The application requires evidence of the child's identity, age and parental responsibility, applicable court orders, and an individual learning plan. Applications and supporting documents are submitted by email. The VRQA must notify its decision within 28 days of receiving a complete application; home education may begin after approval, or on a specified later date. The VRQA may request further information and refuse registration if the response is insufficient. Applicants receive written notification and may appeal a refusal. [Regulations 2017, regs.73–74, p.65](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-06/17-44sra009%20authorised.pdf#page=73); [VRQA: register to home educate](https://www2.vrqa.vic.gov.au/register-home-educate).

Regulation 72 requires applications by 30 November for commencement at the beginning of the following school year; applications for other starting dates may be submitted at any time. Pending approval, school enrolment and attendance continue unless otherwise authorised under Part 2.1 of the Act. The first-year plan must specify timing, location, subject matter, resources and how learning outcomes will be recorded. Parents must notify the VRQA before 30 November each year whether home education will continue the following year. [Education and Training Reform Regulations 2017, regs.72 and 78, pp.63–64, 67](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-06/17-44sra009%20authorised.pdf#page=71).

## Content and learning methods

Regulation 76 requires regular, efficient instruction which:

> […] taken as a whole, substantially addresses the learning areas set out in Schedule 1 to the Act […]

Instruction must also reflect the principles and practice of Australian democracy. The VRQA may exempt a child from one or more learning areas where requiring instruction in them would be unreasonable. [Regulations 2017, reg.76, pp.66–67](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-06/17-44sra009%20authorised.pdf#page=74).

The eight areas cover English, mathematics, sciences, humanities and social sciences, arts, languages, health and physical education, and technologies. The VRQA states:

> You decide what content to include in your home education program. You don't have to follow the Victorian Curriculum or any other specific curriculum.

[VRQA: register to home educate, step 3](https://www2.vrqa.vic.gov.au/register-home-educate).

The learning-plan guidance permits self-directed learning and activities organised around the child's interests. It provides an activity-based template using natural learning across several learning areas, alongside a subject-based template. Parents may combine approaches, select an appropriate learning level, and use their own plan format with equivalent detail. [VRQA: develop a home education learning plan](https://www2.vrqa.vic.gov.au/develop-home-education-learning-plan).

## Reviews and assessment

The VRQA selects up to 10% of registrations for review annually and may undertake a specific review of compliance. It states:

> We don’t review your child or their learning outcomes. We only review whether you have met the registration requirements.

Parents demonstrate their programme through descriptions of learning activities, journals, work samples or photographs. Reviews may take place through emailed evidence, telephone or video conference. The VRQA communicates the result in writing and may require corrective action; failure to meet requirements can lead to cancellation. [VRQA: understand home education reviews](https://www2.vrqa.vic.gov.au/understand-home-education-reviews).

## Official statistics

At **30 June 2025**, the VRQA recorded **11,691 children in 8,154 households** registered for home schooling, compared with 11,240 children in 7,716 households in June 2024. [VRQA: home schooling trends and statistics](https://www2.vrqa.vic.gov.au/home-schooling-trends-and-statistics).
