# Unschooling in Queensland

## Compulsory schooling

Under sections 9 and 176 of the *Education (General Provisions) Act 2006*, parents must ensure school enrolment and attendance from age six years and six months until age 16 or completion of Year 10, whichever occurs first, subject to statutory exceptions. Section 199 exempts children provisionally registered or registered for home education. [Sections 9, 176 and 199](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2006-039).

A subsequent compulsory participation phase requires an eligible education, training or other participation option. It ends on obtaining a specified qualification, completing two years of eligible participation, or turning 17, whichever occurs first. Registered home education is an eligible option. [Departmental procedure, definitions](https://ppr.qed.qld.gov.au/pp/home-education-in-queensland-procedure).

## Home education

### Eligibility

Parents or legal guardians living in Queensland may choose home education without a medical, religious or other exceptional ground. The child must not remain enrolled in a state or non-state school. A parent may provide the education or work with a registered teacher. [Queensland Home Education](https://education.qld.gov.au/schools-educators/other-education/home-education).

Section 206 permits registration if the child will be at least five years and six months on 31 December of the application year, and until 31 December of the year the child turns 18. [Section 206](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2006-039).

### Initial approval and provisional registration

An ongoing-registration application uses the approved form, supporting identity and parental-responsibility documents, and an educational programme summary. Once lodged, it gives provisional registration under section 212 while the application is assessed. The Department explicitly states :

> Once you have lodged an application in the approved form with QHE, you will be provisionally registered and can begin home educating

Families can therefore begin before the final registration decision, while QHE assesses the initial application. QHE examines the proposed programme to decide whether the registration conditions, including a high-quality education, will be met. It may refuse registration if those conditions are not satisfied. The normal decision period is 90 days, restarting when requested further information is received. [Registration guidance](https://education.qld.gov.au/schools-educators/other-education/home-education/registration) ; [Departmental procedure, application process](https://ppr.qed.qld.gov.au/pp/home-education-in-queensland-procedure).

The provisional start remains subject to initial approval. A refusal can end provisional registration, subject to the applicable statutory review provisions. Queensland therefore does not meet the criterion that parents can start home education at will. [Sections 210–215](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2006-039).

A separate, temporary 60-day provisional-registration route under section 207 does not require an educational programme or annual report. The Department describes that temporary period as beginning on receipt of its confirmation email. [Registration guidance, “Temporary 60-day provisional registration”](https://education.qld.gov.au/schools-educators/other-education/home-education/registration).

### Educational approach and reporting

Parents must provide a high-quality education and report on educational progress annually. Section 217 requires the report two to three months before each registration anniversary. [Section 217](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2006-039).

The Department accepts individually designed learning plans and recognises that plans may change over the year. Its goal-directed, Australian Curriculum and thematic templates are optional. This accommodates an interest-driven approach, provided the programme supports the child's learning needs and educational progress. [Registration guidance, “Your plan for learning”](https://education.qld.gov.au/schools-educators/other-education/home-education/registration).

Annual review considers evidence of educational quality and progress, together with the next year's programme summary. The report concerns the child's educational progress, rather than only the parents' provision of educational opportunities. Registration may be cancelled if its conditions are not met or the chief executive is not reasonably satisfied about the child's progress, following the statutory show-cause process. [Departmental procedure, reporting process](https://ppr.qed.qld.gov.au/pp/home-education-in-queensland-procedure) ; [Sections 221–225](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2006-039).

## Documented unschooling practice

Public accounts describe families practising unschooling in Queensland :

- **2025 — Sandy Whiteman, Brisbane.** An ABC News report published on 21 October describes her family's unschooling approach, with learning through everyday activities and excursions rather than a prescribed curriculum. [ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-10-21/accidental-homeschooling-on-the-rise/105912808).
- **2024 — Sara, Brisbane.** An Australian Homeschool Stories interview published on 11 March presents her family's experience of unschooling and children directing their own interests and learning. [Interview and summary](https://australianhomeschoolstories.substack.com/p/sara-brisbane-qld-turrbal-country).
- **2022 — Departmental research.** The Department's November survey report on home-educating parents includes an anonymous respondent explicitly describing natural, child-led learning as unschooling, without structured curricula. [Research Insight Report, p.21](https://education.qld.gov.au/schools-and-educators/other-education/Documents/research-insight-report.pdf#page=21).

These accounts document self-described practice. They do not establish the registration status or administrative approval of each individual programme, or the proportion of registered families practising unschooling.

## Recent developments

The Government accepted the Home Education Unit review's recommendations. Implementation includes revised programme templates and report examples, the renamed Queensland Home Education service and a consultative group with regional parent representation. Further work includes exploring online reporting. These are administrative developments within the existing legislative framework. [Departmental review and response](https://qed.qld.gov.au/programs-initiatives/department/queensland-home-education-unit-review).

## Statistics

The Department reports the following **active home-education registrations at its August census** :

| Census year | Active registrations |
| ----------- | -------------------: |
| 2025 | 11,800 |
| 2024 | 11,314 |
| 2023 | 10,048 |
| 2022 | 8,461 |
| 2021 | 5,008 |

The 2025 census date was **1 August 2025**, and registrations increased by 4.30% from 2024. These figures count registered children across home-education approaches ; they do not identify an unschooling subgroup. [Departmental census data](https://education.qld.gov.au/schools-educators/other-education/home-education/qhe-data) ; [Verified 2025 dataset](https://qed.qld.gov.au/our-publications/reports/statistics/Documents/home-education-registrations.pdf).

### Initial applications and refusals

The Department reports these section 208 applications and refusals, with refusals expressed as a proportion of applications received in each collection year :

| Collection year | Applications received | Applications refused | Reported proportion |
| --------------- | --------------------: | -------------------: | ------------------: |
| 2025 | 5,782 | 520 | 9% |
| 2024 | 6,145 | 607 | 10% |
| 2023 | 5,720 | 543 | 9% |
| 2022 | 6,431 | 627 | 10% |
| 2021 | 2,827 | 292 | 10% |

The 2025 collection covers **3 August 2024 to 1 August 2025**. Refusals follow unmet registration requirements after a request for further information, and may concern procedural compliance or the educational programme ; these percentages do not measure rejection of educational plans alone. These administrative figures are indicative and have not been validated. [Departmental data, applications and refusals](https://education.qld.gov.au/schools-educators/other-education/home-education/qhe-data).
