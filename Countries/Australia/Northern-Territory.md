# Home education in the Northern Territory

## Compulsory education and school attendance

Compulsory school age begins on 1 January of the year in which the child is six by 30 June. It ends at 17, or earlier following Year 10 completion and full-time participation in an eligible option or exemption from that participation. Approved home education is expressly excluded from compulsory school enrolment. [Education Act 2015, ss.38–39, pp.19–21](https://legislation.nt.gov.au/api/sitecore/Act/PDF?id=11772).

Official guidance requires participation in approved education, training or work after Year 10 until age 17. [NT Government, stages of schooling](https://nt.gov.au/learning/primary-and-secondary-students/stages-of-schooling).

## Legal route and approval process

Section 46 allows parents of resident children of compulsory school age to apply to the Chief Executive Officer (CEO), who approves proposed home education if suitable. [Education Act 2015, s.46(1)–(5), pp.25–26](https://legislation.nt.gov.au/api/sitecore/Act/PDF?id=11772).

The Department states:

> Until the home education program is approved, your child must remain enrolled in a school and continue to attend.

Parents may teach without formal teacher training or teaching experience; an employed teacher must be registered in the Territory and hold a Working with Children Clearance. Applications are submitted online with a birth certificate, workspace photographs and the proposed programme, plus applicable health, legal and visa documents. A self-developed programme must use the Department's template. The home education unit assesses the application and communicates the decision by email. Approval lasts one school year and requires annual reapplication. Changes or cessation must be notified in writing within 14 days. [NT Government, home education](https://nt.gov.au/learning/primary-and-secondary-students/home-education).

Regulation 12 requires details of the curriculum, work in each subject, resources, instructional time and days, premises and facilities, and the instructor's identity and relationship to the child. It also requires:

> (f) how learning activities will be recorded and how the child's educational progress and achievement will be measured;

[Education Regulations 2015, reg.12, pp.5–6](https://legislation.nt.gov.au/api/sitecore/Act/PDF?id=11774).

## Curriculum, methods and assessment

Section 46(6)(a) provides that:

> […] the curriculum to be used must be one that is approved by the Australian Curriculum Assessment and Reporting Authority […]

The condition has exceptions for a CEO exemption based on special circumstances and for pre-commencement approval covering the immediately preceding school year. [Education Act 2015, s.46(6)–(8), p.26](https://legislation.nt.gov.au/api/sitecore/Act/PDF?id=11772).

Parents must retain learning evidence consistent with the topics and assessment tasks in the approved programme; samples may be requested at any time. For Years 11–12, official guidance requires 25 hours of educational learning weekly and permits subjects reflecting the child's interests, learning needs and future goals, including vocational courses. [NT Government, home education](https://nt.gov.au/learning/primary-and-secondary-students/home-education).

At least one inspection per approval period examines the curriculum, resources, premises and facilities, at a time agreed with the parent. The officer reports on suitability and gives the parent and CEO a copy. [Education Act 2015, s.47, p.27](https://legislation.nt.gov.au/api/sitecore/Act/PDF?id=11772).

## Official statistics

The Department reported **182 students approved for home education on 30 June 2025**. [Department of Education and Training, Annual Report 2024–25, p.54](https://education.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/1567599/det-annual-report-2024-25.pdf).
