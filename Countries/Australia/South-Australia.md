# Unschooling in South Australia

## Compulsory schooling

The Education and Children's Services Act 2019 distinguishes compulsory school age, from six until 16, from compulsory education age, covering 16-year-olds. Sections 60 and 68 require school enrolment and attendance during the former period. Sections 61 and 69 require enrolment and participation in an approved learning programme during the latter, subject to the statutory qualification exception. [Education and Children's Services Act, §§3, 60–61 and 68–69](https://www.legislation.sa.gov.au/__legislation/lz/c/a/education%20and%20childrens%20services%20act%202019/current/2019.19.auth.pdf).

Home education operates through a written exemption from school attendance under section 133. Exemptions may carry conditions. Section 133(3) permits the Minister to :

> vary or revoke an exemption for any reason the Minister thinks fit.

[Education and Children's Services Act, §133](https://www.legislation.sa.gov.au/__legislation/lz/c/a/education%20and%20childrens%20services%20act%202019/current/2019.19.auth.pdf#page=100).

## Home education

### Eligibility and initial approval

Parents may apply during the child's compulsory education years without a medical, religious or other exceptional reason. They accept responsibility for the education, resources, learning environment and social opportunities. [SA Government — Home education](https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/education-and-learning/schools/alternative-schooling/home-education).

The child remains enrolled at a South Australian school. All parents with parental responsibility normally consent. Application assessment includes a home visit, occasionally replaced by a virtual meeting, with the child present. The Minister's delegate decides whether to grant the exemption. [Departmental guide, pp.5–7 and 22](https://www.education.sa.gov.au/docs/curriculum/guide-to-home-education-in-south-australia.pdf).

After applying, parents may seek a four-week attendance exemption from the school principal. Starting during assessment requires that exemption to be granted ; submission alone does not authorise non-attendance. [Departmental guide, pp.20–22](https://www.education.sa.gov.au/docs/curriculum/guide-to-home-education-in-south-australia.pdf).

### Educational approach

The programme must meet the goals of the Australian Curriculum or similar, with tools to monitor progress. [SA Government — responsibilities](https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/education-and-learning/schools/alternative-schooling/home-education).

The guide permits natural learning, child-interest-based projects and integrated themes, without prescribing one teaching approach. However, parents must cover all eight curriculum areas and plan for twelve months, identifying topics, activities, resources and learning goals. Interest-driven learning can therefore operate within the approved programme's required breadth and planning framework. [Departmental guide, pp.5 and 10–12](https://www.education.sa.gov.au/docs/curriculum/guide-to-home-education-in-south-australia.pdf).

A historical parent application published by *The Educating Parent*, titled *South Australian Application Unschooling 2014*, describes learning through the children's interests, play and everyday experiences, with themes integrating several curriculum areas. This documents a proposed unschooling programme ; the document is not an exemption decision or evidence of current approval. [Published application](https://www.homeschoolaustralia.com/curriculum/sampleprograms/SA_Application_Unschooling.pdf).

### Review and continuation

Review usually occurs annually. Parents submit evidence of learning and the programme's implementation, prioritising literacy and numeracy, alongside the next twelve months' plans. A review meeting normally occurs at home, with the child present. Continuation depends on renewed exemption approval. [Departmental guide, pp.7 and 23–24](https://www.education.sa.gov.au/docs/curriculum/guide-to-home-education-in-south-australia.pdf).

## Recent developments

On 2 July 2025, the Department announced a review of non-mainstream education, expressly including home education. The subsequent *Reimagining public education beyond mainstream schooling* consultation sought experiences from families, young people and staff. Its public consultation is marked as concluded. These review and consultation activities are distinct from the exemption requirements set out in the Department's February 2026 guide. [Departmental announcement](https://www.education.sa.gov.au/department/media-centre/our-news/non-mainstream-schooling-review-to-ensure-student-engagement) ; [Government consultation page](https://yoursay.sa.gov.au/reimagining-public-education).

## Statistics

Published sources report the following home-education figures for South Australia :

| Year | Reported children | Source |
| ---- | ----------------: | ------ |
| 2024 | Approximately 2,800 | Home Education Association compilation |
| 2022 | 2,443 | Couper and Harding registration audit |
| 2021 | 1,795 | Couper and Harding registration audit |
| 2020 | 1,606 | Couper and Harding registration audit |
| 2019 | 1,360 | Couper and Harding registration audit |

The rounded 2024 figure is an association compilation, not a directly published departmental exemption census. The older figures come from a study published in 2024 ; its South Australian series is attributed to a personal communication from M. Price dated 23 February 2023. The sources do not establish a common census date, so these figures should not be combined into a precise growth series. They do not identify an unschooling subgroup. [Home Education Association — state information](https://www.hea.edu.au/state-information) ; [Couper and Harding, 2024, Table 4, p.912](https://www.iier.org.au/iier34/couper.pdf#page=7).
