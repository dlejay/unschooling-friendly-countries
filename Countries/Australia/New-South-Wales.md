# Home education in New South Wales

## Compulsory education and school attendance

Compulsory schooling begins at age six. It continues until age 17 unless the child has completed Year 10 and participates full-time in approved education or training, or, from age 15, paid work or a combination of work and approved education or training. Registered home education is an alternative to school enrolment and attendance. Section 22(1)(b) of the Education Act 1990 provides for a child:

> […] to be registered for home schooling under Part 7 and to receive instruction in accordance with the conditions […]

[Education Act 1990, ss.21B–22](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-1990-008); [official registration guidelines, §12](https://www.nsw.gov.au/nesa/regulation/home-schooling/registration-guidelines/legislative-basis-for-registration).

## Registration and approval

A parent or legal guardian of a child residing in NSW may apply to the Department of Education. Registration is available from the calendar year in which the child turns five by 31 July, including before compulsory school age. Parents retain responsibility for planning and delivering the programme. [Department: applying to register](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/home-schooling/applying-to-register).

Applications are submitted through the Home Schooling Platform or by emailing the application form. Supporting documents include proof of the child's identity, home address, and applicable court orders or guardianship documents. The Department states:

> It may take up to 12 weeks to complete the process from the time of application to receipt of a certificate.

Until registered with a current certificate, a child of compulsory school age must remain enrolled in and attend a government or registered non-government school, subject to applicable statutory exemptions. [Department: submitting an application](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/home-schooling/applying-to-register/submitting-an-application).

An Authorised Person assesses the proposed programme during a home visit and recommends approval or refusal. The assessment covers the child's learning needs, programme, planning and recording systems, progress records, learning time, resources and home environment. Registration lasts at most two years; initial periods are generally shorter. Renewal examines both the implemented programme and future plans. Failure to meet the requirements may lead to a refusal recommendation, for which internal review is available. [Registration guidelines, §6](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/home-schooling/registration-guidelines/6-application-and-assessment-process).

Parents with a conscientious religious objection to registration may seek exemption from registration. They must nevertheless demonstrate that the child would qualify for registration and that the same educational requirements would be met. Home education must be delivered primarily in the child's home. [Registration guidelines, §12](https://www.nsw.gov.au/nesa/regulation/home-schooling/registration-guidelines/legislative-basis-for-registration).

## Curriculum, methods and assessment

The registration requirements specify that the programme must be:

> […] based on and taught in accordance with the relevant NESA syllabuses […]

It must suit the child's learning needs, with sufficient learning time, adequate resources and records of teaching, progress and achievement. Adjustments are permitted for disability or additional learning needs. [Registration guidelines, §4](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/home-schooling/registration-guidelines/requirements-for-registration).

Primary education includes English, mathematics, science and technology, human society and its environment, creative arts, and personal development, health and physical education. Years 7–10 require English, mathematics, science and human society and its environment, plus two subjects from different areas among arts, technology, health/physical education and languages. Years 11–12 require an approved pattern of study. [Registration guidelines, §3](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/home-schooling/registration-guidelines/registration-at-a-glance).

Parents choose methods and resources, including projects, integrated themes and approaches based on the child's interests. Planning may use syllabus stage statements, outcomes or content. This methodological flexibility operates within the compulsory NSW syllabus requirements. [Registration guidelines, §5](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/home-schooling/registration-guidelines/methods-and-resources).

The Authorised Person normally sees the child and learning environment but does not assess the child. Renewal visits review implementation records. Additional monitoring visits may occur where information raises concerns about continuing compliance. [Department: assessment by an Authorised Person](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/home-schooling/applying-to-register/assessment-by-an-authorised-person); [guidelines, §6](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/home-schooling/registration-guidelines/6-application-and-assessment-process).

## Official statistics

The Department's June 2026 report records **13,385 children at 31 December 2025**, compared with 12,762 at 31 December 2024. These totals include children exempted from registration. In 2025, **5.27% of registration applications were refused**, including initial and renewal applications and religious exemptions; applications for Year 10 completion certificates are excluded. [Home Schooling Data Reports relating to 2025, pp.4, 15–16](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2026-06/home-schooling-data-reports-relating-to-2025.pdf).
