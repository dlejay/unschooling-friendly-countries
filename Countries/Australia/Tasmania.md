# Home education in Tasmania

## Compulsory education

Section 11 requires school enrolment or registered home education from the year in which a child is five on 1 January, until a transition statement is issued. [Education Act 2016, s.11](https://www.legislation.tas.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-2016-051).

The Department states that young people must remain in education or training until completing Year 12, obtaining a Certificate III, or turning 18, whichever occurs first. Approved home education is an alternative to school attendance. [Department for Education, Children and Young People, school starting and leaving ages](https://www.decyp.tas.gov.au/learning/primary-school-to-year-12/school-starting-leaving-ages/).

## Legal route and approval

Section 67(1) provides:

> A parent who intends to home educate his or her child may apply for approval of a proposed home education program […]

Applications cover one year and require the approved form and a proposed programme. [Education Act 2016, s.67(1), (5)](https://www.legislation.tas.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-2016-051).

Ministerial Instruction No 13 requires identification, evidence of the parental relationship and Tasmanian residence, and applicable parenting orders. Both parents' consent should be obtained where possible; otherwise, the applicant should supply an explanation. Provisional registration authorises commencement before final registration and excuses daily school attendance. The assessment appointment is confirmed at least four weeks beforehand; entry to premises requires consent, and the child is expected to be present unless otherwise approved. [Ministerial Instruction No 13, §§3.1–3.3, pp.2–3](https://documentcentre.education.tas.gov.au/_layouts/15/DocIdRedir.aspx?ID=TASED-1060461114-1374).

The Registrar decides after considering Tasmanian Home Education Advisory Council advice and an officer's report, including a visit and assessment against the standards. Renewal applications normally fall 12–15 weeks before expiry and include a summary of activities and learning. Withdrawal from school requires written notice to the principal that the child will be home educated. [Education Act 2016, ss.20, 67(2)–(5), 68–69](https://www.legislation.tas.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-2016-051).

## Content, methods and assessment

Regulation 5(2) states:

> There is no obligation for an approved home education program to provide a curriculum other than as specified in the standards […]

Schedule 1, clauses 2–11, establishes ten standards: diverse learning needs; a suitable home educator; pedagogy; safety, health and wellbeing; interpersonal skills; literacy; numeracy; a broad range of educational areas; future directions; and evaluation. The programme must specify learning methodologies and delivery, identify educational areas suited to the child's needs, and explain how progress will be evaluated. Future education, training and employment pathways must be addressed from age 13. These requirements establish educational standards while permitting parents to select content and methods suited to the child. [Education Regulations 2017, reg.5 and Schedule 1](https://www.legislation.tas.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/sr-2017-042).

Education must be provided primarily by the parent, who may use tutors and other activities and choose any days and hours. The Registrar may reassess continuing compliance and delivery. [Education Act 2016, ss.67(7), 75](https://www.legislation.tas.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-2016-051).

## Official statistics

On 30 June 2023, 1,441 children from 844 families were registered for home education. [Department for Education, Children and Young People, Annual Report 2022–23, p.190](https://documentcentre.education.tas.gov.au/_layouts/15/DocIdRedir.aspx?ID=TASED-1087178304-6195).
