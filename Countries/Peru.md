# Unschooling in Peru

## Compulsory education

Article 17 of the Peruvian Constitution provides that initial, primary and secondary education are compulsory.

Under *Ley N.º 28044*, Regular Basic Education comprises initial, primary and secondary education. Initial education serves children from birth to age five; primary education lasts six years and normally begins at age six; secondary education lasts five years.

Article 12 of *Ley N.º 28044* provides that initial, primary and secondary education are compulsory. It also states that parents, or those acting in their place, have a duty to ensure the timely enrolment of students and their continued attendance at educational institutions or programmes.

## Home education

Article 13 of the Constitution provides that:

> Parents have the duty to educate their children and the right to choose educational institutions and to participate in the educational process.

Article 5 of *Ley N.º 28044* similarly provides that parents, or those acting in their place, have the duty to educate their children and the right to participate in the educational process and to choose the institutions in which they are educated.

Peruvian educational regulations also provide for the *reconocimiento de estudios independientes* (recognition of independent studies). Under *Resolución Viceministerial N.º 094-2020-MINEDU*, a person who has undertaken studies independently may request an authorised educational institution to assess the competencies acquired and recognise the corresponding studies.

The assessment is carried out by reference to the competencies established for the relevant grade or educational level. MINEDU's current enrolment and school-management rules continue to provide for the recognition of independent studies.

## Context

Article 6 of the Constitution provides that:

> It is the right and duty of parents to nourish, educate and protect their children.

Article 13 establishes freedom of teaching and provides that parents have both a duty to educate their children and rights concerning the choice of educational institutions and participation in the educational process.

In *Sentencia 171/2021*, the Constitutional Court stated that the parental duty of education includes ensuring that children begin and complete formal basic education, comprising initial, primary and secondary education.
