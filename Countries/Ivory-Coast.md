# Unschooling in Ivory Coast

The quotations below are unofficial English translations of the French texts.

## Compulsory schooling

Article 10 of the [Constitution of 8 November 2016](https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.ci/sites/default/files/constitution_2016.pdf#page=4) provides:

> School attendance is compulsory for children of both sexes, under the conditions determined by law.

Article 2-1 of Law No. 95-696 of 7 September 1995, inserted by [Law No. 2015-635 of 17 September 2015](https://www.travaildesenfants.org/sites/default/files/pdf_documents_fondateurs/LOI%20N%C2%B0%202015-365%20DU%2017%20SEPTEMBRE%202015%20PORTANT%20MODIFICATION%20DE%20LA%20LOI%20N%C2%B0%2095-696.pdf#page=1), provides:

> Within the public education service, schooling is compulsory for all children of both sexes aged six to sixteen.

Article 17, as amended by the same law, requires enrolment and attendance:

> Parents whose children reach the age of six are required to enrol them in a school. Parents must ensure their children's regular attendance until the age of sixteen.

## Private and distance education

Article 5 of the [1995 Education Act](https://www.muscop-ci.com/Document/d6aab308c956a5558cca4e38410a5d18.pdf#page=2) subjects private educational establishments to state-defined requirements for opening, approval, operation and inspection. Article 7 provides:

> Schools, lower-secondary schools, training centres, upper-secondary schools and higher-education institutions are responsible for providing the public education service. This service may also be provided through a distance-education system.

## Enforcement and transitional provision

[Article 17-1, inserted in 2015](https://www.travaildesenfants.org/sites/default/files/pdf_documents_fondateurs/LOI%20N%C2%B0%202015-365%20DU%2017%20SEPTEMBRE%202015%20PORTANT%20MODIFICATION%20DE%20LA%20LOI%20N%C2%B0%2095-696.pdf#page=2), provides:

> A parent who breaches the compulsory-schooling obligation established by this law is punishable by imprisonment for two to six months and a fine of 50,000 to 500,000 CFA francs, or either penalty alone.

Article 17-2 set the end of 2025 as the deadline for progressively providing school infrastructure and qualified teaching and supervisory staff. Until then, the parental duty applied only where schooling was available in a public educational establishment near the family's residence.
