# Unschooling in Seychelles

## Compulsory schooling

Sections 49 and 57 of the Education Act 2004 require school attendance. Section 57 places the duty on parents :

> It is the duty of the parent of every child of compulsory school age to cause the child to attend school regularly.

Section 41 provides for educational programmes for children of compulsory school age and residents of Seychelles. The attendance obligation is not expressed as applying only to Seychellois citizens. [Education Act, §§41, 49 and 57](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/natlex2/files/download/69645/SYC69645%202012.pdf).

The Education (Amendment) Act 2017 defines compulsory education as Primary 1 to Secondary 5, or its equivalent, and compulsory school age by reference to those stages. This represents eleven years of primary and secondary education. Seychelles' national report to the UN Human Rights Council in 2026 confirms that the amendment remains in force. [2017 amendment, §2(1)(iii)–(iv)](https://seylii.org/akn/sc/act/2017/28/eng%402018-01-04/source) ; [2026 national report, §80](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/g26/024/10/pdf/g2602410.pdf).

## Home education and attendance exceptions

Section 45 of the Education Act provides a choice between State and private schooling. Section 58 excuses attendance where a student is excluded under another law, cannot attend because of illness or danger of infection, or has compelling family reasons. These are exceptions based on particular circumstances, rather than a general parental option to replace schooling with home education. Section 65 provides for penalties where parents neglect or refuse to secure attendance without an excuse under section 58. [Education Act, §§45, 58 and 65](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/natlex2/files/download/69645/SYC69645%202012.pdf).

The classification reflects this school-attendance framework and the absence of a general statutory home-education option. It does not imply that no families educate their children at home.

## Reported home education and unschooling

The family behind *Boho Family World* describes itself as practising worldschooling and unschooling in Seychelles. On Expat.com's Seychelles forum, parents also describe homeschooling on Mahé and Frégate. One contributor, identifying herself as local, reports educating children aged five and seven at home ; another describes living on Mahé with an unschooled son. These are personal accounts of practice, without documentation establishing the legal arrangements applicable to those families. [Boho Family World — Seychelles](https://www.bohofamilyworld.com/seychelles) ; [Expat.com — Playdate for homeschooled kids, posts 4, 17 and 33](https://www.expat.com/en/forum/africa/seychelles/835663-playdate-for-homeschooled-kids.html).

## Recent developments

**2026.** Cabinet approved the revised National Policy for Open and Distance Learning on 26 August. The Ministry launched it, together with its Strategic Plan 2026–2030, on 5 November. The official announcement describes flexible learning, digitally connected classrooms across the islands, and opportunities for adults and young professionals. These announcements do not establish a general exemption from compulsory school attendance for parent-directed home education. [State House — Cabinet decision, 27 August 2026](https://www.statehouse.gov.sc/news/7211/cabinet-business-wednesday-26th-august-2026) ; [State House — policy launch, 5 November 2026](https://statehouse.gov.sc/news/7336/ministry-of-education-today-launches-five-year-strategic-plan-and-revised-open-and-distance-learning-policy-for-a-stronger-education-system).

**2023.** A Ministry of Education study involving 130 participants examined homeschooling, e-learning and hybrid learning. Presented on 14 June, it recommended revising education law, clarifying requirements and structures, and considering home visits, parental qualifications and monitoring. The participants included educators, parents, school council representatives and PTA members ; the sample was not a count of homeschooled children. The findings concerned possible policy development. [Seychelles Nation, reporting the Ministry's presentation, 15 June 2023](https://www.nation.sc/articles/18205/seychelles-explores-new-schooling-methods-).

These figures cover enrolment in public and private schools, not home-education totals. The series were updated on 8 October 2026.
