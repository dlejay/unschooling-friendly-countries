# Unschooling in Bolivia

## Compulsory education

Article 81 of the Constitution provides that education is compulsory through
the *bachillerato*. Law No. 070 describes Regular Education as systematic,
regulated, compulsory and sequential, extending from *Educación Inicial en
Familia Comunitaria* through the end of secondary education.

The regular sequence comprises:

- three years of non-school-based early education, shared between the family,
  community and State;
- two years of school-based early education, ordinarily beginning at ages 4
  and 5;
- six years of primary education; and
- six years of secondary education leading to the *Bachiller Técnico
  Humanístico* diploma.

The 2026 rules nevertheless provide that a child who did not attend the two
school-based early-education years may be enrolled exceptionally and directly
in the first year of primary education.

## Home education

Neither the Constitution, Law No. 070 nor the 2026 rules for Regular Education
expressly recognises or regulates elective home education. They establish no
general parent-directed home-education route. Although Article 88(II) of the
Constitution recognises the right of mothers and fathers to choose the education
appropriate for their children, the Constitution defines the education system
by reference to public, private and agreement-based educational institutions.
Law No. 070 similarly characterises Regular Education as regulated and
compulsory.

The wider legal and administrative framework treats institutional enrolment and
school attendance as the means by which compulsory education is ordinarily
fulfilled. Ministerial Resolution No. 0001/2026 requires district education
authorities to identify students who are not schooled and promote their
enrolment in educational institutions. It also requires teachers to maintain
attendance records.

Under Articles 168 and 169 of the Code for Children and Adolescents, a child
protection judge may order a parent or guardian to enrol a child and control the
child's school attendance and academic progress. The judge may also order the
child to remain in school. Article 32 of Supreme Decree No. 2377 requires school
directors, teachers and administrative staff to report school dropout and
repeated unjustified absences to the municipal child-protection authority.

This interpretation is also reflected in constitutional case law. In SCP
0861/2016-S1, a case concerning disputed school registration rather than home
education, the Constitutional Court held that registration alone was
insufficient. It attributed responsibility to the parent for ensuring the
child's participation, attendance, completion of schoolwork and pedagogical
support.

Independent home education is therefore not established as an alternative means
of satisfying compulsory education. No published decision was identified in
which general parent-directed home education was accepted as equivalent to
institutional enrolment and attendance.

## Practice

Home education nevertheless exists in practice through arrangements commonly
described as *colegios sombrilla* or umbrella schools. Under these arrangements,
the child remains enrolled in a Bolivian educational institution, receives a
RUDE student registration and school reports, while most instruction takes
place at home.

In a 2022 interview, a representative of Homeschooling Bolivia estimated that
approximately 500 families were educating their children at home. The families
described in the report remained enrolled in partner schools; curriculum
providers supplied instruction and assessments, grades were transmitted to the
enrolling school, and at least one partner school required students to sit an
annual examination based on Ministry of Education content. The estimate was
provided by the association and is not an official statistic.

Homeschooling Bolivia's own guidance states that a school-age child who is not
enrolled in the national system is outside the requirements of Law No. 070. It
therefore recommends umbrella-school enrolment, but notes that the participating
schools change over time and that their identities are communicated privately
through the association's regional representatives.

Umbrella-school practice demonstrates that instruction can occur physically at
home while the child remains institutionally enrolled. It does not establish a
recognised home-education category, a general statutory entitlement or a route
shown to be available to every family. No official list, authorisation procedure
or current ministerial guidance for umbrella schools was identified.

The distance and virtual modalities introduced in 2020 do not provide a current
independent home-education route. They operated through educational institutions
and were created in response to the COVID-19 emergency. Supreme Decree No. 4260,
which established that framework, was abrogated by Supreme Decree No. 4449 in
January 2021. The current 2026 rules contain no corresponding general distance
or virtual modality for Regular Education.

## Official education at home

The official *Educación Sociocomunitaria en Casa* programme is distinct from
elective home education. It is limited to students with severe or very severe
disabilities who cannot attend a Regular Education institution. A participating
student must be enrolled both in a Special Education centre and in a Regular
Education institution, and a teacher provides education at the student's home.

The existence of this narrowly defined programme does not create an approval
route for general parent-directed home education.

## Recognition and return to school

Official school records and the Bolivian *Bachiller* diploma are issued through
recognised educational institutions. The 2026 rules do not establish an
independent examination through which a home-educated minor can convert
unregulated learning directly into completed Bolivian school grades.

A student entering the Regular Education subsystem without documents proving a
previous grade is assigned according to age. The institution's Technical
Pedagogical Commission must assess the student and prepare a levelling plan.
Studies formally completed abroad may instead be recognised under the
applicable equivalence rules. Enrolment in a Bolivian or foreign distance
education institution, or in a foreign school that formally enrols students
educated at home, is therefore legally distinct from independent unschooling
and may subject the student to that institution's curriculum and assessment
rules.

## Foreign children

The 2026 enrolment rules apply to Bolivian and foreign students. A foreign child
may enrol using Bolivian or foreign identity documents. Where identity
documents are unavailable, a sworn declaration may be accepted. Immigration
status may not be used to deny access to education.
