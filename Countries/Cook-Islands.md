# Unschooling in the Cook Islands

## Compulsory enrolment and attendance

Section 23 of the Education Act 2012 requires each parent or guardian of a
child resident in the Cook Islands to ensure that the child is enrolled at a
primary or secondary school no later than the child's fifth birthday. If that
birthday falls outside a school term, enrolment by the first instructional
day of the following term is sufficient.

The child must remain enrolled until turning 16. The attendance obligation
continues until the end of the final school term in the calendar year in which
the child turns 16: the parent must ensure attendance during school hours on
each day on which the school is open for instruction. Failure to comply with
the enrolment or attendance duty is an offence.

## Home-schooling exemption

Section 24(1)(b) expressly permits the Secretary of Education to exempt a
parent or guardian from the statutory school-attendance requirements where
the Secretary is reasonably satisfied that the child "is receiving equivalent
education by home schooling or in some other way".

Until the exemption has been granted, the parent remains subject to the
enrolment and attendance duties in section 23. Home education consequently
cannot lawfully begin immediately upon a unilateral parental decision.

## Drafting cross-references

The enacted text contains two inconsistent internal references. Section
23(7)(b), which supplies a defence to the attendance offence, refers to an
exemption by the Secretary "under section 23", although the exemption power
appears in section 24. Conversely, section 24(1) refers to compliance with
section 22, although section 22 concerns entitlement to attend a government
school and the compulsory duties appear in section 23.

The heading of section 24, its express reference to home schooling and its
relationship with the defence in section 23(7)(b) indicate that the two
provisions are to be read together as establishing an exemption from the
section 23 duties.

## Educational content and supervision

The substantive statutory test is equivalence of education. The Act does not
define "equivalent education" for this purpose.

Sections 83 and 84 separately provide for Education Guidelines, including a
curriculum framework for schools, and require the principal of an educational
institution to operate consistently with those Guidelines.

## Foreign families

Section 23 is framed by residence rather than nationality: it applies to the
parent or guardian of a child resident in the Cook Islands. Section 24 does
not impose a citizenship condition on the home-schooling exemption.
