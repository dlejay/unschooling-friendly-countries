# Unschooling in the U.S. Virgin Islands

## Compulsory education

Children must begin compulsory education at the start of the school year in
the calendar year in which they turn 5.

Attendance remains compulsory until the end of the school year nearest the
child’s 18th birthday, unless the child graduates earlier or qualifies for
another statutory exception. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

A bill introduced in 2025 would lower the compulsory starting age from 5 to 4,
but it was held in committee in October 2025 and has not been enacted. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

## Home education

Home education is permitted under Title 17, §84 of the Virgin Islands Code.

The statute provides that children may be educated at home under rules issued
by the Virgin Islands Board of Education, but only after written permission
has been granted by the Commissioner of Education. The Commissioner or an
authorised representative may also examine home-educated children. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

The current published Home Education Policy was amended in 2015 and approved
in its present form in April 2016. It defines home education as learning
outside a public or private school under the control of a parent or guardian.

The person providing the education must hold at least a high-school diploma or
an equivalent qualification. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

## Approval and starting procedure

Parents must submit a **Notice of Intent for Home Education** every year.

The notice must be filed at least 10 days before home education begins. Where
the child is already enrolled in school, the parents must also complete the
Department of Education’s withdrawal process and provide evidence of the
withdrawal. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

The Commissioner must issue an approval or disapproval decision within
15 calendar days after receiving the notice. A reason must be provided when
an application is refused. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

Home education therefore cannot legally begin immediately merely upon a
parent’s decision or the submission of a notice.

## Educational requirements

The home-education year follows the Department of Education’s public-school
calendar and must provide at least **1,080 hours of education for each child**.

Before the school year begins, the parent or instructor must prepare an
instructional plan stating, among other things:

- the instructor’s name and qualifications;
- the subjects to be taught;
- the days of instructional contact;
- the number of instructional hours per day; and
- the methods used to assess progress. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

The programme must include at least the following subjects:

### Primary level

- reading;
- language arts;
- mathematics;
- science;
- social studies; and
- electives.

### Secondary level

- English;
- mathematics;
- science;
- Virgin Islands culture and history;
- physical education;
- a foreign language; and
- digital literacy. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

Because prescribed subjects, instructional hours, assessment and progress
requirements apply, a completely child-led or exclusively interest-driven
unschooling programme would not satisfy the published rules.

Interest-led learning may still be used within a broader programme that
covers all mandatory subjects and complies with the other requirements.

## Assessment and oversight

Home-educated students must take the Virgin Islands Summative Assessment in
grades 3, 5, 7 and 11 at a location designated by the Department of Education.

A Department representative conducts an annual review of the child’s records
and progress. Parents must also maintain a portfolio containing a dated log of
educational activities and examples of the child’s work.

The portfolio must be submitted to the Department annually and retained by the
parent throughout the period of home education. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}

High-school students must comply with the Board of Education’s graduation
requirements. :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}

## Access

Neither §84 nor the published policy states that home education is restricted
to U.S. citizens.

Families residing in the territory can therefore ordinarily apply under the
same approval process, although immigration or residency status does not
remove the obligation to satisfy the home-education rules.
