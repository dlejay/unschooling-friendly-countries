# Unschooling in Syria

## Constitutional framework

Article 22 of the Constitutional Declaration of 13 March 2025 protects children's right to education and health care. Article 51 provides (unofficial translation from Arabic):

> Existing laws remain in force unless amended or repealed.

Source: [signed Constitutional Declaration, pp. 5 and 10](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/34/Constitutional_Declaration_of_the_Syrian_Arab_Republic_%282025%29.pdf).

In its [publication of 14 September 2026 on the Minister's parliamentary hearing](https://moed.gov.sy/news/11193), the Ministry of Education and Teaching expressly refers to implementing compulsory basic education under Law No. 7.

## Compulsory education and school attendance

Article 2 of **Law No. 7 of 22 February 2012 on compulsory education** states (unofficial translation from Arabic):

> All guardians of Syrian children, male and female, aged 6–15 are required to enrol their children in basic-education schools.

Article 2(a) provides for nine regular grades. Under Article 2(c), enrolled children continue until completion of basic education even beyond fifteen, under ministerial study and attendance rules.

Article 7(f) requires a warning after fifteen days' absence without excuse. Articles 11–12 provide proceedings against guardians refusing to send children to school following notice.

Source: [Parliament's publication of Law No. 7/2012, preserved PDF, pp. 1–3](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/r2e002/7d68c1bfed8da126140086e8ef8caf2e65228461.pdf); [original parliamentary record](http://parliament.gov.sy/arabic/index.php?node=201&nid=4329).

## Alternative provision and examinations

Article 2(b) provides school-attached classes for previously unenrolled or returning children aged 8–15. A Ministry-prescribed curriculum and study plan cover four years, leading to eligibility for further study or the basic-education certificate examination. Article 2(d) provides specialised institutes or centres for children with severe disabilities, and mainstream schooling for children with milder disabilities meeting ministerial inclusion criteria.

For general upper-secondary examinations, the Ministry also administers a qualifying test for *free candidates* in the scientific and literary streams. Its [notice of 6 October 2026](https://moed.gov.sy/news/11409) addresses the test date, prescribed subjects and units, and passing percentage. This is an examination candidature arrangement; the enrolment and attendance duties during basic education are set out in Law No. 7.

## Official statistics

At the [parliamentary hearing published by the Ministry on 14 September 2026](https://moed.gov.sy/news/11193), the Minister estimated that approximately **two million pupils were out of school**. He forecast that enrolment could reach **six million during the current school year**. The former is a dropout estimate and the latter an enrolment forecast; neither figure measures home education.
