# Unschooling in East Timor

## Compulsory schooling

Basic education is compulsory and lasts nine years under Article 13 of the framework annexed to Law No. 4/2026 of 1 April. Entry normally requires turning six by 31 December before the school year begins. Article 13(5) expressly refers to compulsory attendance (*obrigatoriedade de frequência*), ending at the end of the school year in which the pupil turns seventeen. Preschool and secondary attendance are optional under Articles 10 and 16. [2026 Education Framework, Articles 10, 13 and 16, printed pp.300–302](https://www.mj.gov.tl/jornal/public/docs/2026/serie_1/SERIE_I_NO_13.pdf#page=15).

School enrolment is expressly required by Ministerial Diploma No. 69/2016 of 21 December, which regulates enrolment in public basic schools. Article 6, headed “Enrolment obligation”, provides, in translation :

> Enrolment is compulsory for all children and young people of compulsory school age, aged between 6 and 17, who have not completed basic education.

Article 3 defines enrolment as the formal registration of a child or young person to pursue studies in a specific school. Article 16 places responsibility for enrolling a minor on the parent or other person responsible for the child. These provisions impose school registration, rather than merely a general duty to provide education. [Ministerial Diploma No. 69/2016, Articles 1–3, 6 and 16, printed pp.755–758](https://www.mj.gov.tl/jornal/public/docs/2016/serie_1/SERIE_I_NO_49_pdf#page=12).

The subsequent school-staffing regulation expressly refers to the 2016 enrolment regulation in Article 9(5). Decree-Law No. 32/2023 also requires public authorities and public basic schools to promote school entry and attendance and monitor non-attendance ; schools must monitor dropout among enrolled pupils and engage with their parents or other responsible adults. [Ministerial Diploma No. 84/2023, Article 9(5), printed p.2432](https://www.mj.gov.tl/jornal/public/docs/2023/serie_1/SERIE_I_NO_49.pdf#page=30) ; [Decree-Law No. 32/2023, Article 11, printed p.1132](https://www.mj.gov.tl/jornal/public/docs/2023/serie_1/SERIE%20I%20N.%2020%20NORMAL.pdf#page=131).

## Home education and distance learning

Article 19 of the 2026 framework lists inclusive education, specialised artistic education, recurrent education and distance learning as special modalities, all remaining part of school education. Articles 53 and 58 also recognise licensed private and cooperative educational provision. The framework does not establish a general parental right to replace compulsory school enrolment and attendance with independent home education. [2026 Education Framework, Articles 19, 53 and 58](https://www.mj.gov.tl/jornal/public/docs/2026/serie_1/SERIE_I_NO_13.pdf).

Compulsory schooling does not necessarily require daily physical attendance. Article 23 recognises distance learning as a complementary or alternative modality of school education, requiring content, methods and assessment equivalent to face-to-face provision. It also allows distance learning to replace in-person attendance in exceptional circumstances, including individual impediments. This is a recognised modality of school education, distinct from autonomous parental provision. [2026 Education Framework, Article 23, printed pp.304–305](https://www.mj.gov.tl/jornal/public/docs/2026/serie_1/SERIE_I_NO_13.pdf#page=18).

### The “Eskola iha Uma” programme

Decree-Law No. 35/2022 and Ministerial Diploma No. 37/G-MEJD/VIII/2022 established *Eskola iha Uma*, officially also called “Homeschooling”. Under the 2022 implementing rules, it targets children aged five to seven outside preschool or the first year of basic education, excluding pupils already attending Year 2 or above. Ministry-contracted teachers provide support through mobile teams and local learning centres, preparing children for entry into Year 1 or progression to Year 2. [Ministerial Diploma, Articles 1–5 and 13, printed pp.1536–1539](https://www.mj.gov.tl/jornal/public/docs/2022/serie_1/SERIE_I_NO_34.pdf#page=52).

The pilot was designed for 195 hours, with a weekly load of fifteen hours. It uses the essential national curriculum, adapted to the programme's duration, and assesses learning progress. This is targeted public educational provision, rather than a general parental choice to replace compulsory schooling with independent home education. [Ministerial Diploma, Articles 3, 8–10 and 18](https://www.mj.gov.tl/jornal/public/docs/2022/serie_1/SERIE_I_NO_34.pdf#page=54).

## Recent developments

**2026.** Parliament approved the new framework on 9 March. Law No. 4/2026 was promulgated on 26 March and published on 1 April, entering into force ninety days after publication. It repeals Law No. 14/2008. [Government — parliamentary approval](https://timor-leste.gov.tl/?lang=en&p=47746&print=1) ; [Law No. 4/2026, Articles 3 and 7, printed p.297](https://www.mj.gov.tl/jornal/public/docs/2026/serie_1/SERIE_I_NO_13.pdf#page=11).

**2023.** The Government's first-120-days report described *Eskola iha Uma* as serving children aged three to seven without access to basic education and announced restructuring to improve school readiness, learning materials and implementation. This later programme description should be distinguished from the age limits in the 2022 implementing rules. [Government — first-120-days report, education section](https://timor-leste.gov.tl/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/120-dias-relatorio-EN-SCREEN-2023-12-19.pdf).

## Statistics

The Ministry's EMIS tables report the following national enrolments in public and private schools :

| Year | Basic education, Cycles 1–3 | Secondary education |
| ---- | -------------------------: | ------------------: |
| 2025 | 298,236 | 73,406 |
| 2024 | 297,603 | 77,460 |

Both tables identify 6 January 2026 as their EMIS extraction date. Basic education combines primary Cycles 1–2 and pre-secondary Cycle 3. These are school enrolments, not home-education or unschooling totals. [Ministry — 2025 table, National row](https://moe.gov.tl/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/0.-Number-of-Students-District-wise-by-Education-Level-by-School-Type-and-by-Gender-2025.xlsx) ; [2024 table, National row](https://moe.gov.tl/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/0.-Number-of-Students-District-wise-by-Education-Level-by-School-Type-and-by-Gender-2024.xlsx).

**2023.** Reporting statements by the national preschool education director on 24 August, TATOLI described the first *Eskola iha Uma* phase in Díli as reaching more than **600 beneficiaries from 81 villages**, with **37 centres** already established. These are programme-specific figures, not a count of independently home-educated children. [TATOLI — programme implementation, 24 August 2023](https://tatoli.tl/2023/08/24/estajiariu-husi-universidade-tolu-sei-apoia-programa-eskola-iha-uma-faze-daruak/).
