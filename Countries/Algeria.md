# Unschooling in Algeria

## Compulsory schooling

Article 12 of Law No. 08-04 of 23 January 2008 makes education compulsory from age six until 16. Compulsory schooling may be extended by two years for pupils with disabilities where necessary. Parents or legal guardians who breach the obligation face fines of 5,000 to 50,000 Algerian dinars. Article 14 provides for appropriate educational support and school integration for pupils with disabilities or chronic illnesses. [Education Framework Law, Articles 12 and 14, printed p.9](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/JO-FRANCAIS/2008/F2008004.pdf?znjo=04#page=9).

Executive Decree No. 10-02 of 4 January 2010 requires parents or guardians to enrol children in the basic school serving their geographical area. Municipal registration lists cover children born and/or residing in the municipality. [Articles 2–3, printed p.5](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2010/F2010001.pdf#page=5).

## Home education and unschooling

Article 8 of Decree No. 10-02 requires regular school attendance and compliance with school regulations. In translation :

> Pupils must attend school regularly and pursue their studies in accordance with the school regulations in force.

Attendance is monitored daily under the school director's responsibility. Articles 9–11 provide for parental notification, formal notice and referral to the public prosecutor for repeated unjustified absences persisting over a term despite notice. Article 13 expressly repeals Decree No. 76-66 of 16 April 1976. [Decree No. 10-02, Articles 8–13](https://www.joradp.dz/FTP/jo-francais/2010/F2010001.pdf#page=5).

This framework requires enrolment and attendance rather than providing a general parental choice to replace schooling with home education. The classification reflects that restriction.

### Institutional distance learning

The National Office for Distance Education and Training (ONEFD) provides a separate institutional route. At the May 2026 level-attestation examination, its director described distance learning as a second chance to improve educational attainment and work towards the middle-school certificate or baccalaureate. Candidates take examinations corresponding to the middle and secondary cycles. This is a route tied to formal educational levels and assessment ; its existence does not establish a general right to parent-directed unschooling throughout the compulsory years. [Algerian Radio, reporting the ONEFD director's statements, 24 May 2026](https://news.radioalgerie.dz/ar/node/86853).

## Reported practice

In an interview published on 1 February 2010, a mother of six identifying her location as Algiers described educating her children at home after leaving France. She characterised her approach as mainly informal, drawing on Freinet pedagogy for core subjects and adapting learning to the children's interests and rhythms. This is a historical first-person account of home education ; it does not document an exemption from the attendance obligation. [Al-Kanz — interview with Ariss](https://www.al-kanz.org/2010/02/01/ecole-maison-interview/).

## Statistics

For the **2025–2026 school year**, ONEFD's director reported the following candidate numbers for the national level-attestation examination held on 24 May 2026 :

| Cycle | Candidates |
| ----- | ---------: |
| Middle | 272,900 |
| Secondary | 427,200 |
| **Total** | **700,100** |

The examination used 2,369 centres nationwide. The report also identified 37,503 candidates in penal institutions across the two cycles. These figures describe institutional distance-learning examination candidates, including learners outside the usual school setting ; they are not counts of homeschooled children or unschoolers. [Algerian Radio — ONEFD examination, 24 May 2026](https://news.radioalgerie.dz/ar/node/86853).
