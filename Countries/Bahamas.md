# Unschooling in The Bahamas

## Compulsory education

The Education Act, 2026, which repealed and replaced the former Education Act,
recognises public schools, private schools and home schools as the three
classifications of schools in The Bahamas.

A child is of compulsory school age once they have attained the age of four
and until they attain the age of seventeen, unless they satisfy the
requirements for secondary-school graduation earlier.

A parent must ensure that a child of compulsory school age receives full-time
education suitable to the child's ability and aptitude through regular
attendance at one of the recognised classifications of schools, including a
registered home school.

## Home education

Part VII of the Education Act, 2026 specifically regulates home schooling.

A parent or legal guardian may assume responsibility for educating their child
at home as a “parent practitioner”, provided that the parent practitioner is at
least 21 years old and complies with the Act.

Before beginning the home education programme, the parent practitioner must:

- register the child with the Department of Education;
- renew the registration annually;
- submit a plan outlining the programme for each child;
- ensure that the programme is intended to operate for at least one school
  year; and
- ensure that it meets the standards established by the national curriculum.

A parent practitioner is not required to reproduce or implement the national
curriculum exactly. However, the education provided must meet at least the
standards and learning outcomes established through that curriculum.

The child must participate in suitable national assessments and any other
assessments administered by an approved authority.

Home education may include tutors, field trips, tutoring sessions, social
activities and flexible hours. However, the programme must provide at least
900 hours of instruction per year.

The parent practitioner must maintain:

- the dates and times during which instruction was provided; and
- a portfolio of the child's work.

The parent must also report on the child's progress when required. The
Department of Education may monitor the child's scholastic progress, consider
assessment results and recommend changes where the child is not making
reasonable progress.

The Department may modify or terminate a home education programme if it
concludes that the programme no longer complies with the Act or that the child
has failed to meet the applicable learning outcomes, as measured by achievement
testing. A parent may appeal that decision to the Minister within 14 days, but
the Minister's decision is final.

The Ministry's current administrative guidance states that annual registration
normally opens on the third Monday in August and closes on 30 April of the
following year. The Ministry asks for an online application and supporting
documents including identification, the child's birth certificate, National
Insurance Card and immunisation record.

## Unschooling

The current framework permits flexible schedules, experiential learning and
considerable freedom over teaching methods. It does not, however, permit
unrestricted unschooling in which the child follows only their interests
without prescribed educational outcomes.

Any interest-led or child-led approach must still:

- meet national-curriculum standards;
- deliver at least 900 instructional hours per year;
- be described in a programme plan submitted before commencement;
- maintain records and a portfolio;
- include approved assessments; and
- demonstrate satisfactory progress against applicable learning outcomes.

Older Ministry forms or brochures reportedly included “unschooling” as an
educational approach. That option should not be interpreted as an exemption
from the requirements introduced by the Education Act, 2026. The present
online application could not be independently inspected, and the Ministry's
current public guidance does not expressly list unschooling as a separate
category.

## Statistics

In March 2021, the Minister of Education reported that more than 2,000 students
had registered for homeschooling during the preceding four years. This was a
cumulative registration figure rather than the number being homeschooled at
one particular date.

No more recent official national total was located.
