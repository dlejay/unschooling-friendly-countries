# Home education in Bhutan

## Free education and compulsory attendance

Article 9(16) of the Constitution places an obligation on the State:

> The State shall provide free education to all children of school going age up to tenth standard […]

This is a guarantee of publicly provided education. It is distinct from a parental obligation to enrol a child or ensure school attendance. [Constitution of the Kingdom of Bhutan, Article 9(16), p.20](https://www.icnl.org/wp-content/uploads/Bhutan_TsaThrim.pdf#page=28).

In its dialogue with the Committee on the Rights of the Child on 15 May 2024, the Bhutanese government expressly described primary education as non-compulsory. The official meeting record states:

> A representative of Bhutan said that, while no specific legislation made primary education compulsory […]

The representative distinguished this position from the constitutional obligation to provide free education. [Government of Bhutan's statement, CRC/C/SR.2801, paragraph 22, p.5](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?l=en&s=CRC%2FC%2FSR.2801&t=pdf).

## Education outside school

The National Education Policy, approved on 17 January 2025, provides for monitoring the needs of out-of-school children and youth and facilitating either their return to formal education or alternative learning pathways, including skills development and training. Section 11.1 provides for literacy and numeracy programmes across age groups, using learner-centred approaches, self-directed learning, flexibility, and combinations of face-to-face and digital learning. [Prime Minister's Office: approved policies](https://pmo.gov.bt/policies/); [National Education Policy, §§10.1–10.2 and 11.1, pp.16–17, ministry-supplied text](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1rcN-woaZpI87DpNi03KEvWasixbb2IH7/view).

## School curriculum and assessment

Section 7 of the Policy governs the school curriculum from Pre-Primary to Class XII. It provides for national and subject-specific curriculum frameworks specifying knowledge, skills, values and assessment at each stage. Section 12.1 requires schools to meet the Ministry's basic education standards, while §12.4 assigns the Ministry approval and regulation of private, civil-society and international schools and other educational institutions. [National Education Policy, §§7.1–7.2 and 12.1–12.4, pp.9–10, 17](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1rcN-woaZpI87DpNi03KEvWasixbb2IH7/view).

## Official statistics

The Ministry's **August 2026** school summary reports **146,201 students in public schools and 8,540 in private schools**, alongside 517 public and 30 private schools. These are school-enrolment figures. [Ministry of Education and Skills Development: school, teacher and student summary](https://education.gov.bt/).
