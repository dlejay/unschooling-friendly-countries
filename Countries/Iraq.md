# Unschooling in Iraq

The quotations below are unofficial English translations of the Arabic texts.

## Federal compulsory schooling provisions

[Article 1(I) of the Compulsory Education Law No. 118 of 1976](https://ailawyeriq.cloud/ar-IQ/laws/details?id=1152)
provides:

> Primary education is free and compulsory for all children who reach the
> age of six at the beginning of the school year or by 31 December of that
> year.

Article 1(III) specifies the guardian's obligation:

> The child's guardian must enrol the child in primary school upon reaching
> the age specified above and ensure the child's continued attendance until
> completion of primary education or attainment of the age of fifteen.
>
> For the purposes of this Law, the child's guardian means the person
> actually responsible for the child's care.

Article 14 exempts the following cases from the penalties under article 13:

> I. A child whose disability prevents education in ordinary schools, where
> suitable special education is not available nearby.
>
> II. Illness preventing education in ordinary or special schools, supported
> by a certificate from a medical committee.

## Electronic schooling

The Ministry of Education's [Electronic School](https://eschool.iq/)
provides the official curriculum digitally to pupils registered under the
*intisab* system, who remain attached to their original school. Its
[FAQ](https://eschool.iq/faq) identifies the target group as pupils in the
intermediate and preparatory stages. Lessons and examinations follow a
weekly timetable; the platform also provides assignments, teacher contact
and monitoring of pupils' results.

## Kurdistan Region

[Law No. 13 of 2022 on Education in the Kurdistan Region–Iraq](https://legislation.krd/law-detail/?id=5821)
defines basic education as nine grades in article 1(XII). Article 9 provides:

> I. The guardian of every child who has reached the age of six by
> 31 December must enrol the child in a basic education school.
>
> II. Formal basic education is compulsory for everyone who has not
> exceeded the age of sixteen. The pupil must complete this education
> before exceeding the age of nineteen.

Article 11(I) provides:

> No guardian may refuse to enrol their child in a basic education school
> or to ensure continued attendance, except for a medical reason supported
> by a medical report certified by a competent medical committee. Each
> report is valid for one academic year and must be renewed annually.

Under article 11(II), a guardian who refuses enrolment or continued
attendance outside this exception is liable, after one warning and an
order to enrol the child, to a fine of at least IQD 500,000. The penalty
is doubled for a repeat violation, without prejudice to heavier penalties
under other applicable regional laws.
