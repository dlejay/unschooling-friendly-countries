# Unschooling in South Ossetia

## Compulsory education

The Constitution of the Republic of South Ossetia provides that **basic general education is compulsory** and places responsibility on parents or legal representatives for ensuring that children receive such education. The Constitution also recognizes different forms of education and self-education.

The Law of the Republic of South Ossetia *On Education* provides that **primary general, basic general, and secondary general education constitute compulsory levels of education**. The requirement to obtain secondary general education applies until the student reaches the age of 18, unless secondary general education has been completed before that age.

Within the general education system, basic general education concludes after **grade 9**, while secondary general education comprises **grades 10–11**. The regulations governing family education expressly address students in grades 1–11.

## Home education

The Law *On Education* provides for education outside educational organizations in the forms of **family education** (*семейное образование*) and **self-education** (*самообразование*).

Under Article 10, education may be obtained:

1. in organizations carrying out educational activities; or
2. outside such organizations, in the forms of family education and self-education, in accordance with procedures established by the competent executive authority in the field of education.

The Law further provides that education in the forms of family education and self-education may be accompanied by intermediate and state final certification in educational organizations.

Parents or legal representatives have the right to provide a child with preschool, primary general, basic general, and secondary general education within the family. In selecting the form of education before completion of basic general education, the child's opinion must be taken into account, together with the recommendations of a psychological-medical-pedagogical commission where applicable.

The detailed procedure is set out in Ministry of Education and Science **Order No. 54 of 21 March 2022**, which regulates family education and self-education.

Under Order No. 54, family education is available to students by choice and may be adopted at any level of general education. The child's opinion is to be taken into account when the form of education is selected.

### Notification

Where a child is already enrolled in an educational organization, the parents or legal representatives submit an application to the head of that organization concerning the child's transfer to family education.

The parents or legal representatives must subsequently notify the competent city or district education authority:

* within **15 calendar days** following the educational organization's order removing the child from its enrolment in connection with the transfer to family education; or
* at least **15 calendar days before the beginning of the school year** where family education is selected in advance of that school year.

The applicable procedure prescribes notification to the competent education authority rather than an application to that authority for prior authorization.

The local education authority records the child in the register of children subject to education who are not attending educational organizations.

A child receiving family education may subsequently continue education in an educational organization. The legislation also permits combinations of different forms of education.

### Curriculum and assessment

State educational standards apply to education received in the family. Parents or legal representatives are responsible for ensuring that the child masters the applicable general educational programme in accordance with those standards.

Children receiving family education may be enrolled in an accredited educational organization as external students for the purposes of intermediate and state final assessment.

Order No. 54 provides for intermediate assessment according to the following schedule:

* **grade 1:** once annually, in April–May;
* **grades 2–11:** twice annually, in December and May.

The subjects included in intermediate assessment are determined by the applicable curriculum. Assessment materials must correspond to the relevant state educational standard and educational programme.

The model agreement contained in Order No. 54 assigns responsibility to parents or legal representatives for ensuring that the child undergoes intermediate and final assessment and masters the educational programme within the applicable state general-education standards.

An unsuccessful intermediate assessment, or failure to undergo an assessment without a valid reason, results in an **academic debt**. The regulations provide opportunities for the student to retake the relevant assessment. A student receiving family education who does not eliminate the academic debt within the prescribed period is required to continue education in an educational organization.

State final assessment is used for the award of official certificates of basic general and secondary general education.

The regulatory framework therefore requires children receiving family education to follow the applicable state educational standards and educational programme and to undergo the prescribed assessments. The legislation does not prescribe a particular day-to-day pedagogical method for instruction within the family.

Order No. 54 also contains provisions applicable to foreign nationals. Where a foreign parent or legal representative registers a child as an external student for assessment, documentation establishing the parent's or legal representative's right to reside or stay in South Ossetia must be submitted.
