# Unschooling in Wales

## Compulsory education

In Wales, education is compulsory, but attendance at school is not.

A child becomes of compulsory school age on the first of 31 March, 31 August or 31 December following their fifth birthday. Compulsory school age ends on the last Friday in June of the school year in which the child turns 16.

## Unschooling

Parents may fulfil their duty under section 7 of the Education Act 1996 by providing an efficient, suitable, full-time education at home.

The education must be suitable to the child’s:

* age
* ability
* aptitude
* additional learning needs, if any

Parents do not need teaching qualifications and are not required to follow the Curriculum for Wales, school hours, school terms, a fixed timetable or a prescribed syllabus. Welsh statutory guidance expressly recognises autonomous and child-led education as legitimate approaches. Education may therefore be interest-driven, provided that it remains full-time, efficient and suitable and gives the child opportunities to make progress across a reasonably broad range of learning experiences.

### Starting home education

If a child has never attended school, parents are not currently required to register the child with or notify the local authority.

If the child is registered at a school, the parent must notify the headteacher in writing that the child is being withdrawn to be educated otherwise than at school. The school must then remove the child from its admissions register and notify the local authority.

Advance permission from the local authority is not normally required. However, local-authority consent is required before a child registered at a special school under arrangements made by the authority can be removed from the school roll.

## Local-authority oversight

The Welsh Government first published statutory elective home-education guidance for local authorities in May 2023. The currently published version was updated in March 2025.

The guidance expects local authorities to make enquiries so that they can determine whether a child is receiving a suitable education. Authorities are encouraged to meet the family, see examples of learning and seek the child’s views.

Under the current law, however, parents cannot be compelled solely under the home-education guidance to admit an officer to their home or participate in a meeting. Parents may provide evidence in other ways. A refusal to engage does not by itself prove that the education is unsuitable, although an authority may begin the school-attendance-order process if the information available does not satisfy it that a suitable education is being provided.

An evaluation of the statutory guidance was published in January 2026. The Welsh Government subsequently approved targeted amendments to the guidance in April 2026, but as of 26 July 2026 the current published guidance remains the March 2025 version.

## Children’s Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026

The Children’s Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 received Royal Assent in April 2026 and contains new “children not in school” provisions for Wales.

When these provisions are brought into force in Wales, they will introduce:

* local-authority registers of children not in school
* a duty on parents to notify the local authority and provide specified information
* a requirement for home-educating parents to provide information about the education being delivered
* information duties for certain out-of-school education providers
* additional local-authority powers to request meetings with families and children
* local-authority consent before certain children subject to child-protection processes can be withdrawn from school
* changes to the school-attendance-order process

The reforms will not require home educators to follow the Curriculum for Wales, use fixed timetables or abandon autonomous or child-led education.

These Welsh provisions are **not yet operational**. Their practical details, commencement date and implementation arrangements must be established through Welsh secondary legislation and guidance, following consultation and Senedd scrutiny. Until that happens, the existing legal framework continues to apply.

## Children Missing Education database pilot

A separate Children Missing Education (CME) database pilot operated in seven Welsh local-authority areas.

The pilot regulations came into force on 8 April 2025 and applied until 8 April 2026. They required health bodies and independent schools to provide limited identifying information so that local authorities could identify children for whom they had no record of an educational placement.

The CME database was not a register of home-educated children. A child identified through the matching process was to be removed from the CME database once the local authority was satisfied that the child was receiving a suitable education.

The pilot evaluation was published on 24 March 2026. It found that health data helped identify large numbers of potentially missing children, but that inaccurate or outdated records, cross-boundary school attendance and inconsistent matching systems produced many false or unresolved matches.

The evaluation concluded that expanding the pilot in its existing form would not be an effective use of resources until data-quality problems and inconsistencies in local-authority processes had been addressed. No nationwide CME database had therefore been implemented as of 26 July 2026.

## Statistics

These figures record children **known to local authorities** to be electively home educated. The actual total may be higher because, under the current system, families whose children have never attended school are not required to notify the local authority.

| Academic year | Children known to be home educated |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------: |
| 2024/25       |                              7,176 |
| 2023/24       |                              6,156 |
| 2022/23       |                              5,330 |
| 2021/22       |                              4,681 |
| 2018/19       |                              2,517 |

The number known to local authorities has therefore almost tripled since 2018/19. In 2024/25, 15.3 of every 1,000 pupils were known to be electively home educated, and age 15 was the most common age for home education.
