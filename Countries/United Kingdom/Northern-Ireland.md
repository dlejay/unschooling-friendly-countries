# Unschooling in Northern Ireland

## Compulsory education

In Northern Ireland, parents must ensure that every child of compulsory school
age receives an efficient, full-time education.

Compulsory school age normally begins at the start of the school year following
the child's fourth birthday. However:

* children born between 2 July and 31 August normally begin compulsory
  education in the September following their fifth birthday
* children born between 1 April and 1 July may have their start deferred by one
  year at their parent's request
* the same deferral is available to certain children who were due to be born
  after 1 April but were born prematurely before that date

Compulsory school age ends on 30 June:

* if the child turns 16 between 1 September and 1 July, it ends on 30 June of
  that school year
* if the child turns 16 between 2 July and 31 August, it ends on 30 June of the
  following year

As of 25 July 2026, proposals to require young people to remain in education or
training until age 18 have not become law. The proposed system would still
permit home education as a form of participation.

## Unschooling

Elective Home Education (EHE) is legal in Northern Ireland.

Article 45(1) of the Education and Libraries (Northern Ireland) Order 1986
requires the parent of every child of compulsory school age to ensure that the
child receives:

> efficient full-time education suitable to his age, ability and aptitude and
> to any special educational needs he may have, either by regular attendance
> at school or otherwise

Education is therefore compulsory, but attendance at school is not.

Parents do not need:

* permission from the school or the Education Authority (EA)
* teaching qualifications
* to follow the Northern Ireland Curriculum
* to follow school hours, days or terms
* a fixed timetable
* to provide formal lessons
* to conduct formal tests or assessments
* to reproduce school-based, age-specific standards

The EA's official guidance recognises a wide range of valid educational
approaches, from following a highly structured curriculum to responding
dynamically to a child's interests. Interest-driven or unschooling approaches
are therefore permitted, provided the education remains efficient, full-time
and suitable to the individual child.

### Starting home education

If the child has never attended school, there is currently no legal requirement
for the parent to register the child with, or notify, the EA.

If the child is already registered at a school, the parent must send the
principal a signed and dated letter stating:

* that the child is being withdrawn for elective home education
* the intended starting date of home education

The school cannot legally prevent or delay deregistration once it has been
formally notified. The child should be removed from the school register on the
day after their final attendance.

Parents may also home educate a child who has special educational needs,
including a child with a statement or who attended a special school. EA
guidance says that the ordinary deregistration process still applies, although
parents should engage with the relevant EA statementing officer so that any
special educational provision can be considered.

### Education Authority oversight

The EA may make informal enquiries if it has reason to establish whether a
suitable education is being provided.

Parents may demonstrate the provision through methods such as:

* a written educational report
* examples of the child's work or activities
* records of projects or educational visits
* a meeting with an EA officer
* a home visit, if the parent agrees to one

If it appears that a child is not receiving a suitable education, the EA may
ultimately pursue a School Attendance Order.

## Statistics

These figures count children known to the Education Authority as being
electively home educated:

| Academic year                | Children known to the EA |
| ---------------------------- | -----------------------: |
| 2024/25, as at 15 April 2025 |                    1,099 |
| 2023/24                      |                      945 |
| 2022/23                      |                      907 |
| 2021/22                      |                      808 |
| 2020/21                      |                      710 |

The true number may be higher. Children who have never been enrolled in a
school do not have to be reported to the EA, and the EA may also be unaware of
a withdrawal if the school does not submit the relevant deregistration form.

The 1,099 figure remains the latest published total located as of 25 July 2026.
A February 2026 Assembly answer separately reported 62 primary-school
deregistrations for EHE during 2025/26 to date, but that is a flow of new
deregistrations rather than the total number of home-educated children and is
not directly comparable with the table above.
