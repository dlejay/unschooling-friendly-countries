# Unschooling in Kenya

## Compulsory education

Under the Basic Education Act 2013, basic and secondary education are compulsory, beginning at age 4.
Compulsory education lasts 12 years.

## Unschooling

Article 28 of the Basic Education Act says :

> The Cabinet Secretary shall implement the right of every child to free and compulsory basic education.

which is interpreted by Kenyan authorities as meaning that schooling is compulsory.

# Unschooling in Kenya

## Compulsory education

The Constitution guarantees every child the right to free and compulsory basic education.

Under the *Early Childhood Education Act 2021*, parents must cause their child to be admitted to an early childhood education centre; children aged four to six are eligible for admission. The *Basic Education Act 2013* subsequently requires every parent of a child who is Kenyan or resident in Kenya to ensure regular attendance at a school or another institution authorised by the Cabinet Secretary.

Kenya's current 2:6:3:3 structure comprises two years of pre-primary education, six years of primary education, three years of junior school and three years of senior school.

## Home education

Kenyan law does not establish home education as a generally authorised alternative to attendance at a recognised educational institution.

In *Were & another v Cabinet Secretary, Ministry of Education & 2 others* (2023), the High Court rejected a constitutional challenge to the statutory attendance requirements. It held that homeschooling could operate only within educational standards established by the State and stated that homeschooling, like other systems of education, must be regulated by the Government. The petition seeking recognition of homeschooling under the *Basic Education Act* was dismissed.

Accordingly, there is presently no general statutory framework permitting autonomous home education or unschooling in place of compulsory institutional attendance.
