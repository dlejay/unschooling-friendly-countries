# Unschooling in Kyrgyzstan

## Compulsory education

Primary, basic and secondary general education are compulsory for citizens of
Kyrgyzstan.

Kyrgyzstan is phasing in a 12-year general-education system. Under the standard
6+3+3 structure, children enter grade 1 at age 6:

* primary general education covers grades 1–6, normally ages 6–11;
* basic general education covers grades 7–9, normally ages 12–14;
* secondary general education covers grades 10–12, normally ages 15–17.

The transition to the 12-year system is being implemented gradually, so some
cohorts temporarily follow accelerated or integrated grade arrangements.

## Family education

The 2023 Law on Education recognizes **family education** as a form of education
available to all students.

Family education means completing an educational program outside an educational
organization while remaining subject to intermediate and final certification
at an educational organization, in accordance with the state educational
standard.

The detailed procedure is governed by the Regulations on obtaining general
education in the form of family education, approved by Cabinet Resolution
No. 297 of 30 May 2025.

Parents or legal representatives must:

* submit an application for permission through the competent territorial
  education authority;
* have the child attached to a state or municipal general-education
  organization;
* ensure that the child studies the relevant state-standard educational
  program;
* choose a language of instruction offered by the attached school;
* apply for and present the child for intermediate, transfer and final
  certification at the times determined by the school;
* ensure that any academic debt resulting from an unsatisfactory assessment is
  remedied;
* maintain a systematic, continuous and secular educational process;
* cooperate with the attached school and follow teachers’ educational
  recommendations;
* provide suitable conditions for the child’s development and physical and
  mental health.

The child’s opinion must be taken into account when family education is chosen.

A child receiving family education may return to education in a school at any
stage, subject to successfully completing the required certification. Parents
are also entitled to methodological, psychological-pedagogical and consultative
support from the education system.

Because an application, official permission, registration and attachment to a
school are required, parents cannot legally begin family education immediately
through a unilateral declaration.

Family education is also not equivalent to unrestricted unschooling. Learning
may be organized outside school, but the child must still complete the
state-standard program and pass the prescribed assessments. A fully
curriculum-free or exclusively interest-driven approach is therefore not
legally recognized.

## Statistics

There is no reliable basis for stating that 6% of all school-age children in
Kyrgyzstan are out of school.

UNESCO’s 2025 country profile instead reports level-specific out-of-school rates
of approximately:

* 1.0% at primary-school level;
* 2.1% at lower-secondary level;
* 13.9% at upper-secondary level.

These percentages should not be combined into a single unweighted national
rate.

A separate 2025 report, citing a joint assessment involving the Ministry of
Justice and the National Institute for Strategic Initiatives, stated that
approximately 7,700 children with disabilities remained entirely outside the
education system.
