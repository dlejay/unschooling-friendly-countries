# Unschooling in the Falkland Islands

## Compulsory education

Section 7 of the Education (Governance and Accountability) Ordinance 2022
requires a parent to ensure that a child or young person of compulsory
education age receives an efficient education. The duty begins at the start of
the academic year in which the child turns 5 and ends at the close of the
academic year in which the young person turns 16. Failure to comply is an
offence punishable by a fine.

The Ordinance defines an efficient education as one suitable to the child's or
young person's age, ability and aptitudes. Section 7 expressly permits the
parent to discharge the duty through a government school, an approved school,
education outside the Falkland Islands after notification, or education at
home or by other means under section 42.

## Prior approval

Section 42 provides that home education is not treated as efficient education
unless it has been approved by the Director of Education. Approval may be for
a specified or unlimited period and may be made subject to conditions. The
Director may inspect the provision, require information from the parent and
withdraw approval on at least seven days' written notice if no longer
satisfied that the education is efficient.

The Falkland Islands' Elective Home Education Policy sets out the application
procedure. A parent must notify the Director in writing and provide, for each
child, the reasons for the application, a current assessment of educational
development, a curriculum plan for the following 12 months, details of who
will teach and where, and arrangements for the child's physical, social and
emotional needs, including contact with children of a similar age.

The Director then meets the parent, normally at the proposed place of
education, and may request further information. Once the meeting has taken
place and all requested information has been received, the policy provides for
a written decision within five working days, unless the Director explains why
more time is required. The statutory recognition of home education therefore
depends on a decision rather than notification alone.

## Educational content and supervision

Neither section 42 nor the policy prescribes the national curriculum, fixed
subjects, minimum teaching hours or standardised examinations. The proposed
education must nevertheless be set out in a 12-month curriculum plan and be
suitable to the individual child's age, ability and aptitudes. This permits a
plan to be organised around the child's interests, subject to the Director's
assessment and any conditions attached to approval.

During home education, a senior member of school staff visits the child where
the education is taking place three times a year to assess the provision and
progress, and prepares a report for the parent and the Director. The Director
may request a further curriculum plan at key educational stages, require
improvements, arrange additional visits and withdraw approval if suitable
improvements are not made.

## Foreign families

Neither the Ordinance nor the policy limits applications by nationality. For
the purposes of the Ordinance, a parent includes a person who has parental
responsibility or has care of the child or young person. The Ordinance
separately provides that the Government is not required to make educational
provision for a person who is not ordinarily resident in the Falkland Islands;
the text does not extend that limitation to applications under section 42.

## Administrative practice

The Education Directorate reported no electively home-educated children in
July 2024. Its report to the Education Board in April 2025 recorded two
children in home education, indicating that the approval framework was being
used in practice.
