# Unschooling in Zambia

## Compulsory schooling

Section 17(1) of the Education Act 2011 requires parents to enrol children of school-going age at an educational institution and ensure their attendance. Section 16 leaves the determination of school-going age to the Minister by statutory instrument. [Education Act, §§16–17, printed p.434](https://www.edu.gov.zm/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Education-Act-2011.pdf#page=16).

The Ministry's *Primary Education Provision Guide 2026* specifies a primary entry age of six and a six-year primary programme, Grades 1–6. This entry-age guidance should be distinguished from the statutory power to prescribe the compulsory-attendance age range. [Primary Education Provision Guide, printed p.20](https://www.edu.gov.zm/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Primary-Education-Provision-Guide-final-signed.pdf).

## Home education and attendance exemptions

Section 17(2) permits the Minister, after consultation with the regional director of education and by Gazette notice, to suspend compulsory attendance for specified geographical areas or age groups on health or public-interest grounds. It also permits total or partial individual exemptions :

> if it is in the best interest of the person or in the public interest.

These are ministerial exemptions subject to a statutory interest test, rather than a general parental home-education option. Under section 17(4), unexcused non-enrolment or withdrawal, and refusal to comply with an attendance notice, constitute offences punishable by a fine, up to one year's imprisonment, or both. [Education Act, §17(2)–(4), printed pp.434–435](https://www.edu.gov.zm/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Education-Act-2011.pdf#page=16).

Section 12(2), as amended in 2026, permits educational institutions to establish open and distance learning. Such institutional provision is distinct from autonomous family instruction. [Education (Amendment) Act 2026, §3, printed p.597](https://pbo.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%2017%20of%202026.pdf#page=3).

### Home education in practice

Emmanuel Home School Academy, based in Chongwe, describes itself as a support organisation for families in Zambia who educate their children at home. It offers parent development, academic management and graduation assistance. [EHSA — Academy and services](https://www.ehsa-zm.com/the-academy/).

## Recent developments

The Education (Amendment) Act No. 17 of 2026 received assent on 4 June and commenced on 12 June 2026. It defines primary education as Grades 1–6 and secondary education as Forms 1–6, and provides for the transformation of basic and high schools into primary and secondary schools between 30 May 2026 and 30 May 2031. Section 17's attendance and exemption provisions remain unchanged. [Amendment Act, §§2 and 15, printed pp.595–597 and 602](https://pbo.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/acts/Act%20No.%2017%20of%202026.pdf).

## Statistics

The Ministry's *Education Statistics Bulletin 2025*, Table 2.1, reports the following national school-census enrolment totals :

| Census year | Enrolled learners |
| ----------- | ----------------: |
| 2025 | 6,833,236 |
| 2024 | 6,527,980 |

The census covers government, private and community schools, with these totals including early childhood, primary and secondary education. They are institutional enrolment figures, not counts of independently home-educated children. [Education Statistics Bulletin 2025, Foreword and Table 2.1, printed pp.iv and 7](https://www.edu.gov.zm/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025-ESB-.pdf#page=26).
