# Unschooling in Singapore

## Compulsory primary education

[Section 2 of the Compulsory Education Act 2000](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CEA2000?ValidDate=20250309)
defines a child of compulsory school age as:

> a child above the age of 6 years who has not yet attained the age of
> 15 years and who satisfies such conditions for receiving primary education
> as the Director-General may determine;

Primary education is defined as a six-year course normally commencing when
a child has attained the age of six. Section 3(1) provides:

> A child of compulsory school age who is —
>
> (a) born after 1 January 1996;
>
> (b) a citizen of Singapore; and
>
> (c) residing in Singapore,
>
> must attend regularly as a pupil at a national primary school.

Section 3(3)(b) excludes children exempted under section 4 from this duty.

## Home-education exemption

[Paragraph 3(1)(b) of the Compulsory Education (Exemption) Order](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/SL/CEA2000-OR1?DocDate=20180404)
provides a conditional exemption for home-schooled children.
Paragraph 3(2)(a)(ii) requires that:

> the Director-General must —
>
> (A) be satisfied that the home-schooling arrangements for the child are
> appropriate and adequate; and
>
> (B) approve the home-schooling curriculum mentioned in sub-paragraph (i)
> submitted by the parent;

Under paragraph 3(2)(a), parents must submit the arrangements and curriculum,
possess the qualifications required by the Director-General, and permit
home visits. Paragraph 3(4) authorises visits at reasonable times.

## Curriculum

The Ministry of Education's [exemption guidance](https://www.moe.gov.sg/primary/compulsory-education/exemptions)
requires parents of children without special educational needs, or with mild
special educational needs, to:

> Provide an academic learning plan for the 4 subjects (English Language,
> Mother Tongue Language, Mathematics and Science).

The guidance also requires a teaching plan for Character and Citizenship
Education and the necessary parental qualifications.

For children with moderate to severe special educational needs, parents must
provide an Individualised Education Plan specifying the curriculum and
intended learning outcomes. They must demonstrate the qualifications,
skills and competencies needed to deliver its educational component.

## Assessments

Paragraph 3(2)(b) requires an undertaking that the child will undergo an
assessment before age eleven, and sit the Primary School Leaving Examination
(PSLE) and National Education Quiz after age eleven and before fifteen.
Paragraph 3(2)(b)(iii) provides:

> the child must, before the child attains the age of 15 years, perform,
> for the Primary School Leaving Examination and the National Education
> Quiz, at such level of educational achievement as the Director-General
> may determine.

These assessment conditions exclude children with special educational needs
as defined in paragraph 3(3).

For children without special educational needs, or with mild special
educational needs, the Ministry's [guidance](https://www.moe.gov.sg/primary/compulsory-education/exemptions)
requires all four PSLE subjects to be taken at standard level. The benchmark
is the 33rd percentile of pupils in national primary schools taking four
standard-level subjects in the same year. The National Education Quiz must
be taken before the PSLE.

A [ministerial parliamentary reply of 5 October 2021](https://www.seab.gov.sg/newsroom/5-oct-2021-support-for-parents-of-p4-for-psle/)
describes the Mid-Term Assessment taken in Primary 4. Subject information
booklets are provided in Primary 3, and parents may request the examiners'
reports on cohort performance. The Ministry follows up with parents of
children whose assessment results indicate difficulties.

## Revocation and enforcement

Section 4(2) of the Act provides:

> An exemption granted under this section may be revoked at any time.

Under sections 3(2) and 7(1), failure to secure the required attendance is a
parental offence punishable by a fine of up to S$5,000, imprisonment for up
to twelve months, or both.
