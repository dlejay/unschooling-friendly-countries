# Home education in the Sovereign Base Areas of Akrotiri and Dhekelia

## Applicable law

The [Education Provisions Ordinance 2005](https://www.sbaadministration.org/home/legislation/01_02_09_05_ORDINANCES/01_02_09_05_46_ORD_2005/20050101_Ord-09_G1371_u.pdf), sections 2–3 and Schedule, applies specified Cypriot education laws in the Sovereign Base Areas, including Law 24(I)/1993 on compulsory attendance and Law 113(I)/1999 on special education. Section 2(1) includes amendments to those laws whenever enacted. Section 5 assigns the corresponding official powers and duties to the SBA Chief Officer and permits their exercise by authorised Cypriot officers, subject to the Ordinance's conditions. The YAML classification concerns this territorial regime.

## Compulsory school attendance

[Law 24(I)/1993, section 3(1)](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/1993_1_24/full.html), requires attendance in compulsory pre-primary education, primary school and lower-secondary school:

> Attendance […] is compulsory until the pupil completes lower-secondary education or reaches fifteen years of age, whichever occurs first.

Unofficial English translation from Greek, section 3(1).

For the 2026–2027 school year, section 8(c) sets compulsory pre-primary admission at four years and four months, attained before 1 September 2026. Section 2 provides for education in public or registered private schools. Section 4 makes failure by a custodian to enrol the child or supervise attendance an offence. [Law 24(I)/1993, sections 2–4 and 8](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/1993_1_24/full.html).

## Exemption and education at home

Section 3(2) of [Law 24(I)/1993](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/1993_1_24/full.html) permits an exemption from compulsory attendance when the competent authority is satisfied that there are:

> special and serious reasons

Unofficial English translation from Greek, section 3(2); the transfer of official powers is governed by section 5 of the SBA Ordinance.

[Law 113(I)/1999, section 3(2)](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/1999_1_113/full.html), provides for education at home or in hospital for children with special needs in primary or secondary education who cannot attend school for an extended period because of their health.

The special-education committee assesses needs and determines provision and placement under sections 9–12. Under section 14, parents may propose alternative arrangements at their own expense within thirty days of receiving its report; the committee approves or rejects the proposed provision. [Law 113(I)/1999, sections 9–14](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/1999_1_113/full.html).

## Programme and assessment

The statutory home or hospital provision retains its connection to the child's school programme:

> Lessons outside school are considered part of the regular programme of lessons in the regular classes in which the children are enrolled.

Unofficial English translation from Greek, [Law 113(I)/1999, section 3(2)](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/1999_1_113/full.html). Section 13 requires an individual education programme and progress monitoring, developed with teachers and parents, and reports at least every six months.

## British Service families

The Ministry of Defence's [JSP 342 Policy, version 6, chapter 6](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6735e638b613efc3f1823101/JSP_342_-_Education_of_Service_Children_Overseas_Policy.pdf#page=18), supports elective home education for Service families overseas. It requires consideration of local law and applicable international agreements and states:

> These international agreements do not create an exemption from host nation law.

JSP 342, section 6.1, p. 19.

Section 6.3 requires consultation with Defence Children Services before withdrawal, engagement with the overseas chain of command, a multi-agency assessment of educational supportability, and notice to the child's school. Section 6.2 provides for a register and periodic educational reviews. These are MOD administrative requirements for Service families, distinct from the territorial attendance exemption. [JSP 342, pp. 19–20](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6735e638b613efc3f1823101/JSP_342_-_Education_of_Service_Children_Overseas_Policy.pdf#page=19).
