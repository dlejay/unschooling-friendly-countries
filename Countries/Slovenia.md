# Unschooling in Slovenia

## Compulsory education

Article 3 of the [Basic School Act (*Zakon o osnovni šoli*, ZOsn)](https://pisrs.si/pregledPredpisa?id=ZAKO448)
provides:

> Compulsory basic education lasts nine years.

Article 44 requires enrolment in the first grade:

> Parents must enrol in the first grade of basic school children who will
> reach the age of six in the calendar year in which they begin attending
> basic school.

Under Article 45, entry may be deferred for one year if the child is found
not to be ready. Basic school encompasses primary and lower secondary
education, from grades 1 to 9.

## Home education and notification

Article 88(1)–(3) establishes the general home-education procedure:

> Parents have the right to organise their child's basic education at home
> (hereinafter: home education). By choosing home education, parents assume
> the obligations associated with providing their child's education.
>
> Parents shall notify in writing the basic school in which the child is
> enrolled of home education for the following school year no later than
> 16 August of the current school year. The right to home education cannot
> be exercised during the school year. Parents shall notify the basic
> school in writing of home education separately for each school year.
>
> The notification referred to in the preceding paragraph shall contain
> the child's first name and surname, the place where education will take
> place, and the first names and surnames of the persons who will teach
> the child.

The [Ministry's guidance](https://www.gov.si/teme/o-osnovni-soli/)
specifies that the child retains pupil status and remains registered with
the school. It also states that no qualification requirements are
prescribed for home educators under the general procedure.

Article 88(5) governs a return to school:

> Parents may discontinue home education during the school year. The
> pupil may enter education at a basic school until the beginning of the
> assessment periods for home-educated pupils laid down in the regulation
> governing the basic-school calendar. Parents must notify the basic
> school in writing of the discontinuation of home education.

## Curriculum and assessment

Article 89 requires the school, at the beginning of the school year, to
present parents with the educational objectives, knowledge standards,
assessment criteria and conditions for progression to the next grade.
The school must also communicate assessment arrangements and make
textbooks available through its textbook lending scheme.

Article 90 provides:

> A home-educated pupil's knowledge shall be assessed in all subjects
> according to the subject timetable for the relevant grade of the
> officially recognised basic-school programme in which the pupil is
> educated.
>
> A home-educated pupil shall attend the assessments prescribed in
> accordance with this Act and its implementing regulations. Assessment
> shall be conducted by the basic school in which the pupil is enrolled.
> The pupil's knowledge shall be assessed by an examination board.
>
> If a home-educated pupil does not attain the minimum knowledge
> standards or receives a failing grade in an individual subject, the
> pupil has the right to reassessment before the beginning of the next
> school year.
>
> If a home-educated pupil does not pass the reassessment, the pupil must
> continue basic education in the following school year at a public or
> private basic school referred to in the first or second indent of
> Article 5 of this Act. In that case, the parents may no longer exercise
> the right to home education for the pupil in subsequent grades.
>
> If a home-educated pupil in grade 9 does not pass the reassessment, the
> pupil may take a remedial examination or repeat grade 9.

Article 25 of the [Rules on the Assessment of Knowledge and Progression
of Pupils in Basic School](https://www.uradni-list.si/glasilo-uradni-list-rs/vsebina/2025-01-2806),
in force since 18 October 2025, requires assessment on school premises.
Paragraph 3 specifies:

> Before assessment, the basic school shall inform the parents of a
> home-educated pupil of the methods of assessing the pupil's knowledge.
> In Slovenian and Italian or Hungarian as a mother tongue, a foreign
> language, the language of the local environment, and mathematics,
> home-educated pupils' knowledge shall be assessed through a written
> test and oral answers. For other subjects, other assessment methods
> referred to in Article 7 of these Rules may also be specified, through
> which it is possible to establish and evaluate directly before the
> examination board the extent to which the pupil attains the knowledge
> standards laid down in the curriculum.

Under Article 25(6)–(8), one reassessment is available. A pupil who first
attends assessment in the second assessment period may also be reassessed
within that period. A justified absence or interruption permits a new
date within the same period, set by the headteacher. Under paragraph 9,
unjustified non-attendance at assessment or reassessment entails continuation
of education in a public or private basic school.

The Ministry's [2026/2027 school calendar](https://www.gov.si/teme/solski-koledar-za-osnovne-sole/)
sets the following assessment periods for home-educated pupils:

| Grades | First assessment period | Second assessment period |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–8 | 1 February–24 June 2027 | 18–31 August 2027 |
| 9 | 1 February–15 June 2027 | 18–31 August 2027 |

Article 91 applies the national assessment provisions of Article 64 to
home-educated pupils. Article 64 states:

> In grades 3, 6 and 9, pupils' knowledge is tested through national
> assessment, which tests the objectives and knowledge standards laid
> down in the curriculum. National assessment is compulsory for pupils
> in grades 3, 6 and 9.

Under Article 92, the school issues a certificate at the end of each grade.

## Pupils with special educational needs

Article 92a establishes a separate arrangement:

> Parents have the right to organise basic education at home for their
> child with special educational needs who is included in a basic-school
> programme with adapted delivery and additional professional assistance,
> adapted programmes, or the first four stages of the special education
> programme, if this is provided for in the placement decision. To assist
> with home education, parents shall provide a person who meets the
> requirements for delivering the officially recognised programme
> (hereinafter: home-education teacher) to which the pupil is assigned,
> and the equipment or teaching aids necessary to attain the objectives
> and knowledge standards laid down in the curricula.

Under Article 92b, the school and home-education teacher prepare an
individualised programme. The [Ministry's explanation of the 2025
amendment](https://www.gov.si/novice/2025-08-21-informacija-o-novostih-v-solskem-letu-20252026/)
states that pupils with special educational needs may enter home education
during the school year and regain that right after returning to school.

## Foreign families

Article 10 provides:

> Children who are foreign citizens or stateless persons and reside in
> the Republic of Slovenia have the right to compulsory basic education
> under the same conditions as citizens of the Republic of Slovenia.

## Enforcement and constitutional review

Article 102(2) provides for fines of EUR 500–1,000 for parents who, without
justification, fail to ensure that a home-educated pupil attends the
required assessments. The same range applies to failure to enrol a child
as required by Article 44(2).

In [Decision U-I-32/25 of 11 September 2025](https://www.uradni-list.si/glasilo-uradni-list-rs/vsebina/2025-01-2746/odlocba-o-ugotovitvi-da-5--88--89--in-90--clen-zakona-o-osnovni-soli-niso-v-neskladju-z-ustavo),
the Constitutional Court held:

> 1. Article 5 of the Basic School Act […] is not inconsistent with the
> Constitution.
>
> 2. Articles 88, 89 and 90 of the Basic School Act are not inconsistent
> with the Constitution.
