# Unschooling in Namibia

## Compulsory education

Under the Basic Education Act, 2020, school attendance is compulsory from the first school day of the academic year in which a child turns 6 until the earliest of:

- the last school day of the year in which the child turns 18;
- completion of secondary education;
- completion of a specialised education programme; or
- a determination by the Executive Director that the learner is ready to leave school.

## Home education

Home education is legal but requires registration with the Ministry.

Section 87 of the Basic Education Act, 2020 provides that a parent may apply to the Minister to register a learner for home schooling. Registration may be granted where:

> (a) a situation relating to health or disability exists that prevents the learner from attending or fully benefiting from the regular school attendance;
>
> (b) the registration is in the best interest of the learner; or
>
> (c) the education to be received by the learner at home —
>
> (i) meets the minimum requirements of the curriculum at State schools; and
>
> (ii) is of a standard not inferior to the standard of education provided at State schools.

Home education is therefore not limited to children with health conditions or disabilities.

Under the Basic Education Regulations, 2023, parents must apply annually for registration. The Minister has up to 60 days to approve or decline the application, and a quality assurance officer must conduct a pre-registration site visit.

Registration is conditional on the parent providing suitable educational resources, monitoring the learner's academic progress and arranging assessment at a standard not inferior to that of State schools.

The proposed educational programme must:

> (i) be suitable for the age, grade, level and ability of the learner; and
>
> (ii) cover the acquisition of content and skills comparable to the relevant national curriculum outcomes.

Consequently, genuinely curriculum-free unschooling is not compatible with the statutory requirements. An interest-led approach may be used as a teaching method, but the programme must still cover content and skills comparable to the national curriculum outcomes and the learner's attainment must be assessed.

The Act and Regulations do not create a separate home-education regime for foreign nationals. Foreign families residing in Namibia should therefore expect to follow the same registration requirements.
