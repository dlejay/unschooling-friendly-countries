# Unschooling in Indonesia

## Compulsory education

The quotations below are unofficial English translations of the Indonesian
texts.

[Article 6(1) of Law No. 20 of 2003 on the National Education System](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/32160/UU%20Nomor%2020%20Tahun%202003.pdf#page=7)
provides:

> Every citizen aged seven to fifteen years must undertake basic
> education.

Article 17(2) includes primary and lower-secondary education, or their
equivalents, within basic education. Article 7(2) provides:

> Parents of children of compulsory-education age are obliged to provide
> basic education for their children.

[Article 3 of Government Regulation No. 47 of 2008 on Compulsory Education](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/37221/PP%2047%20Tahun%202008.pdf#page=3)
specifies the permitted pathways:

> (1) Compulsory education is provided through formal, non-formal and
> informal education pathways.
>
> [...]
>
> (4) Compulsory education through the informal education pathway is
> provided through family education and/or education in the surrounding
> environment.

Article 7(4) permits regional governments to extend compulsory education
to the upper-secondary level.

## Informal education

[Article 27 of Law No. 20 of 2003](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/32160/UU%20Nomor%2020%20Tahun%202003.pdf#page=14)
provides:

> (1) Informal educational activities undertaken by families and the
> surrounding environment take the form of independent learning.
>
> (2) The educational outcomes referred to in paragraph (1) are recognised
> as equivalent to those of formal and non-formal education after the
> learner passes examinations in accordance with national education
> standards.

## Home-education registration

[Ministerial Regulation No. 129 of 2014](https://peraturan.go.id/files/bn1660-2014.pdf#page=3)
distinguishes single-family, multiple-family and community *sekolahrumah*
(home education).

[Article 6(1)](https://peraturan.go.id/files/bn1660-2014.pdf#page=5)
provides:

> Providers of single-family and multiple-family home education must
> register with the regency/city education office.

Article 6(2) requires identification, both parents' declaration of
responsibility, the learner's written agreement from age thirteen, and a
programme document containing a learning plan for single-family registration.
Article 6(4) requires community home education to obtain a permit establishing
a non-formal education institution as a learning group.

## Curriculum

[Article 7 of Ministerial Regulation No. 129 of 2014](https://peraturan.go.id/files/bn1660-2014.pdf#page=6)
provides:

> (1) The curriculum applied in home education refers to the national
> curriculum.
>
> (2) Home-education providers must teach religious education, Pancasila
> and citizenship education, and Indonesian language education.
>
> (3) The national curriculum referred to in paragraph (1) may be the
> formal-education curriculum or the equivalency-education curriculum,
> with greater breadth or depth depending on the learner's interests,
> potential and needs.

## Affiliation and assessment

[Director-General Regulation No. 51 of 2025](https://duniapenfi.kemendikdasmen.go.id/dokumen/25),
adopted on 31 October 2025, governs home education affiliated with
non-formal institutions. Article 7 requires an accredited *sanggar kegiatan
belajar* or *pusat kegiatan belajar masyarakat* with a national school
identification number. Article 37 provides:

> When this Director-General Regulation enters into force, home-education
> learners who are not yet affiliated and wish to obtain recognition of
> their learning outcomes must affiliate with a non-formal education
> institution no later than one year after this Regulation is adopted.

Under article 15, the education office verifies registration documents
and issues a certificate identifying the approved or designated affiliate.
The family and institution conclude an agreement covering support,
monitoring, assessment and reporting.

Articles 12–14 require six- or twelve-month learning and support plans,
periodic family evaluations and progress reports, and institutional
assessments each semester. Article 36 requires institutional supervision,
online or in person, at least once per semester. Article 35 permits written
warnings or withdrawal of the registration certificate for non-compliance.
