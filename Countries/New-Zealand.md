# Unschooling in New Zealand

## Compulsory enrolment

Section 35(1) of the [Education and Training Act 2020](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2020/38/en/latest/#LMS204312)
provides:

> Every domestic student must, during the period beginning on the student's
> sixth birthday and ending on the student's 16th birthday, be enrolled at
> a registered school.

Section 35(2) provides an exception before the seventh birthday where the
school is more than three kilometres' walking distance from the student's
residence. The statutory definition of a domestic student is set out below.

## Home-education exemption

[Section 38(1)](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2020/38/en/latest/#LMS176208)
provides:

> An employee of the Ministry designated by the Secretary for the purpose
> (a designated officer) may, on application by a parent of the student,
> grant the parent a certificate that exempts the student from the
> requirements of section 35 if the designated officer is satisfied that
> the student—
>
> (a) is to be taught at least as regularly and well as in a registered
> school; or
>
> (b) is to be taught at least as regularly and well as in a specialist
> school or a special service (if the student would otherwise be likely
> to need special education).

Under [section 40](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2020/38/en/latest/#LMS176211),
while the certificate remains in force:

> (a) the exempted student does not have to be enrolled at any school; and
>
> (b) no person has to have the exempted student enrolled at any school.

The Ministry's [application procedure](https://www.education.govt.nz/parents-and-caregivers/schools-year-0-13/home-education/apply-home-educate-your-child)
requires a separate application for each child, submitted to the regional
office by a parent or legal guardian. All legal guardians must agree.
Applications are free and normally take four to six weeks to process.
Children subject to compulsory enrolment must remain enrolled and attend
school while the application is considered, as specified in the
[application form](https://web-assets.education.govt.nz/s3fs-public/2024-03/Home-education-application-form.pdf).

The Ministry states:

> When you receive your Certificate of Exemption, you can start home
> educating your child from the date on your certificate.

An application may be made when a child is 5, with the exemption taking
effect on the sixth birthday. Sections 38(7) and 38(8) provide that the
certificate continues until revoked, the child turns 16, or the child
enrols at a registered school, whichever occurs first.

## Curriculum and organisation of learning

Section Two, requirement A, of the Ministry's [Detailed guidance for home
education applicants](https://web-assets.education.govt.nz/s3fs-public/2024-03/Home-education-application-guidance-2020.pdf)
lists unschooling among the educational philosophies on which an application
may be based. It states:

> We do not require home educators to specifically teach the New Zealand
> Curriculum [...].

The guidance requires a broad, age-appropriate programme with strong literacy
and numeracy components. Requirement C asks for educational goals for the
next twelve months and a method of monitoring progress, supported by regular
written or digital records.

Requirement E requests an example of a project or topic and provides:

> The Ministry of Education appreciates that some home education philosophies
> do not forward-plan projects or topics. If this is the case for you, then
> you may wish to describe a topic or project which you have already completed
> with your child.

Section Three requires applicants to explain the frequency and duration of
learning. It accepts different forms of description, including a typical
week, a timetable, or time spent on a particular topic.

## Review, revocation and appeals

[Section 466](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2020/38/en/latest/#LMS172334)
empowers the Chief Review Officer to review education provided to exempted
students and requires such reviews when directed by the Minister.
Section 468(1) permits review officers, at a reasonable time and after
reasonable notice, to inspect or inquire, require relevant documents and
statements, inspect the student's work, and meet the student.
[Section 468(2)](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2020/38/en/latest/#LMS172336)
provides:

> Nothing in this section confers on a review officer the power to enter
> any dwelling house without the consent of the owner or occupier.

Section 38(5) provides:

> The Secretary may revoke an exemption certificate, but only if the
> Secretary—
>
> (a) has made reasonable efforts to get all of the relevant information; and
>
> (b) has considered a report on the matter from the Chief Review Officer; and
>
> (c) is not satisfied under subsection (1).

Section 38(6) separately permits revocation and a direction under section 37
where the Secretary considers that the student would be better off receiving
special education. Under sections 38(2) and 38(3), refusal of an application
may be appealed to the Secretary, who must consider a report from the Chief
Review Officer before confirming the refusal or granting the certificate;
the Secretary's decision is final.

In May 2026, a proposal to introduce additional regulatory conditions for
maintaining home-education exemptions was removed from the System Reform
Bill. The Ministry's [account of the legislation](https://www.education.govt.nz/our-work/information-releases/issue-specific-information-releases/education-and-training-system-reform-amendment-bill)
records that work on those proposed regulations was paused.

## Foreign families

Section 10(1) of the Act defines a domestic student as:

> an individual who is—
>
> (a) a New Zealand citizen; or
>
> (b) the holder of a residence class visa granted under the Immigration
> Act 2009 who satisfies the criteria (if any) prescribed by regulations
> made under subsection (2); or
>
> (c) a person of a class or description of persons required by the Minister,
> by notice, to be treated as if they are not international students
> (see subsection (11))

Section 35(3) expressly excludes international students from that section's
compulsory-enrolment provisions:

> Subsections (1) and (2) do not apply to international students.

## Statistics

The Ministry's [Education Counts statistics](https://www.educationcounts.govt.nz/statistics/homeschooling)
record 11,010 homeschooled students in 6,518 families on 1 July 2025,
representing 1.3% of total school enrolments.
