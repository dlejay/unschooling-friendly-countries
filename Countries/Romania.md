# Unschooling in Romania

Quotations below are unofficial English translations from Romanian.

## Compulsory education

Article 13(1) of the [Pre-University Education Law, No. 198/2023](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocumentAfis/271896)
provides:

> Compulsory education comprises preschool education, primary education,
> lower secondary education and upper secondary education.

Article 248(3) establishes the implementation date for the youngest preschool
group:

> The youngest group in preschool education becomes compulsory beginning
> with the 2030–2031 school year.

The middle and oldest preschool groups concern children aged 4 and 5,
respectively, as specified in the Ministry's [2026–2027 admissions guidance](https://www.edu.ro/inscriere_invatamant_prescolar_anteprescolar_faq).
Under articles 15 and 31, primary education normally begins at age 6 and
comprises the preparatory class and grades I–IV. Lower secondary comprises
grades V–VIII, followed normally by four years of upper secondary education.
Article 13(4) treats pupils in the technological pathway who obtain a level 3
qualification and do not continue upper secondary studies as having completed
compulsory education.

## School attendance

Article 14(1) provides:

> Parents or the legal representative have a duty to ensure the minor
> learner's attendance at classes throughout the entire period of
> compulsory education. Failure to fulfil this duty constitutes an
> administrative offence and is punishable under article 148(1)(a).

Articles 17(1) and 17(2) specify the forms of attendance:

> (1) The forms of organisation of pre-university education are: daytime
> attendance-based education, evening attendance-based education and
> reduced-attendance education. Attendance refers to presence on school
> premises.
>
> (2) Compulsory education is attendance-based education. For persons who
> have exceeded the age corresponding to their grade by more than three
> years in primary education, more than four years in lower secondary
> education, or more than five years in upper secondary education,
> compulsory education may also be organised in reduced-attendance form,
> in accordance with the methodology approved by order of the Minister of
> Education, or through the "Second Chance" programme.

Article 18(2) provides for online or hybrid teaching:

> To ensure the exercise of the fundamental right to education, where a
> state of emergency, alert or siege is declared, or whenever the Ministry
> of Education so orders, at the request of the pre-university educational
> institution and with the approval of the county or Bucharest
> pre-university education directorate, teaching, learning and assessment
> activities may also take place online or in hybrid form for a fixed period.

## Schooling at home for medical reasons

Article 72(2) provides:

> For children in nursery and preschool education and pupils who, for
> medical reasons or because of a disability, are unable to travel,
> schooling at home shall be organised for a fixed period according to
> their needs, in accordance with the methodology for the organisation
> and operation of schooling at home, approved by order of the Minister
> of Education.

The procedure is set out in the [Framework Methodology approved by Order
No. 5,086/2016](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocumentAfis/183766).
Articles 6 and 7 require a parental application to the educational assessment
and guidance service of the county resource and educational assistance centre
(CJRAE), or its Bucharest equivalent (CMBRAE). The file includes a specialist
medical recommendation for schooling at home. Following assessment, the
centre issues a school and vocational guidance certificate identifying the
type of school that will organise the provision.

Article 7(3) of the Methodology provides:

> The educational institution organising the child's, pupil's or young
> person's schooling at home shall prepare and submit for approval to
> the county school inspectorate or the Bucharest School Inspectorate
> the individualised educational plan and its proposals concerning the
> teacher or teachers who will provide instruction to the pupil at home.

## Curriculum and supervision

Articles 9(1) and 9(2) of the Methodology provide:

> (1) Pupils schooled at home study all subjects in the framework curricula
> corresponding to their level of study.
>
> (2) Children schooled at home benefit from individualised educational
> plans drawn up on the basis of syllabuses adapted by specialist teachers
> from the educational institution organising schooling at home, and
> approved by the county school inspectorate or the Bucharest School
> Inspectorate.

Article 8(1) assigns monitoring responsibility:

> Monitoring the classes conducted during the period of schooling at home
> is entirely the responsibility of the residential educational institution.

The "residential educational institution" is the school in which the pupil
is enrolled, normally near the pupil's home, under article 7(2). Article 13
requires the designated teachers to conduct diagnostic, formative and
summative assessments. Article 14 provides for individualised remedial plans,
where necessary, following teachers' annual evaluation reports.

## Enforcement

Article 148(1)(a) of Law No. 198/2023 sets a fine of 1,000 to 5,000 lei for
failure to fulfil article 14(1) through a parent's or legal representative's
fault in preventing participation in the school programme, where the conduct
does not constitute a criminal offence.

Article 380(1) of the [Criminal Code, Law No. 286/2009](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/FormaPrintabila/00000G004EC4TYEEJMG1SSEXDYO0ZICZ)
provides:

> A parent or a person to whom a minor has been entrusted in accordance
> with the law who, without justification, withdraws the minor or prevents
> the minor by any means from attending compulsory general education shall
> be punished by imprisonment for three months to one year or a fine.

Article 380(2) provides:

> The act shall not be punished if, before the completion of the criminal
> investigation, the defendant ensures that the minor resumes attending
> classes.

## Foreign families

Article 9 of Law No. 198/2023 defines access to the education system:

> The Romanian State ensures equitable access, without any form of
> discrimination, to all levels and forms of pre-university education for
> Romanian citizens, citizens of Member States of the European Union,
> hereinafter referred to as the EU, citizens of Member States of the
> European Economic Area and of the Swiss Confederation, minors who seek
> or have obtained a form of protection in Romania, foreign minors and
> stateless minors whose stay in Romania is officially recognised under
> the law, and citizens of non-EU states legally present in Romania, as
> well as British citizens and members of their families who are
> beneficiaries of the Agreement on the withdrawal of the United Kingdom
> of Great Britain and Northern Ireland from the European Union and the
> European Atomic Energy Community, 2019/C 384 I/01.
