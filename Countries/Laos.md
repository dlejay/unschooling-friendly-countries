# Unschooling in Laos

## Compulsory education and school attendance

Article 28 of the [Education Law No. 69/NA of 11 December 2024](https://laoofficialgazette.gov.la/kcfinder/upload/files/Education%20Law%20Eng%20version%2020250715.pdf#page=6) requires citizens to pursue and complete education through lower secondary level. The [General Education Law No. 83/NA of 25 June 2025](https://deqa.edu.la/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Law_General_edpressed.pdf) likewise defines compulsory education by completion of lower secondary education (Article 18, printed p. 6). Primary admission begins at six (Article 36, printed p. 10).

The latter law separately imposes a school-attendance obligation on parents and guardians. Article 77(1), printed p. 22, provides (unofficial translation from Lao):

> Send their school-age children to school and ensure that they complete their studies without dropping out.

Article 84(2), printed p. 24, prohibits obstructing school attendance and causing children to leave education.

## Education outside school

The General Education Law recognises formal and non-formal provision, including literacy and educational upgrading for disadvantaged groups (Articles 8 and 14–16, printed pp. 4–5). Article 37, printed p. 10, permits teaching within or outside educational institutions, with detailed arrangements governed by separate regulations.

The broader Education Law also recognises informal learning. Article 25, pp. 5–6, describes self-directed learning and research in different settings:

> with no restrictions on times [durations], contents, and locations.

This quotation follows the Ministry of Education and Sports' unofficial English translation. Article 84, p. 19, separately requires families to encourage, support and facilitate school-age children's attendance and completion of their studies.

## Curriculum and assessment

Under the General Education Law, curricula for formal and non-formal general education must meet equivalent standards and receive ministry recognition (Article 32, printed p. 9). The ministry approves their use (Article 34). Assessment measures learning against the general-education curriculum (Article 41, printed p. 11).

## Official statistics

For **2025–2026**, the [Department of General Education](https://dge.moes.edu.la/news.php) reports **1,866 secondary schools**, including **165 private schools**. These are school counts.

The Lao Statistics Bureau's [NIPN data repository](https://nipn.lsb.gov.la/repository/indicatorDetails.php?tag=QoO1KYscdEma0397a8eLlA%3D%3D), using ministry statistics and updated in April 2025, records a national lower-secondary gross enrolment ratio of **73.6% in 2024**. This measures enrolment in that level, rather than home education.
