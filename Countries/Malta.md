# Unschooling in Malta

## Compulsory education

Article 2 of the Education Act, Chapter 605, defines compulsory school age
as ages 5 to 15 inclusive. The definition also includes a child who has not
yet completed the final year of secondary school.

Articles 6 and 59 require parents to secure school registration and regular
attendance, subject to a good and sufficient cause for absence. Article 59
extends these duties to the end of the scholastic year in which the child
ceases to be of compulsory school age.

## Home education and licensing

Article 46 permits a parent to apply for home education. The Division of
Education must consider the child's and family's circumstances and the
child's best interests. Applications based on racist or similar grounds,
or reasons the Division considers improper, are excluded.

Article 21 requires a valid licence before home education is provided.
The Directorate for Quality and Standards in Education (DQSE) administers
applications. Its Board for Home Education evaluates the proposed programme
and supporting documents and recommends whether a licence should be issued.
The DQSE handbook requires school attendance until the licence is issued.

## Home educators

Article 2 requires an authorised home educator to hold a teacher's warrant
issued under the Teaching and Allied Professions Act, Chapter 606. The
educator may be the parent or another person. The DQSE handbook specifies a
valid permanent warrant. Parents retain legal responsibility and bear the
costs of home education.

## Educational programme

Article 47(1) requires a social experience equivalent to that provided in
schools, the same experience and facilities as in schools, and an accredited
programme approved by the Division. The Division may impose additional
conditions in the child's best interests. Compliance with all conditions
satisfies the parental duties under article 6, by virtue of article 47(2).

Sections 5.1–5.3 of the handbook require suitable premises and resources,
an approved curriculum, individualised learning outcomes, assessment
opportunities, and records of attendance and academic, personal and social
development. The application includes a detailed first-term scheme of work
identifying timing, locations and educators.

The programme must allocate 30 to 45 hours of learning weekly. The Board
assesses that allocation according to the child's age and the programme's
delivery arrangements.

## Monitoring and renewal

DQSE officers conduct regular external review visits, announced or
unannounced, to monitor the quality of provision. Licence holders must
implement the recommendations and required actions in the review report.
The handbook provides for renewal annually or according to programme
duration at the Board's discretion, and for suspension or withdrawal
following non-compliance detrimental to the child.

## Foreign families

Article 46 imposes no citizenship condition on applicants. The handbook
requires residence in Malta and delivery of the programme within the
Maltese Islands.
