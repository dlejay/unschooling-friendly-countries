# Unschooling in El Salvador

## Constitutional and statutory framework

Article 56 of the Constitution gives every inhabitant both the right and the
duty to receive preschool and basic education. Article 55 recognises the
parents' preferential right to choose their children's education, while
Article 54 places the organisation of the education system with the State and
protects the freedom to establish private teaching centres.

The General Education Law gives this framework more specific content. Under
Article 5, preschool and basic education are compulsory. Preschool normally
comprises three years for children aged 4 to 6, while basic education normally
comprises nine years beginning at age 7 (Articles 18 and 20).

The Law distinguishes formal, non-formal and informal education. Formal
education is delivered in authorised educational establishments through a
regular sequence governed by progressive curricular rules and leading to
grades and qualifications (Article 9). Non-formal education is systematic,
intended to complete, update or supply knowledge in response to short-term
needs; informal education is acquired freely and spontaneously from people,
institutions, media, traditions and other unstructured sources (Article 10).

## Enrolment and attendance

The *Ley Crecer Juntos para la Protección Integral de la Primera Infancia,
Niñez y Adolescencia*, in force since 1 January 2023, imposes express duties
on parents, representatives and other responsible adults. Article 55 requires
them to enrol each child or adolescent promptly in an educational centre and
to encourage, require and verify regular attendance at classes throughout the
educational process.

Article 56 requires educational institutions to report repeated unjustified
absences and school dropout to the education authority and to follow up on
both. The legislation examined does not establish an exemption from these
enrolment and attendance duties for education directed independently by a
parent at home.

## Informal learning and accreditation

Articles 11 and 63 of the General Education Law recognise learning acquired
outside formal education. Article 11 permits a person coming from non-formal
or informal education to enter the formal levels after the prescribed
evaluation. Article 63 permits a self-taught person to request proficiency
tests from the Ministry of Education in order to obtain accreditation for
entry into the different levels of the education system.

These provisions concern evaluation and entry into the formal system. They do
not state that a child of compulsory-education age may remain outside an
educational centre, and they do not displace the later, express enrolment and
attendance duties in Article 55 of the *Ley Crecer Juntos*. Similarly, the
parental right to choose an education does not itself identify home education
as an alternative to attendance.

## Foreign families

Article 6 of the *Ley Crecer Juntos* applies the Law to every child and
adolescent, whether Salvadoran or foreign, who is present in the national
territory. No separate exemption from the enrolment and attendance duties was
identified for foreign families.
