# Unschooling in Mauritius

## Compulsory education

The Ministry of Education's [Education Card 2024](https://education.govmu.org/Documents/2025/EducCard2024.pdf)
identifies age 5 as the beginning of compulsory education.

Sections 37(1) and 37(2) of the [Education Act](https://lawsofmauritius.govmu.org/portal/viewlegislationdocument/web/?docnumber=&doctitle=RWR1Y2F0aW9uIEFjdA%3D%3D&doctype=act)
provide:

> (1) Every child who has attained such age as may be prescribed for
> admission to a primary school shall attend a primary school.
>
> (2) It shall be compulsory for every child to attend school up to the end
> of the academic year in the course of which he attains the age of 16.

## Schools covered by the attendance requirement

Section 37(4) defines "school" for the purposes of this obligation:

> “school” means –
>
> (a) a primary or secondary school registered under this Act;
>
> (aa) “a special education needs institution registered under the
> Special Education Needs Authority Act 2018”;
>
> (b) such institution as may be authorised by the Minister to run
> the pre-vocational course; or
>
> (c) such institution as the Minister may authorise to run a
> technical or vocational course.

## Parental responsibility and enforcement

Section 2 defines the responsible party:

> “responsible party”, in relation to a child or young person, means the
> person who has for the time being the legal charge of or the control over
> that child or young person;

Section 37(3) provides:

> Any responsible party of a child under the age of 16 who, without
> reasonable cause, refuses or neglects to cause the child to attend school
> regularly in accordance with subsection (1) or (2) shall commit an offence
> and shall, on conviction, be liable to a fine not exceeding 10,000 rupees
> and to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 2 years.

## Distance education during temporary school closures

Section 36 permits the Minister to order temporary school closures in the
event of an epidemic, necessary repairs or other exceptional circumstances.
Where such an order is made, section 36A(1) provides that:

> the Minister may, during the temporary closure or for any further period
> as may be necessary, order any educational institution to provide distance
> education and online learning programmes, including broadcast lessons.

Section 36A(2) assigns the production and delivery of these programmes to
the institutions' teaching staff and other personnel, and their administration,
monitoring and supervision to designated Ministry and statutory-body employees.

## Home-education reform proposal

In its December 2024 [discussion paper on a legal framework for home-schooling](https://lrc.govmu.org/lrc/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Discussion-Paper-on-Home-Schooling-LRC_RP-183-December-2024s.pdf),
the Law Reform Commission proposed a new section 37C for children with
diagnosed special needs or disabilities significantly affecting their ability
to benefit from a traditional school environment. Paragraph 133 of the paper
proposes ministerial approval, an individualised education plan, evidence of
instructors' qualifications, and periodic reviews. These provisions form part
of the Commission's proposed amendment, not the Education Act quoted above.
