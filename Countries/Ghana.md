# Unschooling in Ghana

## Compulsory education and school attendance

Article 25(1)(a) of the [Constitution of the Republic of Ghana](https://osp.gov.gh/media/Resources/Ghana_Constitution.pdf#page=38)
provides:

> basic education shall be free, compulsory and available to all;

Section 2(1) of the [Pre-Tertiary Education Act, 2020 (Act 1049)](https://ghalii.org/akn/gh/act/2020/1049/eng%402020-10-27)
provides:

> A child who has attained school going age shall attend a course of
> instruction as laid down by the Minister in a school at the basic level
> recognised for that purpose by the Ministry.

Section 1(a) specifies that basic education comprises two years of
kindergarten, six years of primary school and three years of junior high
school. The National Council for Curriculum and Assessment's [National
Pre-Tertiary Education Curriculum Framework, section 2.2.1](https://nacca.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/National-Pre-tertiary-Education-Curriculum-Framework-1.pdf)
identifies age 4 as the beginning of kindergarten and ages 12–15 as the
junior-high phase.

## Children with special educational needs

Section 5(1) of Act 1049 requires the Ministry and District Assemblies to
provide a basic-education environment suitable for persons with disabilities
and children with special educational needs. Section 5(3) provides:

> A parent or guardian shall take advantage of inclusive education
> facilities to send a child with special needs to the appropriate
> education facility or make a request for the provision of an appropriate
> education facility which may be provided, subject to the availability
> of resources.

## Establishment of private schools

Article 25(2) of the Constitution provides:

> Every person shall have the right, at his own expense, to establish and
> maintain a private school or schools at all levels and of such categories
> and in accordance with such conditions as may be provided by law.

[Section 38(1) of Act 1049](https://gheducate.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/PRE-TERTIARY-EDUCATION-ACT-1049.pdf#page=20)
provides:

> Subject to article 25 of the Constitution, a person may establish,
> manage, operate and maintain a private basic or senior high school
>
> (a) in accordance with this Act and Regulations made under this Act; and
>
> (b) subject to the approval of the National Schools Inspectorate
> Authority.

Under section 38(2), an application concerning a private basic school is
submitted to the District Education Directorate for the area in which the
school is located. For a private senior high school, the application is
submitted to the Ministry and the Education Service through the Regional
Education Directorate.

Section 38(3) provides, in part:

> A private basic or senior high school shall
>
> (a) be incorporated as a corporate body with a governing body;
>
> (b) be staffed with teachers who are professionally qualified and
> licensed;
>
> (c) provide the requisite education and training for a child or any
> other person who qualifies to attend the school;
>
> (d) meet the standards set by the regulatory bodies of the Ministry in
> respect of the physical, academic, and any other facilities and
> requirements of the private basic or senior high school;

Section 38(3)(e) places private basic schools under the supervision of the
District Directorate of Education and the regulatory bodies, and private
senior high schools under the Regional Directorate and the regulatory
bodies. Section 38(3)(f) requires an annual report on the school's programmes
and activities to be submitted to the Education Service through the
corresponding directorate.

## Inspection of educational institutions

Section 92(1) of the [Education Regulatory Bodies Act, 2020 (Act 1023)](https://ctvet.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Education-Regulatory-Bodies-Act-2020.pdf#page=57)
provides:

> The Authority shall establish inspection panels to provide an
> independent external evaluation of the quality and standards in
> pre-tertiary institutions.

Under section 92(2), the panels examine leadership and management,
teaching and learning, academic achievement, facilities, internal and
external examinations, the values taught, and institutional data.
