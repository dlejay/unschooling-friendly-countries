# Unschooling in St Helena

## Compulsory education

Compulsory school age is governed by section 34 of the Education Ordinance 2008.

A child is ordinarily of compulsory school age once the child has attained the age
of five and until the child has attained the age of 16. However, the Ordinance applies
this by reference to the school year:

- a child who turns five during a school year is treated as having reached compulsory
  school age at the beginning of that school year;
- a child who turns 16 during a school year remains of compulsory school age until
  the day following the last examination that the child is required to take during
  that school year.

A parent of a child who has attained the age of 16 may also apply to the Education
Committee for the child to cease being of compulsory school age where further
compulsory school attendance would not be beneficial to the child.

## Home education

The Education Ordinance expressly excludes “home schooling activities” from its
definition of a private school. Home education therefore does not require registration
as a private school.

Section 36 requires the parent of every child of compulsory school age to secure
suitable education either:

> by regular attendance at a public school or otherwise.

The Ordinance does not establish a registration process or require parents to obtain
permission before beginning home education.

## Suitable education

Under section 35, education is “suitable” where, having regard to the curriculum and
educational facilities available in St Helena, it is as nearly as may be appropriate to
the child’s:

- age;
- ability;
- special educational needs, if any;
- aptitude; and
- health.

The Ordinance does not expressly require a home-educated child to follow the
curriculum taught in public schools. An interest-driven education should therefore
be possible, provided that the overall education can be shown to be suitable to the
individual child.

However, the legal framework does not clearly protect a completely unstructured
approach. If a parent is required to demonstrate that suitable education is being
provided otherwise than at school, section 37(3) requires the claim to be accompanied
by appropriate details setting out the “schemes of work” being undertaken by the
child. Interest-driven learning should consequently be documented in a way that
demonstrates coherent and continuing educational provision.

## Oversight and enforcement

If a question arises as to whether a child is receiving suitable education, the matter
is determined by the Director of Education and Employment.

If an attendance officer considers that the parent is not fulfilling the duty to provide
suitable education, the officer may, with the Director’s approval, issue a school
attendance order requiring the child to be enrolled at a named public school.

A parent may appeal a determination that the education is unsuitable to the Appeals
Committee. A further appeal may be made to the Supreme Court within 30 days of
the Appeals Committee’s decision.

## Administrative policy

The Education Committee approved a Home Education Policy in March 2018, but a
current copy of that policy could not be located on the St Helena Government website.

The St Helena Safeguarding Board’s 2023–24 annual report subsequently stated that
a Home Education Policy was to be developed because the Education Ordinance did
not provide sufficiently detailed legal guidance on home education. No newer
published policy was located as of this review.

Parents may therefore wish to confirm the current administrative procedure directly
with the Education, Skills and Employment Portfolio, even though the Ordinance
itself does not make prior approval a condition of home education.
