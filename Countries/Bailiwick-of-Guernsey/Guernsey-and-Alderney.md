# Unschooling in Guernsey and Alderney

## Compulsory education

Under the Education (Guernsey) Law, 1970, parents must ensure that every
child of compulsory school age receives an efficient, full-time education
suitable to the child’s age, ability and aptitude and to any special
educational needs, either through regular attendance at school or otherwise.

Compulsory education begins at the start of the school term following the
child’s fifth birthday and normally ends on the last Friday in June of the
school year in which the child reaches 16.

The Education (Guernsey) Law, 1970 applies in Alderney through the Alderney
(Application of Legislation) (Education) Ordinance, 1970. The same
home-education procedure is administered by the States of Guernsey Education
Services for families living in Guernsey and Alderney.

## Starting home education

Parents must apply to Education Services and obtain approval before beginning
home education.

Where a child is already registered at a school, the child must remain on the
school roll and continue attending full time until Education Services has
approved the proposed home-education provision. Consequently, parents cannot
simply deregister the child and begin immediately.

The Education Development Officer will normally discuss the proposal with the
parents and the child. Parents must provide enough information to demonstrate:

- a serious intention to provide education;
- an approach consistent with their educational values and aims;
- an understanding of how the child learns effectively; and
- a provision likely to constitute an efficient, suitable and full-time
  education.

Approval may be refused where Education Services is not satisfied that the
proposed provision will meet these requirements. A refusal can be appealed
under the procedure set out in the policy.

## Interest-driven education

Home education does not have to reproduce the content, timetable or structure
of school education. Home-educated children are not required to undertake
ordinary school tests.

An interest-driven or child-led approach is therefore possible. However, the
parents must still be able to demonstrate that the overall provision amounts
to an efficient, suitable and full-time education. A purely passive arrangement
with no convincing evidence of education, engagement or development would be
unlikely to be approved.

## Monitoring

The normal arrangement under the current policy is for an Education
Development Officer to visit the family and child twice each year. A School
Attendance Officer normally accompanies the Education Development Officer on
one of those annual visits.

The purpose of the visits is to consider whether the education remains
efficient, suitable and full time. Education Services may intervene if the
provision becomes unsuitable and may ultimately use the school-attendance
procedures contained in the Education Law.

## Alderney

The official guidance expressly includes children living in Alderney. Where
home education is approved for a child attending school in Guernsey or
Alderney, Education Services informs the school and its admissions team so
that the child can be removed from the school roll.

The Alderney (Application of Legislation) (Education) Ordinance, 2026, which
came into force on 6 July 2026, updated the application of Guernsey’s school
governance legislation to Alderney. It did not introduce a separate
home-education regime for Alderney.
