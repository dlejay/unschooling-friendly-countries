# Unschooling in Estonia

## Compulsory education

Children who turn seven by 1 October are subject to compulsory education.

From the 2025/2026 academic year, Estonia is phasing in a new *compulsory learning obligation* (*õppimiskohustus*). It applies until the young person turns 18 or completes upper secondary or vocational education, whichever comes first.

The reform first applies to pupils who entered grade 9 in September 2025. Children who completed basic school before 1 September 2025, or who had already turned 17 before that date, are not covered by the extended obligation.

## Home education

Home education (*koduõpe*) is legal in Estonia during basic education.

A parent may apply to organise their child's education independently outside school. However, the child remains enrolled in a school, and home education requires a decision by the school's director.

Since September 2025, the rules have become significantly more restrictive.

The parent must submit a reasoned application to the school director by **20 August** for home education beginning at the start of the academic year, or by **20 December** for home education beginning with the second semester.

The application must include:

* the reasons for requesting home education;
* a description of the proposed educational arrangements;
* the intended duration of home education; and
* details of the person who will teach the child.

The director must assess whether home education is in the child's best interests, whether the parent is capable of organising the education, and whether the child is capable of achieving the learning outcomes prescribed by the national curriculum under those arrangements.

The person providing the home education must prepare, in cooperation with the school, an **individual curriculum based on the school's curriculum**.

The school must check the child's achievement of the learning outcomes specified in the individual curriculum **at least once a month**.

The director must terminate home education immediately if, among other things:

* the child is substantially failing to achieve the required learning outcomes;
* the school is unable to assess the child's progress;
* home education is no longer considered to be in the child's best interests; or
* other circumstances make successful home education unlikely.

## Language of instruction

Estonia began its phased transition to Estonian-language education in September 2024, initially covering pre-school and grades 1 and 4. The transition is being extended to additional grades each year and is intended to be completed by 2030.
