# Unschooling in Cambodia

## Constitutional and statutory framework

Article 68 of the Constitution requires the State to provide free primary and
secondary education to citizens in public schools and states that citizens
shall receive at least nine years of education. The latter clause establishes
the duration of education, but does not require that education to be received
through attendance at a school.

Article 17 of the Education Law defines basic education as grades 1 through 9.
Article 31 separately guarantees citizens access to at least nine years of
quality education in public schools free of charge.

Article 36 lists among the obligations of parents and guardians the taking of a
child aged 6 years, or at least 70 months, to enrol in grade 1 at a school
holding the appropriate educational certificate. It also refers to supporting
the child's studies and maintaining relations between the school, family and
community. The provision concerns initial enrolment; it does not set out a
general continuing-attendance obligation, a compulsory period of schooling or
a penalty for its breach.

This distinction is reflected in Cambodia's 2018 report to the Committee on
the Rights of the Child, in which the government stated that it had not yet
been able to make basic education compulsory. In 2022, the Committee likewise
observed that primary education was not compulsory and recommended that
Cambodia make it compulsory as a matter of priority.

## Home education

The Education Law contains no provision expressly addressing home education
conducted within a single family. No provision examined prohibits such
instruction or subjects it to notification, approval or registration. Article
15 recognises formal, non-formal and informal education as components of the
national education system.

Non-formal education is defined as organised education outside formal
programmes, including basic education for learners who do not attend formal
schools. It is linked to continuing or equivalent studies and to assessment by
the education authorities. The provision does not specifically address
instruction given by parents to their own children.

Under Article 11, an educational establishment or training class must obtain a
licence before operating. Article 23 requires educational establishments to
implement the fundamental education programme prescribed by the Ministry. The
Law does not expressly apply either provision to instruction confined to a
single family, nor does it prescribe a curriculum for such instruction.

## Foreign families

The constitutional guarantee of free public education and the corresponding
right in Article 31 of the Education Law are worded in terms of citizens.
Article 36 is not expressly limited by citizenship, but the text does not
specify its application to non-citizen residents. No separate provision
concerning home education by foreign families was identified.
