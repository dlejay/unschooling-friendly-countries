# Unschooling in American Samoa

## Compulsory school attendance

The American Samoa Code Annotated imposes compulsory **attendance at a
school**, rather than merely a duty to provide education. Under A.S.C.A.
§ 16.0302, every child from the age of 5 through the age of 18 inclusive
must attend a public or private school, unless the Director of Education
excuses or excludes the child for good reason. Section 16.0303 makes the adult
who directly supervises the child for school purposes responsible for the
child's regular attendance throughout this period.

The attendance duty is enforceable. Under § 16.0308, where a parent fails to
send a child to a certified school, the truant officer must direct that the
child attend the appropriate public school. Continued non-compliance may lead
to a complaint before the District Court and a fine, imprisonment for up to
one month, or both.

## Legal route for education at home

The Code does not create a distinct category of home education and does not
expressly refer to a home school. Chapter 7 instead provides the general legal
framework for education outside the public-school system. Section 16.0701
designates as private schools those schools which receive no government
financial support and carry on regular educational work equivalent to the
public-school curriculum.

Read together, §§ 16.0302, 16.0701 and 16.0703 make certification under
Chapter 7 the only route identified by the Code through which a home-based
school could satisfy compulsory attendance. If the Department of Education
accepts the setting as a private school and authorizes it, § 16.0703 exempts
the children attending it from attendance at a public school.

This route is not limited in the statute to children with a disability,
illness or other exceptional circumstance. Its application to a school
operated for one family is nevertheless implicit rather than express: the
Code neither defines a home school nor states that the Department must
certify every home-based application meeting the listed requirements.

The separate power in § 16.0302 to excuse or exclude a child for good reason
does not establish a general home-education option. It depends on an
individual decision by the Director, and the section specifies neither an
entitlement nor criteria directed specifically to education at home.

## Authorization

No private school may operate without a certificate of authorization from the
Department of Education. The certificate is issued annually following an
inspection, and the Director may refuse it where the school does not maintain
the prescribed standards of instruction, curriculum and physical plant
(§ 16.0701(b)).

A first application must state the school's aims and purposes; identify its
sponsors and supporters; give the ages, grades and sex of the children to be
enrolled; state any tuition; and describe the curriculum to be pursued
(§ 16.0701(c)). Consequently, a family cannot rely on the private-school
route merely by deciding to withdraw a child and begin instruction at home:
authorization must precede operation.

## Curriculum, school year and records

The private-school regime is incompatible with a wholly curriculum-free form
of unschooling. Section 16.0701 requires educational work equivalent to the
public-school curriculum and compliance with the Director's standards of
instruction and curriculum. Private schools must also operate for the same
number of days each year as public schools (§ 16.0702), and must include any
curricular material required by the Director in the interest of good
citizenship (§ 16.0704).

Oversight continues after authorization. Under § 16.0706, a private school
must submit monthly enrolment reports and, every year, a school calendar, a
list of teachers and their experience and professional qualifications,
health certificates for the teachers, a grade-by-grade list of the curriculum
and books, and a class schedule. The school must also maintain a permanent
record card for each pupil.

These provisions do not prescribe a particular teaching method, so interests
and projects may inform how the required programme is delivered. They do,
however, prevent the child alone from determining whether the prescribed
curricular breadth, school-year requirements and reporting structure will be
followed.

## Foreign families

The compulsory-attendance rule applies to "all children" in the stated age
range, and the private-school provisions contain no nationality-based
eligibility restriction. The same authorization and substantive requirements
therefore apply to foreign families residing in the territory. Immigration
status and permission to reside in American Samoa remain separate questions.
