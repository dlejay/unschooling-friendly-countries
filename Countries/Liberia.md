# Unschooling in Liberia

## Compulsory schooling

Section 4.4.1(c)(i) of the Education Reform Act 2011 requires parents to ensure school attendance from the first school day of the year a child turns six until the last school day of the year the child turns eighteen. Under paragraphs (ii)–(iii), the nearest public school authority investigates non-enrolment or non-attendance and issues a compliance notice ; continued non-compliance without just cause constitutes a misdemeanour. [Education Reform Act, printed pp.23–24, official PDF in ZIP archive](https://moe.gov.lr/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Liberia-Education-Reform-Act-2011.pdf.zip).

Sections 4.4.1(a) and 4.4.2(a) make primary and junior secondary education compulsory and free in public schools. These comprise nine years of basic education, Grades 1–9. The Ministry's Education Sector Plan 2022/23–2026/27 describes senior secondary schooling as non-compulsory. This administrative description differs from the broader age-eighteen attendance wording in section 4.4.1(c)(i). [Education Reform Act, §§4.4.1–4.4.2](https://moe.gov.lr/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Liberia-Education-Reform-Act-2011.pdf.zip) ; [Education Sector Plan, p.91](https://www.moe.gov.lr/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/ESP-2023.pdf#page=91).

## Home education and attendance exemptions

Section 4.4.1(c)(iv) provides :

> A school may exempt a child entirely, partially or conditionally from free and compulsory school attendance

The exemption must be in the child's best interests. The provision particularly mentions children with disabilities, without expressly restricting exemptions to that group. Schools must maintain a register of exempt children, available to certified institutions addressing disadvantaged children's educational needs. These are individual exemptions ; the Act establishes no general parental home-education option. [Education Reform Act, §4.4.1(c)(iv), printed p.24](https://moe.gov.lr/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Liberia-Education-Reform-Act-2011.pdf.zip).

The school-based alternative is subject to educational regulation. The Ministry's Administrative Regulations provide for school accreditation and pre-approved curricula, and require schools to use prescribed courses and textbooks. Establishing a school therefore entails institutional requirements beyond educating one's own children at home. [Liberia Education Administrative Regulations, pp.9–10, 37–38 and 93](https://www.moe.gov.lr/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Admin.-Regulations-LEAG-Volume-4.pdf#page=16).

## Recent developments

On 17 August 2026, the Ministry announced the removal of registration fees in public schools from kindergarten through Grade 6 for the 2026/27 academic year. The announcement also reaffirmed that school operators require valid ministerial authorization. [Ministry of Education announcement](https://moe.gov.lr/minister-jallah-briefs-public-on-free-primary-school-registration-and-school-accountability/).

## Statistics

The Ministry published its *Annual School Census Report 2025/2026* on 29 October 2026. Its historical comparison reports :

| Academic year | Census enrolments | Physical schools |
| ------------- | ----------------: | ---------------: |
| 2025/26 | 1,300,815 | 6,494 |
| 2024/25 | 1,271,656 | 6,394 |
| 2021/22 | 1,421,429 | 6,249 |

These figures cover institutions and enrolments reported in the school census, including early childhood, primary, secondary and alternative programmes. They are not home-education totals. The 2025/26 census uses school-level reporting and digitally supported processing. [Ministry publication notice](https://moe.gov.lr/projects/annual-school-census-report-2025-2026/) ; [Annual School Census Report, pp.16–17 and 30–32](https://moe.gov.lr/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/20252026-Annual-School-Census-Report_Final_Signed-Copy-1.pdf#page=30).
