# Unschooling in the Isle of Man

## Compulsory education

Section 23(1) of the [Education Act 2001](https://legislation.gov.im/cms/images/LEGISLATION/PRINCIPAL/2001/2001-0033/2001-0033_13.pdf#page=27)
provides:

> Subject to subsection (2), in any statutory provision “compulsory school
> age” means any age between 5 years and 16 years, and a person shall be
> treated as being of compulsory school age if he has attained the age of
> 5 years and has not attained the age of 16 years.

Section 23(2) provides that a person who is registered at a school, or has
been registered within the previous twelve months, and turns sixteen
during a year beginning on 1 September is deemed to reach that age on the
Friday before the last Monday in May in that year.

## Education otherwise than at school

[Section 24(1)](https://legislation.gov.im/cms/images/LEGISLATION/PRINCIPAL/2001/2001-0033/2001-0033_13.pdf#page=28)
provides:

> It is the duty of the parent of every child of compulsory school age to
> cause him to receive suitable education, either by regular attendance
> at school or otherwise.

## Notification

[Section 24A(1)](https://legislation.gov.im/cms/images/LEGISLATION/PRINCIPAL/2001/2001-0033/2001-0033_13.pdf#page=28)
provides:

> Where a child of compulsory school age is not a registered pupil at a
> provided school, maintained school or special school, the parent of the
> child must notify the Department in writing of the arrangements made
> for the child to receive education.

Under section 24A(2), the notification must identify the child by full
name, address and date of birth, give the parent's full name and address,
and state whether education is being or will be provided through regular
school attendance. It must give the school's name and address or, where
the child is educated otherwise, the names and addresses of the persons
providing the education.

Section 24A(3) specifies the notification deadlines:

> The parent of a child must give a notification under subsection (1) —
>
> (a) within 3 months after the child —
>
> (i) attains the compulsory school age;
>
> (ii) becomes resident in the Island; or
>
> (iii) ceases to be a registered pupil at a provided school, maintained
> school or special school; or
>
> (b) at any time, within 21 days after being required by the Department
> to do so by notice in writing.

## Withdrawal from school

[Section 16(2)](https://legislation.gov.im/cms/images/LEGISLATION/PRINCIPAL/2001/2001-0033/2001-0033_13.pdf#page=20)
requires regulations to prescribe the procedure for withdrawal from a
school and deletion from its register. Paragraph (c) requires those
regulations to:

> provide that a child who has under arrangements made by the Department
> become a registered pupil at a special school shall not be withdrawn
> from the school without the consent of the Department;

Section 29(1) makes a parent's failure to secure regular attendance an
offence where a child of compulsory school age remains a registered
pupil, subject to the exceptions specified in that section. School
attendance orders are addressed below.

## Educational standard and curriculum

[Section 24(3)](https://legislation.gov.im/cms/images/LEGISLATION/PRINCIPAL/2001/2001-0033/2001-0033_13.pdf#page=28)
defines the applicable educational standard:

> “suitable education”, in relation to a child, means efficient full-time
> education suitable to his age, ability and aptitude and to any special
> educational needs he may have.

The curriculum-making duty in [section 8(1)](https://legislation.gov.im/cms/images/LEGISLATION/PRINCIPAL/2001/2001-0033/2001-0033_13.pdf#page=14)
applies to registered pupils at provided and maintained schools:

> The Department shall by order prescribe a curriculum for all registered
> pupils of compulsory school age at provided schools and maintained
> schools.

## Enforcement and school attendance orders

[Section 25(1)](https://legislation.gov.im/cms/images/LEGISLATION/PRINCIPAL/2001/2001-0033/2001-0033_13.pdf#page=29)
provides:

> If it appears to the Department that a child of compulsory school age
> in the Island is not receiving suitable education, either by regular
> attendance at school or otherwise, it shall serve a notice in writing
> on a parent of the child requiring him to satisfy it within the period
> specified in the notice (not being less than 15 days beginning with
> the day on which the notice is served) that the child is receiving
> such education.

Section 25(2) provides:

> A notice under subsection (1) may require the parent on whom it is
> served to submit the child for examination or assessment for the
> purpose of the notice.

Under sections 25(3)–(4), where the parent fails to satisfy the Department
and it considers school attendance expedient, the Department must give
a further notice of at least twenty-one days before making a school
attendance order under section 26.

[Section 27(2)](https://legislation.gov.im/cms/images/LEGISLATION/PRINCIPAL/2001/2001-0033/2001-0033_13.pdf#page=31)
provides for revocation of an existing order:

> If at any time the parent applies to the Department —
>
> (a) stating that arrangements have been made for the child to receive
> suitable education otherwise than at school, and specifying those
> arrangements, and
>
> (b) requesting that the order be revoked on that ground,
>
> the Department shall comply with the request, unless it is of the
> opinion that those arrangements are not satisfactory.

Under sections 27(5)–(7), a refusal must state the reasons and allow at
least twenty-one days for an appeal to a court of summary jurisdiction.
The court may confirm the refusal or revoke the order.
