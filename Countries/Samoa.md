# Unschooling in Samoa

## Compulsory enrolment and attendance

Section 2 of the [Education Act 2009, as amended](https://www.ag.gov.ws/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Education-Act-2009.pdf#page=4), defines compulsory school age as four for early childhood education and five to sixteen for primary and secondary education. Section 4 requires enrolment until Year 13 completion or age sixteen, whichever occurs first, subject to exemption. Section 6 requires attendance throughout each school day.

## Exemption and withdrawal

Section 5(1) permits a carer, including a parent or guardian, to apply to the Ministry's Chief Executive Officer for exemption from enrolment. Under section 5(2), the Chief Executive Officer must be satisfied that exemption is:

> in the best interests of the child’s education.

Section 9 requires an exemption certificate, valid for its specified period unless revoked. Revocation is permitted for breach of conditions or where appropriate in the circumstances. Section 10 requires written notification to the principal of withdrawal and the child's proposed subsequent education.

## Educational requirements

Section 5(3) permits individual exemption conditions to be imposed, added, varied or revoked. The educational-interest test and these conditions govern the exemption. Sections 58–60 regulate curriculum, assessment and certification for schools and early childhood education centres, including ministerial curriculum requirements.

Source: [Education Act, sections 2, 4–6, 9–10 and 58–60, pp. 4, 6–9 and 28–30](https://www.ag.gov.ws/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Education-Act-2009.pdf).

## Official statistics

The Ministry's [Education Statistical Digest 2025, Table 3, p. 22](https://www.mesc.gov.ws/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Education-Statistical-Digest-2025.pdf#page=23), reports **65,515 enrolled students**: **4,999** in early childhood education, **45,170** in primary education and **15,346** in secondary education. These figures describe school and centre enrolments in 2025.
