# Unschooling in the Islamic Republic of Mauritania

## Compulsory schooling

Article 3 of Law No. 2022-023 of 17 August makes education compulsory from six to fifteen. Article 26 specifies nine years of basic education, comprising six primary and three lower-secondary years. Preschool is optional under Article 21. [2022 Education Framework Law, Articles 3, 21 and 26, printed pp.774 and 778–779](https://hepo.iesalc.unesco.org/pc/static/countrydocs/cp/2025/mrt/mrt_HEEdLawLi.pdf#page=3).

The obligation includes enrolment in an educational institution. Article 4 of Law No. 2001-054 of 19 July provides, in translation :

> Those legally responsible must enrol the children in their care during the fifteen days preceding the start of the school year.

Article 6 requires the responsible person to inform the school of the reasons for a child's temporary absence. Article 10 provides sanctions for refusing enrolment without a valid reason, withdrawing a child from class for more than fifteen days in a term without a valid reason, or causing a temporary or permanent interruption of schooling. These provisions concern school enrolment and attendance, rather than solely an obligation to provide instruction. [Law No. 2001-054, Articles 4–6 and 10, pp.1–2](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/r2e002/86fd4970f2f17d50ebfcaf846478ac07e8e080ad.pdf).

Under Articles 89–90 of the 2022 framework, earlier education legislation remains applicable pending its repeal, replacement or amendment, except where contrary to the new framework. [2022 Education Framework Law, Articles 89–90, printed p.791](https://hepo.iesalc.unesco.org/pc/static/countrydocs/cp/2025/mrt/mrt_HEEdLawLi.pdf#page=20).

## Home education, mahadras and distance learning

Article 1 of the 2001 law lists public and private institutions, *mahadras* (traditional Islamic educational institutions) and other duly approved establishments as providers of fundamental education. These are institutional alternatives. Article 10’s reference to a valid reason does not establish a general parental-choice exemption from enrolment. [Law No. 2001-054, Articles 1 and 10](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/r2e002/86fd4970f2f17d50ebfcaf846478ac07e8e080ad.pdf).

Articles 51–53 of the 2022 framework recognise traditional education, promote pathways from mahadras into formal education and envisage online mahadras. Article 72 calls for a policy integrating distance education. These institutional arrangements do not provide a general right to replace compulsory schooling with autonomous family learning. [2022 Education Framework Law, Articles 51–53 and 72, printed pp.783–784 and 788](https://hepo.iesalc.unesco.org/pc/static/countrydocs/cp/2025/mrt/mrt_HEEdLawLi.pdf#page=12).

## Recent developments

**2026–2027.** The *École républicaine* reform entered its fifth school year. Official preparations continued to emphasise attendance from the first day of school and coordination between families, schools and local authorities. The statutory reform agenda includes progressively limiting private provision to post-primary education under Article 50 of the 2022 framework. [AMI — school-year preparations, 30 September 2026](https://ami.mr/fr/archives/303421) ; [2022 Education Framework Law, Article 50, printed pp.782–783](https://hepo.iesalc.unesco.org/pc/static/countrydocs/cp/2025/mrt/mrt_HEEdLawLi.pdf#page=11).
