# Unschooling in Guinea

## Compulsory education

School attendance is compulsory in Guinea.

The 2019 Children’s Code states that every child has a duty :

> d’aller obligatoirement à l’école jusqu’à 16 ans

(“to attend school compulsorily until the age of 16”).

The Code also refers expressly to an “obligation de fréquentation scolaire”
(compulsory school-attendance obligation) and prohibits employers from employing
a child subject to that obligation during school hours.

The Constitution adopted in 2025 extends the requirement. Article 21 guarantees
compulsory access to school by the age of 5 at the latest and requires children
to remain in school until at least the age of 17.

## Unschooling

There is no identified legal provision allowing parents to satisfy compulsory
school attendance through independent home education.

UNESCO's country profile for Guinea found no provisions concerning
homeschooling. The legal language is particularly restrictive: both the
Children's Code and the 2025 Constitution expressly refer to attendance at
school rather than merely requiring parents to provide an education.

Homeschooling does appear to occur in practice in at least some expatriate
contexts. For example, a missionary project in Fria publicly recruits
"Homeschool Teachers" to teach missionaries' children. This does not, however,
establish a legal right to homeschool, an exemption from compulsory school
attendance, or a generally available registration procedure.

More broadly, compulsory attendance is imperfectly enforced in practice and
large numbers of children remain outside the school system. This should not be
confused with legal recognition of home education.

## Statistics

According to UNICEF’s 2025 annual report, nearly one million children aged 6–14
were out of school, representing about 28 % of children in that age group.

UNICEF’s 2023 annual report had reported that 34 % of primary-school-age
children and 47 % of lower-secondary-school-age children were out of school.

## News

A new Constitution was approved by referendum on 21 September 2025 and
promulgated on 26 September 2025, restoring a constitutional framework after
the Constitution had been suspended following the 2021 military takeover.

Mamadi Doumbouya was subsequently elected President in the election of
28 December 2025 and was inaugurated on 17 January 2026.
