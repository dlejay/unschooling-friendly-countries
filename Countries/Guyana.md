# Unschooling in Guyana

## Compulsory education

Section 13 of the Education Act, Chapter 39:01, requires parents to provide :

> efficient elementary instruction in reading, writing, and arithmetic

Section 22 applies sections 13–16 to children between six and fifteen. The Ministry's attendance guidance of 26 September 2016 gives the range as five years and nine months to fifteen. The statutory provision and administrative starting age should therefore be distinguished. [Education Act, §§13 and 22](https://www.mola.gov.gy/public/laws/Volume%209%20Cap.%2036.02%20-%2046.071696884543.pdf#page=13) ; [Ministry — attendance guidance, 26 September 2016](https://www.education.gov.gy/en/index.php/media2/news-events/179-children-5-years-9-months-to-15-years-required-by-law-to-attend-school).

## Home education

### Starting and oversight

Section 13 imposes an educational duty without specifying school attendance as its sole means of fulfilment or requiring initial home-education approval. Under section 14, an officer may complain to a magistrate where parents habitually fail, without reasonable excuse, to provide efficient instruction. The magistrate may then order school attendance. Section 12 also permits daytime inquiries at homes by attendance officers or other authorised persons. [Education Act, §§12–14](https://www.mola.gov.gy/public/laws/Volume%209%20Cap.%2036.02%20-%2046.071696884543.pdf#page=13).

On 24 August 2021, Education Minister Priya Manickchand stated that parents could keep children at home and educate them themselves, taking responsibility for their engagement. This statement concerned reopening schools during the COVID-19 pandemic ; it should be read in that context. [News Source — ministerial statement](https://newssourcegy.com/news/parents-can-opt-to-homeschool-their-children-education-minister/).

### Educational approach

Section 14(4) links efficient instruction to regulations. Education Code regulation 70 addresses schemes of work, but regulation 2 defines its schools by public grant funding ; these school provisions are not an express home-education curriculum procedure. [Act, §14(4) and Education Code Regulations, regulations 2 and 70, printed pp.14, 63 and 98](https://www.mola.gov.gy/public/laws/Volume%209%20Cap.%2036.02%20-%2046.071696884543.pdf).

## Reported practice

In October 2020, *Stabroek News* interviewed Candice Rowena Ramessar and a parent identified as Adriana, both educating their children at home before the pandemic. Ramessar described using libraries, nature and cooking as learning resources. Adriana described an approach guided by the child, adapted to individual learning needs and interests, with less emphasis on rote learning. These accounts document child-centred home education, without establishing that every participating family identifies as unschooling. [Stabroek News — Homeschoolers see ‘child-centred’ advantage outside formal learning, 25 October 2020](https://www.stabroeknews.com/2020/10/25/sunday/homeschoolers-see-child-centred-advantage-outside-formal-learning/).

## Recent developments

**2026.** On 2 November, the Ministry announced free live reinforcement classes for Grades 1–11 through the Guyana Digital School. On 10 November, it encouraged Grade Six and Grade 11 students to use the platform's lessons and resources for their forthcoming mock examinations. These are curriculum-based resources supporting learning at home and in school, rather than a new statutory home-education approval regime. [Ministry — live classes, 2 November 2026](https://en.education.gov.gy/news/guyana-digital-school-launches-free-live-reinforcement-classes-for-grades-1-6) ; [DPI — examination preparation, 10 November 2026](https://dpi.gov.gy/students-encouraged-to-use-digital-school-ahead-of-mock-examinations/).

## Statistics

**2026.** The Ministry of Finance's *Mid-Year Report*, dated 28 August, reports more than **30,000 Grade 10 and 11 students registered on the Guyana Digital School**. These are platform registrations, not home-education totals or a count of unschoolers. [Mid-Year Report 2026, §3.135, printed p.40](https://finance.gov.gy/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Mid-Year-Report-2026.pdf#page=50).

**2020.** The organisers of the Guyana Homeschool Association reported **19 members** when the network was being established. Its constituency included both practising and prospective home educators. This was an association membership figure, not a census of homeschooled children. [Stabroek News, 25 October 2020](https://www.stabroeknews.com/2020/10/25/sunday/homeschoolers-see-child-centred-advantage-outside-formal-learning/).
