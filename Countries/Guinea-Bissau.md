# Unschooling in Guinea-Bissau

## Compulsory schooling

Articles 12–13 of the *Lei de Bases do Sistema Educativo*, Law No. 4/2011 of 29 March, establish nine years of compulsory basic education, divided into three cycles : Grades 1–4, 5–6 and 7–9. Admission begins at age six. Children who turn six by 1 October are admitted ; those turning six between 2 October and 31 December may enter at their guardian's request. [Official Gazette No. 13, supplement, p.32, reproduced in Annex D](https://repositorio.iscte-iul.pt/bitstream/10071/31434/1/phd_francisco_bilima_te.pdf#page=298).

Article 9 makes preschool education optional, beginning at age three and continuing until entry into basic education. Article 12 provides full fee exemption through Grade 6 and progressive free provision from Grade 7, subject to the State's resources. [Ministry's text, articles 9 and 12–13](https://www.unesco.org/education/edurights/media/docs/66ed11bae55d3ab337a6c6d2f0b8a69be4fe5638.pdf#page=6).

## Home education and distance learning

The Basic Law does not expressly establish independent parent-led home education as a way to fulfil compulsory basic education.

Article 36 does recognise distance education, using multimedia and information and communication technologies, as a special form of school education. It may complement or replace regular schooling and may operate at any educational level. This is a formal educational pathway ; the provision does not define a separate parental home-education regime. [Article 36](https://www.unesco.org/education/edurights/media/docs/66ed11bae55d3ab337a6c6d2f0b8a69be4fe5638.pdf#page=17).

Articles 59–61 regulate private and cooperative schools. Their establishment and operation require ministerial recognition and are subject to inspection. Schools may adopt their own curricula if these meet the system's general principles, structures and objectives and receive ministerial approval. Teaching staff in institutions integrated into the school network must hold the qualifications required in public education. These provisions concern regulated institutions rather than a distinct family-education exemption. [Articles 59–61](https://www.unesco.org/education/edurights/media/docs/66ed11bae55d3ab337a6c6d2f0b8a69be4fe5638.pdf#page=25).

## Recent implementation and statistics

UNICEF's *Guinea-Bissau Annual Report 2025* describes work with the Ministry of Education on radio learning for remote areas, a pilot programme for out-of-school and over-age children, and support for preschool access. It reports the following programme results :

| Year | UNICEF-supported education activity | Children or enrolments supported |
| ---- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------: |
| 2025 | Preschool enrolments supported | 7,835 |
| 2025 | Children benefiting from radio lessons reaching remote areas | 1,555 |
| 2025 | Children enrolled in a new catch-up pilot for out-of-school and over-age learners | 500 |

These figures describe programme beneficiaries and supported enrolments. They are neither national school-enrolment totals nor counts of home-educated children, and should not be added together as a count of distinct learners. [UNICEF Annual Report 2025, p.15](https://www.unicef.org/guineabissau/pt/media/4181/file/UNICEF%20Annual%20Report%202025%20-%20PORTUGUESE.pdf.pdf#page=17).
