# Home education in Equatorial Guinea

## Compulsory education

Article 24(1) of the Fundamental Law provides:

> Every citizen has the right to primary education, which is compulsory, free and guaranteed.

Unofficial English translation. [Fundamental Law, Law No.1/2012, Article 24(1), PDF p.6](https://boe.gob.gq/files/LEY%20FUNDAMENTAL.pdf#page=6).

The General Education Law makes preschool and primary education compulsory for Equatoguineans. Preschool covers ages one–six; primary comprises six school years, normally between six and twelve. [Law No.5/2007 amending Law No.14/1995, Articles 3(2), 14 and 16, PDF pp.3, 6–7](https://boe.gob.gq/files/Ley%20General%20de%20Educaci%C3%B3n.pdf#page=3).

## Parental choice and education outside the classroom

Article 6(2) requires families to comply with compulsory-education rules. Article 6(3) provides:

> Parents […] have the right to choose […] educational establishments from among those legally established […]

Unofficial English translation. [General Education Law, Article 6(2)–(3), PDF p.4](https://boe.gob.gq/files/Ley%20General%20de%20Educaci%C3%B3n.pdf#page=4).

Article 49(1) describes distance education as offering:

> […] opportunities to those who cannot pursue in-person studies.

Unofficial English translation. Article 49(3) permits formal and non-formal distance education. Article 52 subjects admission to the legal requirements for the relevant level and modality. Article 90(1) subjects distance-education centres to regulatory provisions on organisation, admissions, assessment and certification. [General Education Law, Articles 49–52 and 90(1), PDF pp.13–14, 19](https://boe.gob.gq/files/Ley%20General%20de%20Educaci%C3%B3n.pdf#page=13).

## Institutional approval, curriculum and assessment

The constitutional right to establish schools is conditional on compliance with the official pedagogical plan. [Fundamental Law, Article 24(3), PDF p.6](https://boe.gob.gq/files/LEY%20FUNDAMENTAL.pdf#page=6).

Establishing an educational centre requires a ministerial application, educational project, inspected premises, qualified staff and financial-solvency evidence. The Ministry sets primary programmes and pedagogical guidelines. Secondary *enseñanza libre* candidates take end-of-year examinations at the public centres where they are enrolled. [General Education Law, Articles 73, 18(2) and 27(4), PDF pp.17, 7, 10](https://boe.gob.gq/files/Ley%20General%20de%20Educaci%C3%B3n.pdf#page=17).

## Official statistics

INEGE's **2026 Statistical Yearbook** reports **126,372 primary-school pupils in 2022–23**, comprising 64,369 boys and 62,003 girls. These are school-enrolment figures. [INEGE, Anuario Estadístico de Guinea Ecuatorial 2026, Table 73, p.120 (PDF p.150)](https://inege.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/AEGE-2026-Final.pdf#page=150).
