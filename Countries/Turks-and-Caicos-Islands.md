# Unschooling in the Turks and Caicos Islands

## Compulsory education

Education is compulsory from age 4 until age 16. Section 42 of the *Education Ordinance* requires parents to secure efficient full-time education suitable to the child’s age, ability and aptitude by regular attendance at school. Under section 43, failure to secure attendance is an offence unless the child is legally excused; Schedule 2 provides for a fine of $500, one month’s imprisonment, or both.

The Ordinance is Ordinance 8 of 1989, as amended by Ordinance 10 of 2009. The Ministry of Education describes it as currently under review.

## Home education

Home education operates as an excuse from school attendance. Under section 44(a), a child is excused where, in the opinion of the Minister, the child is receiving satisfactory instruction at home or elsewhere.

The provision is not restricted to any category of child or parent, and the Ordinance imposes no qualification requirement on a parent teaching their own child. It also prescribes no application, registration, notification, prior-approval procedure or waiting period. Whether the section 44(a) excuse operates before the Minister has formed an opinion is not expressly resolved, and the Ordinance provides no procedure for obtaining such an opinion in advance.

The Ordinance sets no criteria for “satisfactory instruction” and provides no statutory merits appeal against the Minister’s opinion. Section 54 nevertheless requires the Minister to have regard to parents’ wishes, so far as compatible with efficient instruction and the avoidance of unreasonable public expenditure.

## Administrative guidance

The Ministry’s *Education Digest 2020–21* sets out **draft requirements** for home education. These provide for written notice within ten calendar days of beginning home instruction. The proposed curriculum must either meet the standard of the Turks and Caicos Islands National Curriculum or provide at least 175 days of annual instruction across specified subjects, with quarterly assessment of academic progress.

On or before 1 September of each subsequent year, parents are to notify the Department of their intention to continue home instruction and provide evidence of academic progress, including an educational portfolio. The draft also envisages Departmental supervision and support, adjustment of programmes where delivery or progress is considered unsatisfactory, and Department-administered certification where the chosen programme is not certified.

These requirements remain draft. No source located confirms that they were formally adopted or are currently applied. The 2022–23 Digest does not repeat them, but that edition omits policy narrative generally, so the omission is not probative.
