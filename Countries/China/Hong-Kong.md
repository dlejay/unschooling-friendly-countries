# Unschooling in Hong Kong

## Compulsory education

Hong Kong requires **school attendance from age 6 to 15**.

Under section 74 of the **Education Ordinance (Cap. 279)**, where a child is not attending primary or secondary school without a reasonable excuse, the Permanent Secretary for Education may issue an **attendance order** requiring the parent to cause the child to attend a specified school. Failure to comply with such an order without reasonable excuse is an offence under section 78.

The Education Bureau currently states that:

> all parents must send their children (aged 6 to 15) to attend schools.

Hong Kong provides **12 years of free primary and secondary education**, but only the period from 6 to 15 is compulsory.

## Home education

Hong Kong has **no statutory home-education system and no general legal right to homeschool**.

The Education Bureau's most explicit published statement on homeschooling says that it does not encourage homeschooling in place of formal schooling. Nevertheless, it also stated that it would **not automatically disallow homeschooling** and would examine cases individually, considering whether the family could provide the child with an adequate, all-round education. Parents wishing to homeschool were instructed to contact the Education Bureau for an assessment.

This means that homeschooling is possible in practice, but operates through **case-by-case administrative tolerance rather than a clear statutory entitlement**. A family should therefore not simply withdraw a compulsory-school-age child and begin homeschooling without engaging with the Education Bureau.

As Esther Erlings concludes in her legal analysis, homeschooling is, beyond the concept of a “reasonable cause”, **in principle prohibited by the statutory school-attendance regime**, while a de facto government policy has allowed a homeschooling community to exist.
