# Unschooling in Croatia

## Compulsory education

Primary education in Croatia lasts eight years and is compulsory for all children, generally from age 6 to 15.

In addition, all children must attend the compulsory preschool programme (*program predškole*) in the year before entering primary school. For children who are not already attending kindergarten, this programme normally comprises 250 hours.

## Unschooling

Croatia does not provide a general legal right for parents to educate their children at home.

Under Article 135 of the **Primary and Secondary School Education Act** (*Zakon o odgoju i obrazovanju u osnovnoj i srednjoj školi*), parents are required to enrol their child in primary school and ensure regular attendance at the compulsory programme.

A parent who fails to enrol a child or prevents or fails to ensure regular school attendance may be fined under Article 152.

The Act does provide for *nastava u kući* (“teaching at home”), but this is not homeschooling in the usual sense.

Article 42 provides that, where a pupil cannot attend classes because of significant motor impairments or chronic illness, the school may, with approval from the Ministry and following a proposal from the school-medicine doctor, organise teaching in the pupil's home or in a healthcare institution. Such teaching may also be delivered remotely.

## Statistics

There is no ordinary homeschooling population to report because Croatian law does not establish homeschooling as an alternative form of compulsory education. Pupils receiving medically necessary teaching at home under Article 42 should not be classified as homeschooled children.
