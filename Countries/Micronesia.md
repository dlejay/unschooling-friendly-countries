# Unschooling in Micronesia

## National and state responsibilities

Title 40 of the FSM Code establishes the national educational framework.
Section 103 assigns the Secretary of Education responsibility for national
minimum standards and coordination with the education authorities of Chuuk,
Kosrae, Pohnpei and Yap. Section 111 assigns responsibility for home-learning
programmes to the Secretary and the State Boards of Education.

## Compulsory education

Section 104(1) of [Title 40](https://www.fsmlaw.org/fsm/code/title40/T40_Ch01_2014.html)
provides:

> Education shall be provided and shall be compulsory for all children,
> including children with disabilities, from first grade through graduation
> from the eighth grade, or until the age of 14 years.

Section 104(2) separately addresses attendance:

> Attendance at school shall be required of all children between the ages
> of six and 14 or until completion of the eighth grade, unless excluded
> from school or exempted from attendance for sickness or behavioral problems
> as determined by the appropriate State authorities.

Under section 104(2)(a), the State Director of Education selects whether the
sixth-birthday admission cutoff falls on December 31 or at the commencement
of the school year. Section 104(2)(b) permits a state legislature to set a
later compulsory starting age while preserving admission at six for children
whose parents request it.

[C.B. 23-29, introduced on July 14, 2023](https://www.cfsm.gov.fm/23rd-cfsm-bills/),
and [C.B. 24-103, introduced on January 13, 2026](https://www.cfsm.gov.fm/24th-bills/),
are congressional bills proposing to include kindergarten in elementary
education and reduce the national compulsory starting age to five.

## Home education

Section 102(12) defines home learning:

> “Home learning” means a private form of education or course of study
> administered by parents who for personal or religious reasons choose not
> to send their children to a public or private school but as an alternative
> provide education within a home setting.

Section 111, entitled “Certification of home learning programs”, provides:

> The Secretary of Education and the State Boards of Education may set
> minimum standards for home learning.
>
> (1) The Secretary and the State Boards of Education may, upon application,
> review and approve a course of study of home learning as meeting those
> minimum standards.
>
> (2) The Secretary and State Board of Education shall monitor for compliance
> with the minimum standards established by the Secretary, any home study
> programs which are approved under this section, and may revoke its own
> approval, giving reasonable grounds for such action.

## State provisions

### Pohnpei

The [Pohnpei Department of Education](https://pohnpei.doe.fm/early-childhood-education/)
states that legislation has reduced the compulsory starting age from six
to five. Section 1-111(1) of Title 18 of the [Pohnpei Code](https://psl.gov.fm/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2020-DIGITAL-CODE.htm#t18pc01z111)
uses age 16 as the upper attendance threshold, assessed by December 31 of
the school year.

Section 1-111(2) includes the following exemptions:

> Attendance shall not be compulsory in the following instances:
>
> […]
>
> (c) Where a competent person is employed as a tutor by the household
> wherein the minor resides and proper instruction is thereby imparted as
> approved by the Director;
>
> […]
>
> (g) Where the minor is enrolled in an appropriate alternative education
> program approved by the Director in accordance with the plans and policies
> of the Department.

### Kosrae

Section 12.102 of the [Kosrae State Code](https://www.fsmlaw.org/kosrae/code/title12/t12p01c01.htm)
provides:

> A person attends a public or private school from his sixth through his
> fifteenth years, or until his graduation from elementary school, whichever
> occurs earlier, unless excluded from school or exempted from attendance
> by the Department of Education.

The same section sets September 30 as the sixth-birthday cutoff for the
first year of schooling.

### Yap

Section 202(b) of [Title 16 of the Yap State Code](https://fsmlaw.org/yap/code/title16/T16_Ch02.htm)
provides:

> Attendance at school shall be obligatory unless the student is
> specifically excused from attendance or unless the provisions of
> section 203 apply.

Section 203 provides:

> A child who is a resident of a school district shall be excused from
> attending the school in that district if he or she is attending a
> nonpublic school or otherwise if the district school board and the
> educational administrator approve.

### Chuuk

Section 10 of the [Chuuk State Board of Education Act of 1991,
CSL 191-15](https://www.fsmlaw.org/chuuk/code/title20/T20_CH01.htm),
assigns the Board responsibility for educational policy, regulations,
curriculum standards and school approvals. Its powers include:

> formulating policies and adopting rules and regulations for the
> educational system of Chuuk;

## Enforcement

Section 104(3) of Title 40 makes it a violation knowingly to permit a child's
absence or prevent a child's attendance in the circumstances specified there,
where the absence is unexcused and contrary to applicable law or regulations.
Section 104(3)(a) leaves the penalties to the states; section 104(3)(b)
likewise assigns the determination of good cause for absence to state
authorities.

In Yap, section 202(c) permits a civil penalty of up to US$25 for each day
of absence when a parent or guardian has failed to use proper diligence
to secure attendance. The provision requires proceedings before a trial
court judge on a complaint brought by the State Attorney General.

## Foreign families

The national compulsory-education and attendance provisions in section 104
refer to all children. The definition of home learning in section 102(12)
and the certification provisions in section 111 do not distinguish between
citizen and noncitizen parents.
