# Unschooling in Papua New Guinea outside Bougainville

## Compulsory education

School attendance is not generally compulsory under Papua New Guinea's national framework. The Australian Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade's country report of 25 July 2025 states: “School attendance is not compulsory.” The U.S. Department of Labor's 2024 assessment likewise records no established compulsory-education age. [DFAT, paragraph 2.43](https://www.dfat.gov.au/sites/default/files/country-information-report-papua-new-guinea.pdf#page=10); [U.S. Department of Labor, 2024 findings](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ilab/resources/reports/child-labor/papua-new-guinea).

The Government's objective of access to 13 years of education and its school-fee funding policies concern access to schooling. They do not themselves establish a statutory compulsory-attendance age range. [National Education Plan 2020–2029](https://education.gov.pg/images/nep-final-2021-2029.pdf); [Ministerial Policy Statement 01/2026](https://education.gov.pg/admin/images/products/03_02_26%20_N_MPS_on%20GFEP_202695.pdf).

Bougainville has a separate education law and is covered in a [separate fiche](Bougainville.md).

## Home education

### Family learning and school registration

The Education Act 1983 regulates educational institutions and contains no separate family home-education scheme. In the absence of a general school-attendance obligation, ordinary family learning does not depend on obtaining an exemption from compulsory schooling. [Education Act 1983](https://www.tsc.gov.pg/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/edua1983104.pdf); [DFAT, paragraph 2.43](https://www.dfat.gov.au/sites/default/files/country-information-report-papua-new-guinea.pdf#page=10).

Operating a school is subject to different requirements. Sections 98–100 regulate permitted schools outside the National Education System: under section 100(2), instruction must not begin before a registration certificate has been received. The Department's 2024 School Registration Policy also requires registration before teaching commences at a school. [Education Act, sections 2 and 98–100](https://www.tsc.gov.pg/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/edua1983104.pdf); [School Registration Policy 2024, section 5.1](https://www.pngpie.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/PNP205_SCHOOL_REGISTRATION_POLICY_v9.pdf).

### Educational approach and distance education

For family learning outside an institutional programme, the national framework does not prescribe a home-education curriculum or compulsory school examinations. An interest-driven approach is therefore compatible with the absence of a general schooling obligation. This concerns family learning outside registered schools or qualification programmes. [Education Act 1983](https://www.tsc.gov.pg/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/edua1983104.pdf); [DFAT, paragraph 2.43](https://www.dfat.gov.au/sites/default/files/country-information-report-papua-new-guinea.pdf#page=10).

Flexible Open and Distance Education (FODE) offers a formal route with its own admission and assessment requirements. Its admission guidance describes English and mathematics entry tests, placement by results, assignments and examinations. Those programme requirements apply to enrolled FODE students. [FODE admission guidance](https://fode.education.gov.pg/admission.html).

## Recent policy and statistics

Ministerial Policy Statement 01/2026 continues the Government Free Education Policy for registered schools, including FODE and permitted schools. It records a **K904.5 million appropriation for 2026**, compared with **K859.5 million for 2025**. These are school-funding allocations, not grants specifically for independent home education. [MPS 01/2026, sections 1–4](https://education.gov.pg/admin/images/products/03_02_26%20_N_MPS_on%20GFEP_202695.pdf).

On **6 June 2026**, the Prime Minister's Department reported **2,565,713 students enrolled in subsidised schooling** under the Government Free Education Programme. This is a programme figure reported on that date, rather than a home-education count or a clearly specified school-year census. [Prime Minister's Department, 6 June 2026](https://www.pmnec.gov.pg/marape-government-highlights-education-as-one-of-its-proudest-achievements/).
