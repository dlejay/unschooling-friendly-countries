# Unschooling in Malawi

## Compulsory schooling

Section 13 of the Education Act 2013 makes primary education in Government schools free of tuition and :

> compulsory for every child below eighteen years of age.

The obligation concerns primary education, rather than a general requirement to remain in primary school until eighteen. [Education Act, §13](https://teacherscouncil.mw/assets/pdf/Malawi-Education-Act-2013.pdf#page=12).

The National Education Policy describes an official primary entry age of six and an eight-year cycle, Standards 1–8, with an expected age range of six to thirteen. It also recognises late entry and repetition. These normal progression ages differ from the Act's under-eighteen provision. [National Education Policy, §3.1.4, printed pp.4–5](https://npc.mw/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/malawi_national-education-policy.pdf#page=14).

## Home education and institutional requirements

Section 4(2)(b) requires regard for parental wishes, subject to efficient instruction and training and avoidance of excessive public expenditure. It does not itself create a home-education exemption. [Education Act, §4(2)(b)](https://teacherscouncil.mw/assets/pdf/Malawi-Education-Act-2013.pdf#page=9).

Section 2 defines schools broadly, including regular secular instruction through personal tuition or open and distance learning. Sections 32 and 36 require ministerial approval and registration of private schools before enrolment, with conditions concerning premises, finance and qualified staff. Where home-based provision constitutes a school under this definition, those institutional requirements apply. The Act does not establish a separate parental home-education route replacing compulsory primary schooling. [Education Act, §§2, 32 and 36](https://teacherscouncil.mw/assets/pdf/Malawi-Education-Act-2013.pdf).

Schools must follow a ministerially approved curriculum under section 54(2)(b) and are subject to inspection under section 53. [Education Act, §§53–54](https://teacherscouncil.mw/assets/pdf/Malawi-Education-Act-2013.pdf#page=28).

## Reported practice

The Abel Program describes itself as a grassroots homeschooling and rehabilitation programme for children and young adults with disabilities in northern Malawi. Lessons and therapy take place in children's homes. The programme also encourages school attendance where possible and offers after-school support. This documents home-based educational provision for particular needs, without establishing a general exemption for parent-directed unschooling. [The Abel Program — programme description](https://www.theabelprogram.org/).

The International Mission Board advertises a January–May 2027 placement in Lilongwe to assist two long-term missionary families homeschooling their children. This documents planned support for family home education, rather than the legal conditions applicable to all families. [IMB — Spring 2027 education placement](https://www.imb.org/opportunities/residency-tck-education-and-nextgen-discipleship-spring-2027/).

## Recent developments

**2026.** On 6 October, the Malawi Institute of Education announced work refining competency-based primary syllabuses for Grades 5 and 6, following work on Grades 1–4. The exercise involves curriculum specialists, teachers, lecturers and quality-assurance officers. [MIE — primary curriculum refinement, 6 October 2026](https://mie.edu.mw/shaping-malawi-2063-through-curriculum-reform-curriculum-refinement-moves-to-grades-5-and-6/).

The Ministry states that school development fund charges and MANEB examination-related fees in public schools were abolished in January 2026. Boarding and open-secondary-school fees remain payable. [Ministry of Education — free secondary education FAQs](https://www.education.gov.mw/).

## Statistics

**2025/26.** Reporting the Ministry's Education Statistics Report on 11 November 2026, *Malawi24* gives primary enrolment as **5,663,335 pupils**, with **180,708 recorded dropouts** during the academic year. These are reported school-system figures, not home-education or unschooling totals. [Malawi24 — report coverage, 11 November 2026](https://malawi24.com/2026/11/11/poverty-pushes-180000-learners-out-of-school/).

**2023/24.** The Ministry's Annual School Census records **5,420,159 primary pupils**, comprising **5,222,759 in public schools** and **197,400 in private schools**. It also records **538,800 secondary pupils**. The census measures enrolment in schools. [Malawi Education Statistics Report 2023/24, summary indicators, printed p.xv](https://cms.nsomalawi.mw/api/download/400/ASC-Report-2024.pdf).
