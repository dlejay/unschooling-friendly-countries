# Unschooling in Ethiopia

## Compulsory education

Ethiopia's General Education Proclamation No. 1368/2025 entered into force
on 10 April 2025, when it was published in the Federal Negarit Gazette.

The Proclamation makes pre-primary, primary and middle-level education
compulsory.

Compulsory pre-primary education begins at age 5, while entry into primary
education is normally at age 7.

Under Ethiopia's current education structure:

- primary education covers grades 1–6;
- middle-level education covers grades 7–8.

Compulsory education therefore normally runs from age 5 through completion
of grade 8, or roughly ten school years. High-school education, covering
grades 9–12, is free in government schools but is not compulsory.

## Unschooling

The General Education Proclamation does not establish a general right for
parents to educate their children at home.

Instead, it requires children of compulsory-school age to attend school.
Parents and guardians must ensure that their children attend pre-primary,
primary and middle-level education, except where attendance is prevented by
circumstances such as illness, the absence or shortage of educational
institutions, or displacement.

Government bodies are responsible for registering children of compulsory-school
age and ensuring that they enrol in, attend and complete compulsory education.
Schools must investigate absences and may contact parents or report continued
non-attendance to the relevant authorities.

The Proclamation recognises formal and non-formal education, but both are
presented as education delivered through regulated schools or educational
institutions following approved curricula and standards. This does not amount
to a general homeschooling exemption.

Independent home education or unschooling outside a recognised educational
institution is therefore not a generally available lawful option in Ethiopia.
